# Final Strategic Scorecard

## SECTION 1 Characteristics of Future Winners

1. Garmenting Scale
Garmenting is the missing link in India's textile value chain; the country exports intermediate materials but fails to capture the higher value addition of apparel. Scaling garmenting allows companies to handle large global programs efficiently, shifting the industry away from fragmented, sub-scale clusters toward integrated, globally competitive campuses.

2. Manufacturing Productivity
Competitiveness relies on output per worker and unit conversion cost, rather than mere labor arbitrage. Superior productivity, achieved through automation and industrial engineering, allows manufacturers to absorb wage inflation while maintaining global margins and consistent delivery schedules, emulating China's success.

3. MMF & Technical Textiles
Global demand is shifting rapidly from cotton to performance apparel, activewear, and specialized technical textiles. Developing capabilities in advanced synthetic fibers and functional yarns allows companies to participate in the fastest-growing global segments and offer comprehensive sourcing solutions.

4. Ecosystem Integration
A fragmented value chain increases coordination costs and lengthens lead times. Integrated ecosystems—whether fully vertical or hub-and-spoke structures—reduce procurement complexity, improve quality consistency, and shorten development cycles, directly replicating the advantages of global leaders.

5. Labour Strategy
Labor availability and workforce stability are becoming larger constraints than cost. A capital-to-labor model that moves factories to labor-surplus states improves retention, lowers migration disruptions, and builds long-term operational resilience.

6. Sustainability & Traceability
Global brands increasingly view sustainability, Digital Product Passports (DPPs), and verified traceability as mandatory market entry tickets rather than branding exercises. Investments in renewable energy, circularity, and ZLD lock in preferred-supplier relationships.

7. Customer Franchise
Scale is built through long-term relationships, consistent delivery, and customer trust, which compounds over time. A diversified, high-quality customer base minimizes concentration risk and creates a massive entry barrier for competitors.

8. Management Execution
Favorable trade policies (FTAs) alone do not guarantee success; operational readiness and execution determine market share capture. Disciplined capital allocation, forward-looking investments in technology, and strategic geographic diversification define long-term winners.
