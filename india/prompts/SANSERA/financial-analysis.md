# Prompt 5 — Sansera Engineering Ltd.: Financial & Reinvestment Economics

## Objective

Using ONLY the uploaded Sansera Engineering Ltd. financial statements, Annual Reports, investor presentations, earnings-call transcripts, company filings, and other uploaded company disclosures, evaluate the **historical and current financial economics of Sansera Engineering Ltd.**

This is NOT a generic financial ratio analysis.

The objective is to understand:

- How Sansera's revenue growth has translated into EBITDA and EBIT
- Why profitability and margins have changed
- Why ROE has changed
- Why ROCE has changed
- How the company's capital employed has evolved
- Whether incremental capital is beginning to generate attractive incremental returns
- Whether historical capex is translating into revenue and operating profit
- Whether reported earnings are converting into operating cash flow and free cash flow
- How working capital affects cash generation
- Whether the balance sheet provides financial flexibility
- How management has allocated capital across its major growth initiatives
- Whether the existing financial evidence demonstrates a credible reinvestment engine

The central question is:

> **Is Sansera increasingly able to convert capital invested in manufacturing capacity, technology and capabilities into EBIT, cash flow and incremental returns on capital?**

This analysis should establish the **historical/current financial foundation** for a later scenario-analysis, monitoring-dashboard and DCF exercise.

Do NOT perform the later scenario analysis in this prompt.

---

# Analytical Framework

For every major conclusion, distinguish between:

**FACT**  
Directly supported by the financial statements or company disclosures.

↓

**CALCULATION**  
Derived mathematically from reported figures.

↓

**INTERPRETATION**  
Reasonable interpretation of the financial evidence.

↓

**EVIDENCE GAP**  
Where the available documents are insufficient to establish a conclusion.

Do not present interpretations as management statements.

Do not manufacture precision where the financial statements do not provide sufficient information.
