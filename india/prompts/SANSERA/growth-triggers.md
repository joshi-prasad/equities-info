## Mandatory Analytical Rules

### 1. Separate four levels of evidence

For every important statement, distinguish:

**Fact**
What has actually happened.

↓

**Management expectation**
What management says is likely to happen.

↓

**Analytical interpretation**
What the evidence reasonably suggests.

↓

**Financial implication**
How the development could affect revenue/EBITDA.

Never present management expectations as facts.

---

### 2. Distinguish order-book value from near-term revenue

This is **especially important for Sansera**.

Where order-book figures are cumulative programme value, explicitly distinguish them from:

* annual revenue
* executable revenue
* current-year revenue
* production ramp

Do not convert cumulative order-book numbers into implied annual earnings unless the company provides sufficient evidence.

---

### 3. Distinguish capacity from utilization

A new plant, machine, hangar or capability is **not itself an earnings trigger**.

Determine:

**Capacity created → customer demand → utilization → revenue → operating leverage**

---

### 4. Distinguish qualification from commercialization

FAI, PPAP, customer approval and development activity should not automatically be treated as recurring production revenue.

---

### 5. Use management's latest commentary as the primary source

When older and newer commentary conflict:

1. prioritize the latest commentary;
2. explicitly identify the change;
3. do not silently reconcile contradictory statements.

---

### 6. Do not assume that every strategic initiative becomes a near-term earnings driver

Classify developments as:

* **Near-term earnings driver**
* **Near-term enabler**
* **Medium-term opportunity**
* **Longer-term optionality**
* **Not yet financially material**

---

### 7. Do not include valuation

Do not discuss:

* P/E
* EV/EBITDA
* DCF
* target price
* fair value
* market capitalization
* whether the stock is cheap/expensive.

---

### 8. Do not rank Sansera's businesses as “best” or “worst”

Describe differences in:

* growth stage
* execution stage
* revenue contribution
* capacity
* qualification
* timing
* disclosed economics

without producing an overall winner.

---

### 9. Do not manufacture probabilities

Do not assign numerical probabilities.

If discussing likelihood, use only:

* High evidence
* Moderate evidence
* Limited evidence
* Insufficient evidence

and explain what supports that classification.

---

### 10. Stay within the 6–12 month horizon

A development can be mentioned if it is strategically important, but clearly label it:

**Near-term (0–12 months)**
**Medium-term (>12 months)**
**Long-term / optionality**

Do not allow long-term opportunities to dominate the near-term earnings analysis.

---

The purpose of this exercise is **not to find everything that could make Sansera grow**.

It is to answer:

> **“What specifically has to happen over the next 6–12 months for Sansera's reported earnings trajectory to change materially, where does each trigger currently stand, and what observable evidence will tell us whether it is actually converting into earnings?”**
