# Narrative Drift & Expectations Gap

## Objective

Analyze how management's narrative has evolved across all quarterly conference calls and determine whether the company's execution supports the expectations being created.

Focus on identifying changes in priorities, confidence, and business expectations.

Do **not** discuss valuation or stock price.

Use **only** uploaded company documents, especially conference call transcripts.

---

## Output Structure

### 1. Narrative Evolution

Compare management commentary across:

* FY25 Q4
* FY26 Q1
* FY26 Q2
* FY26 Q3
* FY26 Q4
* FY27 Q1

Identify:

* Themes that remained consistent
* Themes becoming stronger
* Themes becoming weaker
* New themes introduced
* Topics that disappeared

For every change explain:

* What changed?
* Why did it change?
* Is execution supporting the narrative?

---

### 2. Management Expectations

Based on repeated commentary, what is management implicitly expecting over the next 6–12 months?

Discuss expectations around:

* Growth
* ROA / ROE
* Operating leverage
* Credit quality
* Portfolio mix
* Funding
* Technology / AI
* Capital allocation

Summarize in plain English:

> **"Management appears to believe..."**

---

### 3. Execution vs Expectations (Most Important)

Compare:

Management Expectations

↓

Business Evidence

Classify each expectation as:

* Ahead of expectations
* On track
* Behind expectations
* Too early to judge

Support every conclusion with evidence.

---

### 4. Potential Expectation Gaps

Identify where expectations may be:

* Too optimistic
* Reasonable
* Too conservative

Explain:

* What could surprise positively?
* What could disappoint?
* Which assumptions are least proven?

---

### 5. What Matters Most Over the Next 6–12 Months

Identify the **5–10 operational developments** that will determine whether management's narrative continues to gain credibility.

For each include:

* Why it matters
* Leading KPI
* Confidence level
* Expected timeline

---

## Final Output

Summarize in one page:

### A. Five narrative changes that matter most

### B. Five management assumptions that appear well supported

### C. Five assumptions that still require proof

### D. Biggest positive surprise that could lead to earnings upgrades

### E. Biggest disappointment that could lead to earnings downgrades

Finally answer:

> **"Based on management's evolving narrative and current execution, what appears to be under control, what remains uncertain, and what will determine whether the next 6–12 months exceed or fall short of expectations?"**
