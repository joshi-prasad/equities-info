# MACPOWER: Product Mix and Order Book Analysis

Develop a fact-based analysis of MACPOWER's product mix and order-book trajectory.

## 1. Product-category revenue mix

Identify the company's major machine categories and establish the following for each category, wherever information is available:

- Revenue contribution in absolute terms.
- Percentage contribution to total revenue.
- Number of machines sold.
- Average selling price (ASP).
- Gross margin or contribution margin.
- Any disclosed differences in profitability between machine categories.

Include turning centres, VMCs, HMCs, VTLs, DTCs, DCMs, turn-mill machines, 5-axis machines and other relevant categories.

First establish the correct product classification. Do not treat product series, machine variants and machine categories as interchangeable.

Identify:
- Which machine category generates the maximum revenue?
- Which categories are gaining importance?
- What is the contribution of premium/high-end machines?
- What is the contribution of the NEXA series?

IMPORTANT: NEXA is a product series, not necessarily a separate machine category. Avoid double counting.

If product-wise revenue or margin information is not disclosed, explicitly state that it is not established by the available sources. Do not estimate product margins using selling prices or general industry assumptions.

## 2. Order-book evolution

Prepare a quarterly table covering the last 4–5 quarters.

Include:
- Quarter-end pending order book.
- Year-on-year order-book growth.
- Quarter-on-quarter growth.
- Order inflows, wherever disclosed.
- Revenue executed during the quarter.
- Order book to annual revenue ratio.
- NEXA contribution to pending order book.
- Domestic, export, private-sector and government/defence composition, wherever available.

Identify whether order-book growth is accelerating, decelerating or remaining stable.

Reconcile conflicting order-book figures across company presentations and conference calls. Prioritize the latest specific quarter-end disclosure.

## 3. Order-book quality

Investigate:
- Repeat-order contribution.
- Customer concentration.
- Sector concentration.
- Government and defence order exposure.
- Expected execution timelines.
- Cancellation risks.
- Whether order-book growth is translating into revenue growth.

## 4. Investment interpretation

Based strictly on the extracted facts, assess:
- Which product categories appear to be driving business growth?
- Whether the product mix is shifting towards higher-value machines.
- Whether order-book growth provides adequate revenue visibility.
- What information is still missing to establish the quality of growth.

Keep FACTS and INTERPRETATION strictly separate. Attribute management commentary and forward-looking statements to management.
