# Objective: Investigate Jyoti CNC's Manufacturing Capacity, Order Book, Execution Capabilities and Market Position

Having understood the broad business model, investigate whether Jyoti CNC has the manufacturing capacity, execution capabilities and market position required to sustain its growth.

The objective is to understand the relationship between demand, order inflows, manufacturing capacity, order execution and revenue growth.

Maintain strict separation between FACTS, MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY and INTERPRETATION.

## 1. Existing Manufacturing Capacity and Capacity Expansion

### A. Existing Capacity and Utilisation

1. What is Jyoti CNC's current installed manufacturing capacity? Clearly distinguish between Indian standalone capacity and the capacity of overseas subsidiaries such as Huron.
2. What has been the actual capacity utilisation over the last 5–6 quarters?
3. How has the number of machines manufactured or sold evolved over this period?
4. Is the company operating close to its practical manufacturing capacity? Identify evidence of capacity constraints, including management commentary about utilisation exceeding 100% during peak periods.
5. Is the existing manufacturing infrastructure sufficient to support current demand, or has capacity become a binding constraint on revenue growth?

Present quarterly capacity utilisation, installed capacity and machine production/sales in a table wherever data is available.

### B. Status of the Additional 10,000-Machine Capacity Expansion

The company is undertaking a capacity expansion of an additional 10,000 machines per annum, taking its stated capacity from 6,000 to 16,000 machines per annum.

Investigate the following:

1. What exactly is being constructed as part of this expansion?
2. Is this an entirely new manufacturing facility or an expansion of existing manufacturing infrastructure?
3. What manufacturing activities and facilities are being added? Specifically examine:
   - Machine shop
   - Foundry
   - Sheet metal fabrication
   - Paint shop
   - Assembly lines
   - Other backward-integrated manufacturing processes
4. What was the original commissioning timeline?
5. What progress had been reported in the latest available company disclosure?
6. What was the actual status of construction, machinery installation, testing and commissioning of each major manufacturing section?
7. Were there any delays or bottlenecks in individual manufacturing processes?
8. Does the commissioning of the facility mean that it is immediately capable of operating at full production capacity? Explain the distinction between physical completion, trial production, commercial production and full-scale utilisation.
9. What is the latest disclosed commissioning status? Do not assume that the originally announced September 2026 timeline was achieved.
10. What is the expected production ramp-up timeline, if management has disclosed one?

Clearly distinguish between:
- Facility construction completion.
- Machinery installation.
- Trial production.
- Commercial production.
- Achievement of the stated annual production capacity.

### C. Capacity Expansion and Production Mix

1. What types of machines will the additional capacity primarily manufacture?
2. What proportion of the new capacity is intended for entry-level, mid-range and high-end machines, if disclosed?
3. Is the additional capacity primarily designed for high-volume standard machines or specialised high-value machines?
4. How will the new facility change the company's ability to execute orders across different product categories?
5. Are there any supporting manufacturing processes or backward-integration facilities that could become bottlenecks even after the new capacity becomes operational?

## 2. Order Book: Composition, Evolution and Execution Capability

### A. Historical Order Book Evolution

Analyse the order book over the last 4–5 quarters.

Prepare a table showing:

- Reporting date.
- Opening order book, wherever disclosed.
- Order inflows during the quarter.
- Orders executed during the quarter.
- Closing order book.
- Year-on-year and sequential changes, wherever calculable.
- Order-book-to-revenue ratio.

Use the following relationship to reconcile the order book wherever the necessary information is available:

Closing Order Book = Opening Order Book + Order Inflows - Orders Executed

Identify periods where this reconciliation does not work and explain whether cancellations, adjustments, foreign exchange movements or differences in reporting scope could be responsible. Do not manufacture explanations.

### B. Order Book in Terms of Machine Quantities

The company generally reports its order book in rupee value.

Investigate whether the company has disclosed:

1. Total outstanding machines in the order book.
2. Number of machines ordered under entry-level, mid-range and high-end categories.
3. Product-category-wise order-book value.
4. Average selling price or implied value per machine by product category.
5. Whether the order book is dominated by a few large, high-value machines or a large number of standard machines.

If the total number of machines in the outstanding order book is not disclosed, explicitly state so.

Do not estimate the number of machines by dividing the total order book by one average selling price because the product mix is materially different across categories.

### C. Order Book Quality and Revenue Visibility

1. What is the industry-wise composition of the current order book?
2. How has this composition changed over the last 4–5 quarters?
3. Which sectors are contributing most to order inflows?
4. What is the expected execution timeline for the current order book?
5. What proportion of the order book is expected to convert into revenue over the next 12 months, if disclosed?
6. Are there differences in execution timelines between aerospace and defence, automotive, EMS and general engineering orders?
7. What is the extent of customer advances associated with the order book?
8. Are there any significant order cancellations, postponements or changes in delivery schedules?
9. Is the order book sufficiently diversified across customers, sectors and product categories?

Distinguish between order-book value, expected revenue conversion and actual cash collection.

### D. Can the Company Execute Its Existing Order Book?

This is a critical question.

1. Could Jyoti CNC execute its existing order book using its previous installed manufacturing capacity of 6,000 machines per annum?
2. To what extent was the additional 10,000-machine capacity necessary to execute the existing order book versus supporting future order inflows?
3. What was management's explanation for limiting or moderating order acceptance when the existing facilities were operating at high utilisation?
4. What is the likely impact of the additional capacity on delivery timelines and order execution?
5. Will the new facility materially improve the company's ability to accept fresh orders?
6. Could bottlenecks in foundry operations, skilled manpower, imported components, assembly or quality control restrict the utilisation of the expanded capacity?

Clearly separate management's explanations from conclusions that can be established using disclosed operating data.

## 3. Market Position and Industry Opportunity

### A. Jyoti CNC's Domestic Market Position

1. What is Jyoti CNC's disclosed market share in the Indian CNC machine tool market?
2. What is the source and reporting period of this market-share estimate?
3. Is the market-share estimate independently sourced or based on management's assessment?
4. Does the company disclose market share separately for entry-level, mid-range and high-end CNC machines?
5. Does it disclose market share across different customer industries or product categories?
6. How has its market position evolved over time, based on available disclosures?

Do not manufacture market-share estimates where information is unavailable.

### B. Industry Growth and Demand Drivers

1. What is the estimated size of the Indian CNC machine tool market?
2. What is the historical and expected growth rate of the industry according to the available sources?
3. What proportion of Indian machine-tool demand is currently met through imports?
4. Which industries are driving incremental demand for CNC machines?
5. What is the role of aerospace and defence, automotive, EVs, EMS, general engineering, railways and semiconductor manufacturing?
6. Is demand driven primarily by new manufacturing capacity creation, replacement of existing imported machines, technological upgrades or a combination of these factors?
7. Which end-user industries have the strongest disclosed demand visibility?

Clearly distinguish between actual industry data, third-party industry estimates and management's expectations.

### C. Jyoti CNC's Stated Competitive Advantages

Without conducting a competitor comparison, identify the competitive advantages that Jyoti CNC claims or demonstrates through its disclosures.

Examine:

- Backward integration.
- Manufacturing capabilities.
- Product breadth.
- 5-axis and multi-axis technology.
- Application engineering.
- Research and development.
- Huron's technology and international presence.
- Customer relationships and installed machine base.
- After-sales service and technical support.
- Domestic manufacturing and import substitution.

For each claimed advantage, explain what evidence supports it and what remains unverified.

Do not conclude that the company is gaining market share merely because its revenue or order book is growing.

## 4. Additional Questions Relevant to Understanding Future Execution

### A. Product Mix and Volume Growth

1. How has the number of machines sold evolved over the last 4–5 quarters?
2. What is the split between entry-level, mid-range and high-end machine sales?
3. How has the product mix changed over time?
4. Is the company increasingly dependent on entry-level machines for volume growth?
5. What are the implications of this product mix for revenue per machine and manufacturing requirements? Avoid detailed margin analysis at this stage.

### B. Manufacturing Readiness and Working Capital

1. How much inventory has the company accumulated in preparation for the capacity expansion?
2. What portion of inventory relates to existing production requirements versus the new facility?
3. How does the expansion affect the procurement of raw materials and critical imported components?
4. Is there sufficient skilled manpower to operate the expanded manufacturing infrastructure?
5. What operational challenges could arise while ramping up the new facility?

### C. Execution Risks

Identify the major operational risks that could prevent the company from converting its order book into revenue.

Consider:
- Delays in commissioning the new facility.
- Delays in backward-integrated manufacturing processes.
- Availability of skilled employees.
- Imported component availability.
- Long manufacturing and assembly cycles.
- Customer acceptance and commissioning delays.
- Huron's export-control and regulatory issues.

Rank these risks based on their potential importance to near-term execution, but distinguish evidence-based risks from analytical assessments.

## 5. Final Assessment

Conclude by answering the following questions:

1. Is Jyoti CNC currently demand-constrained or capacity-constrained?
2. How much of the next phase of growth depends on the successful commissioning and ramp-up of the additional 10,000-machine capacity?
3. Does the existing order book provide adequate visibility for the next 12–18 months?
4. Is the order book growing because of higher demand, improved market penetration or changes in product mix? What can actually be established from the disclosures?
5. Can the company execute the existing order book and simultaneously accept meaningful fresh orders?
6. What are the three most important operational factors to monitor over the next 2–3 quarters?

For each conclusion, clearly identify the supporting facts and the assumptions involved.

## Important Research Instructions

- Prioritize the most recent company disclosures.
- Give greater weight to recent operating developments than older management expectations.
- Use older disclosures to reconstruct historical trends.
- Clearly identify the reporting date and standalone/consolidated basis of every figure.
- Separate FACTS, MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY and INTERPRETATION.
- Attribute management guidance and expectations explicitly.
- Do not assume that planned capacity is equivalent to commissioned or commercially productive capacity.
- Do not assume that the order book will automatically convert into revenue.
- Do not estimate outstanding machine quantities without adequate product-wise information.
- Do not manufacture market-share estimates.
- If information is unavailable, explicitly state: "Not established by the available sources."

**Scope restriction:** This exercise is focused on manufacturing capacity, order-book quality, execution capabilities and the company's disclosed market position. Do not conduct detailed competitor analysis, unit economics, DuPont analysis, ROE/ROCE analysis, valuation or investment recommendations. These will be examined separately.
