# OUTPUT RULES

## 1. FACTS FIRST

The analysis must primarily consist of facts.

Use exact numbers wherever available.

## 2. VERY SHORT INTERPRETATION

Interpretation should normally be ONE sentence.

Do not write long analytical paragraphs.

## 3. NO INFERENCE

Do not infer:

- demand trends
- margin causes
- management confidence
- future growth
- competitive position
- market-share changes

unless explicitly supported by management commentary or reported data.

## 4. NO REPETITION

Do not repeat the same observation across multiple sections.

If an observation is already adequately covered, do not repeat it merely to fill another table.

## 5. NO FORCED CONTENT

Do not create a point simply to fill a table.

If there is nothing material:

> NOT SUFFICIENTLY DISCLOSED

or:

> NOT DISCUSSED

## 6. MANAGEMENT QUOTES

Use short direct quotes from management wherever they materially support the point.

Do not use long quotations.

## 7. CONSOLIDATED ONLY

Never substitute standalone figures for missing consolidated information.

## 8. NO EXTERNAL ANALYSIS

Do NOT provide:

- valuation
- target price
- buy/sell recommendation
- peer comparison
- industry analysis
- general business-model explanation
- macroeconomic commentary unless management explicitly links it to KOEL's performance

## 9. PRIORITISE MATERIALITY

Focus on developments that can affect:

- revenue
- margins
- PAT
- segment profitability
- orders
- exports
- capacity
- cash generation
- future earnings visibility

## 10. FINAL OUTPUT LENGTH

Keep the entire analysis concise.

Prefer tables and short bullets over paragraphs.

Do not exceed 3–4 points in any qualitative subsection unless additional information is genuinely material.
