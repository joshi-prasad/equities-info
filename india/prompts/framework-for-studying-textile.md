Your framework is already much better than what most investors use. The sequence is logical because it moves from **understanding → hypothesis → market expectations → evidence** rather than starting with ratios and valuations.

For textile companies specifically, I would make a few modifications.

## Suggested Order

### 1. Business Model

Questions:

* What exactly do they sell?

  * Yarn?
  * Fabric?
  * Garments?
  * Home textiles?
  * Technical textiles?
* Domestic vs Export?
* Branded vs Commodity?
* B2B vs B2C?

For example:

* KPR = integrated textile + garments
* Pearl = garment exporter
* Indo Count = home textiles
* Arvind = fabric + brands + garments

This step should answer:

> "Where exactly do they sit in the textile ecosystem?"

---

### 2. Industry Structure & Value Chain (First Principles)

I would slightly expand your value-chain step.

Study:

Raw cotton
→ Ginning
→ Spinning
→ Yarn
→ Fabric
→ Processing/Dyeing
→ Garments/Home Textiles
→ Brands/Retail

Questions:

* Where is value added?
* Where are margins highest?
* Where is differentiation possible?
* Which stages are commodities?

For FTA beneficiaries this is critical.

Many investors simply hear "textile company" and miss that:

* Pearl Global is much closer to the customer.
* KPR captures multiple stages.
* Nitin is more upstream.

Those are very different economics.

---

### 3. Competitive Advantage / Moat

I would add this as a dedicated step.

Questions:

Why can't another textile company do the same thing?

Possible moats:

* Scale
* Customer relationships
* Design capability
* Compliance certifications
* Large integrated facilities
* Cost advantage
* Fast turnaround times

Textiles are usually considered commodity businesses.

Your job is to determine:

> Which of these are less commodity-like?

---

### 4. Growth Triggers

Now move to growth.

Examples:

#### Structural

* China+1
* UK FTA
* EU FTA
* Shift from Bangladesh
* Technical textiles

#### Company Specific

* Capacity expansion
* New customers
* Product mix improvement
* Backward integration

---

### 5. Expectations Gap

This is where alpha usually comes from.

Questions:

What does the market already know?

Example:

Everybody knows:

* UK FTA signed
* EU negotiations progressing

Questions:

* Is the benefit already priced in?
* Is the market overestimating benefits?
* Is the market underestimating them?

---

### 6. Narrative Drift & Management Commentary

Excellent step.

I would compare:

**Management 2 years ago**
vs
**Management today**

Look for:

* New language appearing repeatedly
* Export commentary changing
* Margin aspirations changing
* Capacity plans changing

Most investors read the latest concall.

Few compare 10 concalls.

The drift matters more than the latest quarter.

---

### 7. Capital Allocation

I would add this before financial analysis.

Questions:

What did management do with cash?

* Reinvest?
* Reduce debt?
* Dividends?
* Buybacks?
* Diversification?

A great textile business with poor capital allocation often becomes mediocre.

Example:

Two companies may earn the same ROCE.

One reinvests at high returns.

The other builds unrelated businesses.

Huge difference.

---

### 8. Financial Analysis

Now the numbers.

Since you've already uploaded peer comparisons, I'd focus on:

#### Profitability

* ROCE
* ROE
* OPM

#### Growth

* Sales CAGR
* Profit CAGR

#### Balance Sheet

* Debt
* Interest coverage

#### Cash

* CFO vs PAT

#### Cyclicality

* How bad did earnings get in weak years?

For textiles:

Consistency matters more than one great year.

---

## One Additional Step I Think You Are Missing

### 9. Industry Cycle Position

This is extremely important in textiles.

Textiles are cyclical.

Questions:

* Cotton prices high or low?
* Capacity utilization?
* Export demand?
* Inventory levels?
* Retail demand in US/EU?

A mediocre company bought at the bottom of the cycle can outperform a great company bought at the top.

You don't need perfect cycle forecasting.

You need to know:

> Are we near pessimism, optimism, or somewhere in the middle?

---

# My Final Framework

For these textile companies I would use:

1. Business Model
2. Industry Structure & Value Chain
3. Competitive Advantage / Moat
4. Industry Cycle Position
5. Growth Triggers
6. Expectations Gap
7. Narrative Drift / Management Commentary
8. Capital Allocation
9. Financial Analysis
10. Valuation (only after everything else)

Notice that **valuation comes last**.

Many investors start with PE ratios. By then they are already anchored.

You seem to naturally think from first principles and business quality first, which is particularly useful in cyclical sectors like textiles where a low PE often means "peak earnings" and a high PE often means "trough earnings."
