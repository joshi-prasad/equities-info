# APAR Industries — Unit Economics & Value Chain from First Principles

Analyze APAR Industries' unit economics and value chain from first principles.

The objective is to understand how APAR converts raw materials into products, where value is added, where costs and risks enter the chain, and what economics APAR earns from producing and selling one incremental unit.

Focus on the major businesses separately:
• Conductors
• Cable Solutions
• Speciality Oils & Lubricants
• Polymers
• Telecom / other material businesses where relevant

Do not assume that all segments have the same economics.

## PART 1 — VALUE CHAIN FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES

For each major business, trace:

Raw materials → sourcing → processing/manufacturing → value addition → testing/qualification → distribution → customer → end application.

Identify:

1. Major raw materials and key inputs.
2. Which inputs are commodity-priced versus differentiated/specialised.
3. Where APAR adds meaningful value.
4. Which manufacturing/processing steps are performed in-house.
5. Which steps are outsourced or dependent on third parties.
6. Key suppliers, distributors, contractors or other external dependencies where disclosed.
7. Where pricing power exists at each major handoff.
8. Where APAR may face cost leakage, intermediary costs or supply-chain friction.
9. Any important bottlenecks or capacity constraints.
10. Why APAR has chosen to vertically integrate certain activities while outsourcing others, but only where the sources provide evidence.

Use this table:

| Business | Raw Materials | Major Processing Steps | In-house vs Outsourced | Key External Dependencies | Where Value Is Added | Key Bottleneck / Dependency | Source |

Do not assume that vertical integration automatically produces higher margins or that outsourcing automatically produces lower capital intensity. State only what the sources establish.

## PART 2 — DEFINE THE FUNDAMENTAL UNIT

For each major business, identify the most economically meaningful "unit" for analysing incremental economics.

Examples may include:
• ₹ of conductor sold / MT of conductor
• km of cable sold
• KL of speciality oil
• MT of polymer
• another unit if more appropriate.

For each business explain briefly WHY that is the appropriate unit.

If the economics cannot be meaningfully calculated at the physical-unit level because of product mix or pricing differences, say so.

## PART 3 — UNIT ECONOMICS

For each major business, determine as far as the sources allow:

Revenue per unit
− Raw material cost per unit
− Other direct variable costs per unit
= Gross value addition / contribution per unit

Also identify, where available:

• Material cost
• Processing/manufacturing cost
• Freight/logistics
• Employee cost directly associated with production
• Other variable costs
• Fixed manufacturing costs
• Working-capital requirement
• Capital required to support incremental production

Do NOT manufacture per-unit numbers.

If actual unit economics are not disclosed, identify the relevant disclosed financial metrics and explain what is and is not possible to calculate.

Clearly distinguish:

FACT → directly disclosed number
CALCULATION → arithmetic derived from disclosed numbers
INFERENCE → interpretation based on the evidence

## PART 4 — SCALING ECONOMICS

Determine what happens when APAR produces/sells more units.

Look specifically for evidence of:

• Economies of scale
• Operating leverage
• Better capacity utilisation
• Procurement advantages
• Manufacturing efficiencies
• Fixed-cost absorption
• Product-mix improvement
• Diseconomies or increasing complexity
• Working-capital requirements increasing with growth
• Additional capex required for growth

Distinguish between businesses where incremental volume mainly requires working capital and businesses where incremental volume requires substantial new manufacturing capacity.

## PART 5 — CUSTOMER ACQUISITION / RETENTION

Do NOT force the SaaS-style CAC/LTV framework onto APAR.

Instead determine how APAR obtains and retains business:

• Qualification / approval
• Tendering
• RFQs
• OEM relationships
• Long-term contracts
• Distributor relationships
• Repeat orders
• Customer-specific products
• Technical integration / switching costs

Where possible, identify the commercial cost or effort required to win and retain a customer.

If CAC or LTV cannot be calculated from the available sources, explicitly state:

"Not disclosed / not calculable from available sources."

## OUTPUT REQUIREMENT

Prioritize FACTS over interpretation.

For each section:

1. Give the factual evidence first.
2. Show calculations separately from reported figures.
3. Clearly identify information gaps.
4. Keep interpretation to a maximum of 3 bullets at the end.

Do not discuss valuation, competitive positioning, management quality, growth triggers, bull/base/bear scenarios or investment conclusions.
