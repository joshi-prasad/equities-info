# APAR Industries — Financial Statement Analysis

Conduct a detailed but easy-to-understand financial statement analysis of APAR Industries from a long-only momentum investor's perspective, focusing on the next 6–12 months.

## 1. Mandatory Instructions

- Present findings in **tables wherever possible**.
- Analyse the annual trend from **FY24 to FY26**, and include **Q1 FY27** wherever comparable quarterly data is available.
- Separate **documented facts** from **analytical interpretations**. Never mix the two.
- Prioritise the latest disclosures when comparing information across periods.
- Show formulas, calculations, units, and data sources wherever relevant.
- Do not invent missing figures. Clearly flag unavailable data, assumptions, and inconsistencies.
- Explain technical financial concepts in simple language while maintaining institutional-research-level analytical depth.
- Focus on earnings momentum, profitability, capital efficiency, cash generation, and financial risks.

## 2. ROE and DuPont Analysis

Analyse Return on Equity (ROE) for FY24, FY25, FY26, and Q1 FY27 wherever meaningful.

Use the three-factor DuPont framework:

ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier

Show the individual components, their historical trends, and their contribution to changes in ROE.

Distinguish reported ROE from annualised quarterly ROE. Use average equity where appropriate and explain the methodology.

## 3. ROCE and DuPont Analysis

Calculate and analyse Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) for FY24–FY26 and Q1 FY27 wherever possible.

- Show the formula and definition of capital employed used.
- Break ROCE into operating profitability and capital turnover components.
- Explain whether changes in ROCE arise from margins, asset utilisation, or capital employed.
- Identify whether capital efficiency is improving or deteriorating.

Keep the methodology consistent across periods. Clearly flag any differences in definitions or unavailable data.

## 4. Incremental ROCE

Analyse incremental ROCE to assess whether additional capital is generating attractive returns.

- Compare changes in operating profit with changes in capital employed between FY24, FY25, and FY26.
- Explain the formula and limitations of incremental ROCE.
- Evaluate whether recent growth appears to create value or require disproportionately more capital.
- Include Q1 FY27 only if a meaningful, comparable calculation is possible.

Do not interpret a single-period change as proof of a long-term trend.

## 5. Historical Income Statement Trends

Create a table covering FY24, FY25, FY26, and Q1 FY27, including wherever available:

- Revenue from operations and year-on-year growth.
- EBITDA and EBITDA margin.
- EBIT and EBIT margin.
- Profit before tax.
- Profit after tax (PAT) and PAT margin.
- Year-on-year growth in EBITDA and PAT.
- Exceptional items and other material factors affecting reported earnings.

Explain what is driving changes in revenue, profitability, and margins. Distinguish volume growth, product mix, pricing, operating leverage, and non-recurring effects wherever the documents provide sufficient evidence.

For Q1 FY27, compare year-on-year performance with Q1 FY26 rather than directly comparing a single quarter with a full financial year.

## 6. Historical Cash Flow Trends

Prepare a table covering FY24, FY25, FY26, and Q1 FY27 wherever comparable data is available.

Include:

- Cash flow from operating activities (OCF).
- Capital expenditure (capex).
- Free cash flow (FCF), with the formula clearly defined.
- OCF / EBITDA.
- OCF / PAT.
- Year-on-year trends and material working-capital movements.

Explain whether accounting profits are converting into cash, whether growth is consuming additional cash, and whether internally generated funds are sufficient to support planned capital expenditure.

For Q1 FY27, use the corresponding prior-year quarter for comparison where available. Do not compare quarterly cash flows directly with full-year figures without clearly explaining the limitation.

## 7. Working Capital Economics

Analyse the company's working-capital requirements and their impact on growth and financial resilience.

Where data is available, calculate and compare:

- Trade receivable days.
- Inventory days.
- Trade payable days.
- Cash conversion cycle.
- Net working capital and working capital as a percentage of revenue.
- Changes in inventory, receivables, payables, and other material operating current assets and liabilities.
- Working-capital requirements relative to incremental revenue.

Explain why working capital is increasing or decreasing, whether the trend is sustainable, and whether it is constraining cash generation.

Assess the implications of the QIP for working-capital funding and financial costs, distinguishing actual reported outcomes from management expectations and third-party projections.

## 8. Cash-Flow Quality

Evaluate the quality and sustainability of reported earnings.

Examine:

- OCF relative to EBITDA and PAT.
- The effect of working-capital movements on operating cash flow.
- The relationship between PAT, depreciation, non-cash expenses, and operating cash generation.
- Whether reported earnings depend materially on exceptional items or temporary commodity and inventory effects.
- Whether capex is adequately funded by operating cash flow.
- Whether free cash flow is improving or deteriorating.
- Whether cash generation supports sustainable growth without excessive borrowing or additional equity.

Distinguish temporary cash-flow fluctuations from persistent weaknesses.

## 9. Final Summary for a Momentum Investor

Conclude with a summary table containing:

1. Financial metric.
2. FY24–FY26 trend.
3. Q1 FY27 signal.
4. Evidence supporting improvement or deterioration.
5. Implications for earnings momentum over the next 6–12 months.
6. Key risk or indicator to monitor in the next quarterly result.

Identify the **five most important financial indicators** for tracking APAR Industries' next quarterly result.

Finally, state whether the financial evidence suggests improving, stable, or deteriorating profitability, capital efficiency, and cash-flow quality. Support each conclusion with documented facts and clearly separated analytical interpretations.

Do not provide a stock-price target or buy/sell recommendation.
