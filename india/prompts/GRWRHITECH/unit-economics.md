# Objective: Understand the Unit Economics and Scalability of Garware Hi-Tech Films

Analyse the unit economics of Garware Hi-Tech Films Limited (GHFL) to determine whether its core business model is fundamentally profitable at the product and customer level, and whether scaling the business can generate sustainable incremental profits.

Focus particularly on Solar Control Films (SCF), Paint Protection Films (PPF), and the distribution models of Garware Application Studios (GAS) and Garware Home Solutions (GHS).

Address the following questions:

1. **Product-level unit economics:** What are the economics of manufacturing and selling SCF and PPF? Wherever information is available, examine revenue realisation, raw material costs, variable manufacturing costs, contribution margins and product-level profitability. Use appropriate units such as per square foot, per roll or per unit. Also examine IPD briefly for comparison.

2. **Profitability and scalability:** How do fixed costs, variable costs, capacity utilisation and operating leverage affect profitability? At what level of utilisation does the business become economically attractive? Does incremental production generate attractive incremental margins?

3. **Customer acquisition efficiency:** How much does Garware invest in acquiring customers through GAS and GHS? Examine customer acquisition costs, conversion rates, average transaction value, repeat purchases, customer retention and lifetime value, wherever disclosed.

4. **GAS and GHS economics:** Understand the economics of these distribution models. Who bears the initial investment and operating expenses? How does Garware earn revenue? What is the revenue and profitability contribution per studio, wherever available? Can the network expand without a proportionate increase in capital investment?

5. **Impact of backward integration:** Evaluate how the upcoming TPU manufacturing facility and its relationship with Lubrizol could change PPF's cost structure, contribution margins and overall profitability. Distinguish management's expected benefits from realised benefits.

6. **Incremental returns from scaling:** Assess whether additional SCF and PPF capacity, new studios and higher sales volumes can generate incremental profits without proportionate increases in working capital, fixed costs and capital expenditure.

## Research and Output Instructions

- Prioritise actual company disclosures over theoretical assumptions.
- Clearly distinguish FACTS, MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY and INTERPRETATION.
- Use the latest available information and identify the reporting period.
- Do not manufacture product-level margins, CAC, customer lifetime value, break-even points or studio profitability if these are not disclosed.
- Where exact unit economics are unavailable, identify the data required to calculate them and explain what can and cannot be established.
- Keep the analysis concise and focused. Use tables wherever useful.

**Objective:** Establish whether Garware's core products and customer acquisition channels have attractive underlying economics, whether those economics improve with scale, and whether growth is likely to create sustainable incremental profitability rather than simply increasing revenue.
