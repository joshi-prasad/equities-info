# BHEL

### **1. Nuclear Reactors**
* **Market Position & Capacity**: BHEL is the **only company associated with all three stages of the Indian Nuclear Power Programme**. BHEL-supplied equipment accounts for **60% of India's nuclear power installed capacity** on the secondary side.
* **Track Record**: Up to FY 2025–26, BHEL has supplied **15 nuclear-based utility sets** in India.
* **R&D & Future Pipeline**: Current R&D and technological development efforts focus on 700 MWe nuclear plants, Controls & Instrumentation (C&I) for nuclear plants, and systems based on Pressurised Heavy Water Reactor (PHWR) and Pressurised Water Reactor (PWR) technologies. Non-thermal order visibility is further supported by the Government's target to expand national nuclear power capacity to 100 GW by 2047.

---

### **2. Gas Turbines**
* **Track Record**: BHEL has supplied **105 gas-based utility sets** in India since inception up to FY 2025–26.
* **Services & Joint Venture**: BHEL operates a joint venture with GE, USA—**BHEL-GE Gas Turbine Services Private Limited (BGGTS)** (BHEL holds 50% minus 1 share)—focused on the repair and servicing of GE-designed gas turbines.
  * **BGGTS Financial Performance (FY 2025–26)**: Revenue from operations stood at **₹1,239.60 Crore** (up from ₹1,052.58 Crore in FY 2024–25), with a Profit After Tax of **₹126.82 Crore**.
  * **Transactions with BHEL**: In FY 2025–26, BHEL recorded **₹363.93 Crore** in sales to BGGTS, **₹38.08 Crore** in dividend income, and **₹1.98 Crore** in royalty.
* **Emerging Demand**: Gas turbine-based project demand is increasingly driven by power requirements from expanding data centers.

---

### **3. HVDC Systems (High Voltage Direct Current)**
* **Track Record**: BHEL has executed **6 major HVDC projects**, including a multi-terminal UHVDC project.
* **Key Recent Projects & Orders**:
  * **Bhadla-Fatehpur HVDC Link**: Secured an order for **±800 kV, 6,000 MW HVDC terminal stations** for the Bhadla (Rajasthan) to Fatehpur (UP) corridor from Rajasthan Part-I Power Transmission Ltd (Adani Energy Solutions Ltd), including associated 765/400 kV AC substations.
  * **Malegaon HVDC Electrode Station**: Commissioned a standalone HVDC Electrode Station at Malegaon, connected with the Mumbai–Ahmedabad High Speed Rail project.
* **Sector Outlook**: Two HVDC terminal station orders are under implementation. The Central Electricity Authority (CEA) projects ~66 GW of HVDC capacity under the National Electricity Plan (NEP) to facilitate long-distance renewable energy integration.

---

### **4. Coal Gasification**
* **Commercial Entry & Projects**: BHEL made its entry into coal gasification with the Coal-to-2,000 TPD Ammonium Nitrate Project located at Lakhanpur mines, Jharsuguda district, Odisha.
* **Joint Venture Structure**: Formed **Bharat Coal Gasification and Chemicals Limited (BCGCL)** as a Joint Venture on May 21, 2024 with Coal India Limited (CIL holding 51%, BHEL holding 49%).
* **Technology**: Uses BHEL’s in-house developed **Pressurised Fluidised Bed Gasification (PFBG) technology** to process high-ash Indian coal (~2,400 TPD throughput) into syngas and ammonium nitrate.
* **Order Wins**: Secured orders from BCGCL worth **~₹8,288 Crore** (excl. taxes) across two main packages:
  * **LSTK-1 Package**: Coal Gasification & Raw Syngas Cleaning Plant (~₹5,400 Crore).
  * **LSTK-2 Package**: Syngas Purification Plant (~₹2,800 Crore).
* **Financial Commitments**: BHEL Board approved an equity contribution of **₹3,064.46 Crore** towards promoters' equity in BCGCL (with ₹4.87 Crore already invested as of March 31, 2026) and provided a **Corporate Guarantee of ₹662 Crore**.
* **Policy Alignment**: Supported by the Government of India’s **₹37,500 Crore coal and lignite gasification scheme** and aligned with the National Coal Gasification Mission target of 100 Million Tonnes of coal gasification by 2030.

### **6. Order Book Trend Over the Last 2–3 Years**
BHEL’s order book has nearly doubled over the past three years due to a strong thermal power capex revival and momentum in industrial/non-thermal sectors:

* **FY 2023–24 (FY24)**: Order inflow was **₹77,907 Crore**, bringing the closing order book to **₹1,31,598 Crore**.
* **FY 2024–25 (FY25)**: Order inflow surged to **₹92,535 Crore**, pushing the closing order book to **₹1,96,328 Crore**.
* **FY 2025–26 (FY26)**: Order inflow reached **₹75,916 Crore**, taking the total closing order book to **₹2,39,057 Crore**.
* **Book-to-Bill Expansion**: This surge materially improved BHEL's **book-to-bill ratio from 3.9x/5.5x in FY23/FY24 to 6.9x in FY25 and 7.1x in FY26**, offering strong revenue visibility over the medium term.

### **7. EBITDA & PAT Margin Trajectory**
BHEL has experienced a sharp profitability turnaround over the last 3 years:

* **EBITDA Margins**:
  * **FY24**: EBITDA stood at **₹1,201 Crore** (~5.0% of revenue), while adjusted EBITDA margin was negative (**-2.2%**).
  * **FY25**: EBITDA rose to **₹1,745 Crore** (**6.2%** of revenue; **4.4%–4.5%** adjusted).
  * **FY26**: EBITDA increased **83% YoY to ₹3,189 Crore** (**9.4%** of revenue; **6.3%–6.9%** adjusted).
* **PAT (Net Profit) Margins**:
  * **FY24**: PAT was **₹260 Crore** (~1.1% margin).
  * **FY25**: PAT grew to **₹513 Crore** (~1.8% margin).
  * **FY26**: PAT grew **3x YoY to ₹1,578 Crore** (~4.7% margin).
* **Key Margin Drivers**:
  1. **Price Variation Clauses (PVC)**: Entire new order book carries PVC terms protecting gross input margins.
  2. **Fixed-Cost Absorption**: Higher execution scale and shop floor capacity utilization (up 25%) reduced overhead intensity.
  3. **Working Capital Terms**: Improved mobilisation advances and shorter payment milestones reduced interest and capital lock-up.
  4. **Legacy Order Run-off**: Progressive completion of low-margin, fixed-price legacy orders that previously dragged down margins.

### **18. Typical Margins on New Thermal Orders**
* **Explicit Numerical Target**: **Not available in the sources.** Management does not publish an explicit numerical gross or EBITDA percentage margin figure for new thermal orders.
* **Contractual & Structural Margin Safeguards**:
  * **Price Variation Clause (PVC)**: 100% of the new thermal order book carries PVC terms to pass through raw material cost changes and protect gross margins over long gestation periods.
  * **Improved Payment Terms**: New orders incorporate higher mobilization advances, shorter payment milestones, and lower retention money, preventing working capital lock-up.
  * **Replacement of Legacy Contracts**: High-margin new PVC orders are replacing older, fixed-price legacy contracts that previously dragged down profitability.

### **27. Major FY27 Order Opportunities for BHEL**
The sources highlight key ordering pipelines across several core and emerging sectors:

* **Thermal Power**: Near-term awarding pipeline of **~35 GW of thermal capacity expected by FY28E** (out of 80–97 GW targeted by the MoP/CEA by FY32/FY35) across Central PSUs, State utilities, Merchant, and IPP developers via tariff-based competitive bidding. BHEL maintains an **80%+ market share in thermal ordering**.
* **Nuclear Power**: Opportunities arising from India's policy goal of **100 GW nuclear capacity by 2047**, supported by the enactment of the SHANTI Act (enabling private sector participation) and ongoing developments in Bharat Small Modular Reactors (BSMRs) and PHWR/PWR equipment.
* **Power Transmission**: Demand under the National Electricity Plan (NEP) for **~66 GW of HVDC capacity** and 23.45 lakh MVA transformation capacity, encompassing High Voltage Direct Current (HVDC) corridors, 765/400 kV AC substations, and EHV GIS substations.
* **Energy Storage & Hydro**: Government roadmap for **100 GW of Pumped Storage Projects (PSPs) by 2035–36** alongside a pipeline of hydro projects in India and neighbouring countries.
* **Defence & Aerospace**: Modernization opportunities under the Technology Perspective and Capability Roadmap (TPCR) 2025 across naval guns (SRGMs), marine gas turbines, and compact heat exchangers for airborne platforms.
* **Rail Transportation**: Indian Railways' ongoing procurement for High-Horsepower Locomotives, Trainsets, electric propulsion, and expanded rollout of the **Kavach signalling system**.
* **Gas Turbines & Industrial Captive Power**: Gas turbine project demand driven by power requirements from expanding **Data Centers**, alongside captive power plant (BTG) expansions in steel, aluminum, and fertilizers.

# FY27Q2 Result

### **1. What Went Well in Q2 FY27 (Key Highlights)**

* **Strong Revenue Growth**: Standalone Revenue from Operations grew **24% YoY to ₹9,300 Crore** in Q2 FY27 (up from ₹7,512 Crore in Q2 FY26). H1 FY27 revenue reached **₹16,998 Crore**, representing a **31% YoY expansion**.
* **EBITDA Expansion & Operating Leverage**:
  * Q2 FY27 EBITDA surged **69% YoY to ₹1,286 Crore** (vs. ₹763 Crore in Q2 FY26).
  * EBITDA Margin expanded by **+350 bps to 13.4%** (up from 9.9% in Q2 FY26).
  * For H1 FY27, EBITDA jumped **392% YoY to ₹2,020 Crore** with an **11.6% margin** (vs. 3.1% in H1 FY26), demonstrating strong fixed-cost absorption as execution scaled up.
* **Net Profit (PAT) Surge**:
  * Standalone PAT for Q2 FY27 grew **120% YoY to ₹810 Crore** (vs. ₹368 Crore in Q2 FY26).
  * Profit Before Tax (PBT) grew **119% YoY to ₹1,078 Crore**.
  * H1 FY27 PAT recorded a strong turnaround to **₹1,192 Crore** (reversing a loss of -₹87 Crore in H1 FY26).
* **All-Time High Order Visibility**: The outstanding order book reached **₹2,57,571 Crore** as of September 30, 2026 (**+17.27% YoY growth**). Non-thermal orders account for **over ₹66,000 Crore**, spanning Nuclear (~₹12,800 Cr), Transmission (~₹13,600 Cr), Transportation (~₹16,400 Cr), Defense (~₹7,300 Cr), Coal Gasification (~₹9,000 Cr), and Hydro (~₹6,400 Cr).
* **Cash Collections & Receivable Days**:
  * Total customer cash collections grew **33% YoY to ₹13,179 Crore** in Q2 FY27 (H1 FY27 total collections: ₹24,183 Crore, +34% YoY).
  * Trade receivables days dropped significantly to **94 days** (down from 120 days in September 2025).
* **Operational Deliveries**:
  * Commissioned the **660 MW Unit 1 of Udangudi STPP** (TANGEDCO).
  * Synchronised **199 MW** across hydro (Panchet, Chilla) and industrial turbine sets.
  * Achieved full load on **2 × 20 MW Rahughat HEP** (Nepal) and delivered **3 upgraded Super Rapid Gun Mounts (SRGMs)** to Indian Navy shipyards.

  * While receivable days shortened due to higher turnover, absolute trade receivables marginally increased by 1.3% YoY to **₹9,718 Crore** as of September 30, 2026 (vs. ₹9,592 Crore in September 2025).

### **3. What Went Bad / Key Challenges in Q2 FY27**

* **Lumpy Quarterly Order Booking**: The quarter-on-quarter drop in order intake (~₹4,000 Cr in Q2 FY27 vs. ~₹26,700 Cr in Q1 FY27 and ~₹30,011 Cr in Q4 FY26) highlights the lumpiness of thermal and large-scale industrial contract finalizations.
* **Execution Drag from Legacy Contracts**: Tail-end, fixed-price legacy contracts continued to be executed in FY27. While legacy contracts are expected to be fully completed within FY27, they continue to exert a temporary drag on gross operating margins compared to newer orders carrying Price Variation Clauses (PVC).
