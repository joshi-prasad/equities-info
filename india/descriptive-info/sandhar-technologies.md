# Sandhar Technologies
# Business Model
**Date:** 12 July 2026

Here is a first-principles analysis of Sandhar Technologies Limited, structured for a long-only momentum investor focused on earnings upgrades, margin expansion, and return ratios over the next 6–12 months.

### a) What Does Sandhar Actually Do?
Sandhar is not merely a parts manufacturer; it is an integrated Tier-1 automotive systems supplier acting as an outsourced manufacturing and assembly arm for major OEMs.

*   **Core Products (High Margin/Legacy):** Locking Systems (mechanical/smart locks), Vision Systems (mirrors). These are technologically driven, high-margin components where Sandhar holds significant market share and pricing power.
*   **Scaling Products (Volume/Scale-Driven):** Aluminium Die Casting (ADC), Sheet Metal components, Cabins & Fabrication. These are capital-intensive, scale-driven businesses designed to capture higher "content per vehicle."
*   **Why OEMs buy from them:** OEMs are consolidating supplier bases. Instead of buying raw castings from one supplier and machining from another, they buy from Sandhar because it offers end-to-end integration (High-Pressure Die Casting, Low-Pressure Die Casting, Zinc/Magnesium capabilities, and Machining) under one roof.

### b) Business Segments
Management is aggressively verticalizing the business into independent entities to drive scale.

*   **Aluminium Die Casting (ADC) (31% of FY26 Revenue):**
    *   *Focus:* Engine blocks, structural parts. Accelerated by the acquisition of Sundaram-Clayton’s (SCL) die-casting unit and the new Khed City plant.
    *   *Outlook:* Currently a margin drag due to initial integration costs, but expected to turn EBT-positive by Q2/Q3 FY27. Strategically the largest growth engine.
*   **Locking Systems (19% of FY26 Revenue):**
    *   *Focus:* Traditional mechanical locks transitioning to Smart Locks.
    *   *Outlook:* High-margin (12–14% EBITDA). Two OEMs have initiated pilot lots; commercial scale-up over the next 2-4 quarters will act as a major premiumisation lever.
*   **Sheet Metal (17.1% of FY26 Revenue):**
    *   *Focus:* Structural components. Housed under *Sandhar Engineering*. Scaling rapidly; management aims to grow this from <₹200cr a few years ago to >₹1,000cr.
*   **Cabins & Fabrication (11.1% of FY26 Revenue):**
    *   *Focus:* Supplied to Off-Highway Vehicles (OHV) and construction equipment. Volume is directly tied to infrastructure cycles.
*   **EV Electronics (Negligible Revenue):**
    *   *Focus:* Motor controllers, battery chargers. Currently doing ~₹15cr annually. Management expects turnaround only by FY28. *Not a near-term earnings driver.*

### c) Where Does Sandhar Sit In The Automotive Value Chain?
**Raw Material Suppliers (Steel, Al, Zn) → Sandhar (Casting/Stamping/Machining/Assembly) → OEMs (Hero, TVS) → Dealers → Vehicle Owners**

*   **Value Addition:** Sandhar takes commoditized raw metals and applies capital-intensive processes (die-casting, pressing) and IP-driven assembly (electronic smart locks) to deliver ready-to-fit modules.
*   **Why OEMs Outsource:** OEMs allocate capital to branding, R&D, and EV platform development. They outsource capital-heavy, lower-ROCE component manufacturing to scale-players like Sandhar who can aggregate volumes across multiple OEMs to achieve higher asset turns.

### d) Who Are The Customers?
*   **Two-Wheelers (2W) - 66.8% of Revenue:** The dominant segment. Industry grew 12.9% in FY26, but Sandhar’s 2W business grew 35.1% due to wallet share gains.
*   **Passenger Vehicles (PV) - 13.0% of Revenue:** Currently a smaller segment, but the Khed City ADC plant is specifically targeting PV growth.
*   **Off-Highway Vehicles (OHV) - 12.6% of Revenue:** Tied to tractor and construction equipment cycles.
*   **Customer Concentration Risk:** Extremely high. According to the ICRA rating, the top two customers (**Hero MotoCorp and TVS**) account for **>50% of total revenues**. Honda Motorcycle & Scooter India (HMSI) is also scaling rapidly as a customer.

### e) How Does Business Flow From OEM To Revenue?
1.  **Platform Development & Tech Tie-ups:** Sandhar sources tech (now shifting to Royalty-based Tech Transfers rather than JVs to save capital).
2.  **Prototype/Pilot:** E.g., Smart locks currently being supplied in pilot lots to two OEMs.
3.  **Volume Ramp-up:** Tied to the OEM's vehicle launch and market success.
4.  **Sticky Business:** Once a component (like a lock or engine block) is integrated into a vehicle platform, the supplier is rarely replaced for the life of that platform (usually 3–7 years) due to high switching costs and re-tooling friction.

### f) Manufacturing Process
*   **Commoditized vs. High Value:** Pressing sheet metal is lower on the value chain. However, integrated High-Pressure Die Casting (HPDC) + Low-Pressure Die Casting (LPDC) + **Machining** (added via the TVS/SCL deal) creates high switching costs.
*   **Footprint:** Expanded heavily to 48 plants (up from 42 in FY24).
*   **Technology Sourcing:** Management explicitly stated in Q4 FY26 they are abandoning the Joint Venture (JV) model in favor of Technology Transfers (ToT) on a royalty basis to maximize ROCE on existing infrastructure.

### g) How Does Sandhar Actually Make Money?
*   **Market Share & Wallet Share Gains (Structural):** Capturing more "content per vehicle" (e.g., selling locks *plus* sheet metal *plus* alloy castings to Hero/TVS).
*   **Premiumisation (Structural):** Transitioning from mechanical locks to high-margin Smart Locks.
*   **Inorganic Growth (Structural):** SCL acquisition adds ~₹400cr in annual revenue capability.
*   **Industry Tailwind (Cyclical):** Benefiting from the post-GST 2.0 reform (18% tax bracket) and strong domestic 2W momentum.

### h) Economics Of The Business
*   **Pricing Power:** Limited baseline pricing power, but they possess a **lagged raw material pass-through mechanism**. Domestic pass-through takes 1-2 quarters; overseas takes up to 180 days.
*   **Earnings Catalyst:** Management expects a "price re-trigger" in FY27 to pass on accumulated inflation (power, manpower, gas), which will structurally bump up absolute revenue and margins.
*   **Margin Blending:** The proprietary business (Locks/Mirrors) generates 12-14% EBITDA. Heavy manufacturing (ADC/Sheet Metal) generates lower margins but drives absolute cash flow and scale. Total company EBITDA stabilized around 10.6% in FY26.

### i) Cost Structure
*   **Variable Costs (~63%):** Raw materials (Aluminum, Zinc, Steel). Highly susceptible to global commodity cycles, managed via lagged OEM pass-throughs.
*   **Fixed Costs:** Employee costs (~13%), Depreciation (₹193cr in FY26, up from ₹171cr), Interest (₹69cr). Power costs are a significant fixed/semi-variable headwind, prompting the recent acquisition of a solar entity (Clean Renewable Energy HR 1B) to lock in cheaper power.

### j) Operating Leverage
Sandhar is currently at an operating leverage inflection point.
*   **The Setup:** They have front-loaded massive capex (₹316cr in FY25, ₹298cr in FY26) and acquired SCL. Depreciation and interest are already hitting the P&L.
*   **The Execution:** As capacity utilization rises across new plants (Khed City, Sanaswadi, SCL Hosur), incremental revenues will barely increase fixed costs. This was evident in FY26: **Revenue grew 25%, but PAT grew 40%.**

### k) Working Capital Cycle
*   **Cycle Dynamics:** Receivables are tightly managed at 40-45 days.
*   **Debt Profile:** Gross debt is ₹948cr, but ~₹564cr of this is working capital debt directly linked to revenue scaling.
*   **Momentum Read:** If RM prices stabilize and OEM pass-throughs take effect, working capital intensity should slightly ease, driving better Free Cash Flow (FCF).

### l) Capital Allocation
*   **SCL Acquisition:** Bought for ₹163cr; generates ~₹400cr revenue. *Test of Management:* It is currently dragging margins down. Shifting operations to their own premises by Q2 FY27 is expected to push it into EBT-positive territory by Q3 FY27.
*   **QIP (₹500cr planned):** Earmarked for further inorganic acquisitions. Target criteria: 18% pre-tax ROCE within 2-3 years.
*   **Renewable Energy:** Acquiring solar power companies to control power costs (a smart margin-preservation move).
*   **Returns Focus:** Management explicitly stated a goal to push post-tax ROCE from current ~10-15% levels toward 18-20% as volumes scale to ₹10,000cr.

### m) Competitive Position
*   **The Moat:** Size and integrated capability. They are one of the few players handling Aluminum (HPDC/LPDC), Zinc, Magnesium, and Machining simultaneously. OEMs prefer this "one-stop-shop" over coordinating with 5 smaller vendors.
*   **Vulnerabilities:** Sheet metal is relatively commoditized and relies entirely on scale and proximity to the OEM.

### n) Key Business Risks
*   **Customer Concentration (Structural):** If Hero or TVS loses market share to Bajaj or EV startups (Ola, Ather), Sandhar’s volumes will crater.
*   **2W Dependence (Structural):** With 66.8% exposure, Sandhar is essentially a levered play on the Indian two-wheeler consumer.
*   **Execution Risk (Financial):** SCL turnaround is delayed. It was initially expected to turn around faster but is now pushed to Q3 FY27. Overseas business (Europe) remains volatile and barely broke even in Q4 FY26.
*   **Dilution Risk:** The ₹500cr QIP will dilute equity. If the acquired assets fail to meet the 18% ROCE hurdle, ROE will compress.

### o) Evolution Across FY25 Q4 → FY26 Q4 (Narrative Tracking)
*   **What changed for the better:** Revenue growth accelerated massively (from guiding 14-15% in Q4 FY25 to delivering 25% growth in FY26). The narrative shifted from "waiting for recovery" to "aggressively doubling revenue every 3-4 years."
*   **What changed for the worse (Delays):** The timeline for turning around loss-making units keeps extending. SCL turnaround was pushed to Q3 FY27. EV business profitability was pushed to FY28.
*   **Strategy Shift:** Quietly pivoted away from Capital-heavy Joint Ventures towards Royalty-based Technology Transfers (ToT). This is highly positive for future ROCE.
*   **Structural Reorganization:** Shifted from a hybrid parent company to verticalized subsidiaries (Sandhar Ascast for Die Casting, Sandhar Engineering for Sheet Metal) to empower dedicated management teams.

### p) What Will Matter Most Over The Next 2–4 Quarters? (The Momentum Checklist)
To determine if Sandhar will continue delivering earnings upgrades, monitor these specific variables:

1.  **SCL / Khed City Turnaround (Q2/Q3 FY27):** This is the biggest near-term margin lever. Management guided for EBT break-even in Q2 (Khed City/Sanaswadi) and Q3 (SCL). If they miss this, earnings will face downgrades.
2.  **Smart Lock Commercialization:** Watch for updates on the 2 pilot OEMs moving to full commercial production. Because smart locks carry 12-14%+ margins, this will drive EBITDA margin expansion beyond the current 10.6% baseline.
3.  **Price Re-trigger Execution:** Management hinted at passing accumulated inflation to OEMs. A successful price reset in Q1/Q2 FY27 will drop straight to the bottom line.
4.  **Deployment of ₹500cr QIP:** Dilution without immediate earnings accretion kills momentum. The market will react strongly to *what* they buy with this cash and whether the target actually fits the "18% pre-tax ROCE" mandate.
5.  **Overseas Stability:** The European business printed a minor profit in Q4 FY26. Sustaining this breakeven stops the bleeding and mathematically upgrades consolidated PAT.

### 1. How is the company planning to increase wallet share?
Sandhar’s strategy to deepen wallet share relies heavily on moving from a component supplier to an integrated module supplier, alongside cross-selling multiple product categories to single OEMs.

*   **Moving Upstream to Complete Modules:** Rather than supplying raw castings, Sandhar has vertically integrated its capabilities to offer High-Pressure Die Casting (HPDC), Low-Pressure Die Casting (LPDC), and Machining under one roof. OEMs prefer this "zero to 100" capability as it reduces their vendor base and supply chain friction.
*   **Deepening Wallet Share via Premiumisation:** In the Locking Systems segment, Sandhar is migrating customers from low-margin mechanical locks to high-margin Smart Locks, structurally increasing the "content per vehicle" value. Current volumes are around 5,000 locksets per month but are scaling rapidly.
*   **Cross-Selling Across Verticals:** A prime example is Honda Motorcycle & Scooter India (HMSI). Historically not a major customer, Sandhar has recently broken in and is now cross-selling sheet metal, castings, locks, and mirrors simultaneously to them. The structural reorganization of the company into 4 independent, focused entities (Ascast for Castings, Engineering for Sheet Metal, etc.) is specifically designed to let dedicated teams capture larger market share within their respective product verticals.

### 2. Capital Allocation: Capex, QIP, SCL, and Mexico
Management has stated a clear framework of doubling revenue every 3–4 years while capping capital intensity.

*   **Capex Philosophy:** Management targets maintenance and growth capex at a strict 5% to 7% of revenues. For FY27, this equates to roughly ₹275 crore to ₹310 crore. The heaviest capex cycle (establishing Khed City and Sanaswadi plants) is largely complete, meaning incremental revenue will require less capital intensity going forward.
*   **QIP (₹500 crore):** The Board approved raising up to ₹500 crore, valid for one year, strictly earmarked as a "war chest" for inorganic acquisitions. *Crucial test for investors:* Management promises they will only pull the trigger if target companies can demonstrate an 18% pre-tax ROCE within 2-3 years.
*   **Mexico Expansion:** In Q4 FY26, the Board approved setting up a manufacturing and assembly facility in Mexico (via branch or wholly-owned subsidiary) to secure proximity to key global OEMs and achieve supply chain efficiencies.
*   **Renewable Energy Investment:** Sandhar recently acquired "Clean Renewable Energy HR 1B Pvt Ltd". This is a strategic margin-preservation move to secure captive solar power and offset severe power cost inflation.

### 3. Technology Partnerships: The Shift from JVs to ToT
Sandhar is executing a major strategic pivot in how it acquires technology, shifting away from Capital-Intensive Joint Ventures toward Asset-Light Technology Transfers (ToT).

*   **Why Abandon JVs?** Establishing JVs previously required setting up entirely new legal entities and greenfield infrastructure. By shifting to royalty-based ToT collaborations, Sandhar can manufacture new technologies using its *existing* plant infrastructure.
*   **Impact on ROCE:** Management explicitly stated that this pivot avoids new capital costs, allowing return on capital employed (ROCE) and margins to compound "much faster than they typically do in the preparation of joint ventures".
*   **Current JV Portfolio:** They have exited underperforming JVs (Jinyoung Sandhar and Kwangsung Sandhar). The remaining 5 JVs are now all PAT positive, generating ~₹68.5 crore in revenue (Sandhar's 50% share) with ~10.7% EBITDA margins. These include Sandhar Han Sung, Sandhar Amkin, Sandhar Whetron, Winnercom Sandhar, and Sandhar Han Shin.
*   **Upcoming ToT Pipeline:** The company is currently testing Proof of Concepts (POCs) with customers for new *Telematics* products, expected to enter the production cycle next year.

### 4. EV Electronics Segment
This is a seed-stage business run under *Sandhar Auto Electric Solutions Pvt Ltd*. *Investor note:* This is a narrative driver, not a near-term earnings driver.

*   **Products:** They manufacture Motor Control Units (MCUs), On-Board Battery Chargers (OBCs), DC-DC converters, and integrated 3-in-1 units (MCU + OBC + Power Distribution Unit).
*   **Customers:** Visual disclosures highlight tie-ups with new-age 2W EV players like Yulu, River, Simple Energy, and Brisk EV. In FY26, they sold 41,000 battery chargers and 5,500 MCUs.
*   **Future Plans:** It generated negligible revenue previously but is ramping up (closing FY26 at roughly ₹20 crore). Management targets scaling this to ₹100 crore over three years. The segment is currently loss-making due to R&D and market-capture costs, and is only expected to reach profitability turnaround by FY28.

### 5. Deep Dive into the Sundaram-Clayton (SCL) Acquisition
*   **The Economics:** Acquired for ₹163 crore, the SCL die-casting unit carries a revenue potential of ₹400-₹425 crore, achieving a highly attractive 2.5x asset turnover ratio.
*   **Why it was Acquired:** SCL allowed Sandhar to break into the Low-Pressure Die Casting (LPDC) market overnight, acquire high-tonnage machines, and secure TVS Motors as a captive anchor customer.
*   **How it was Funded:** Internally funded via cash flow (₹113 crore paid pre-March 2025, ₹50 crore paid in April 2025).
*   **Current State & Momentum Read:** *This is the most critical near-term margin lever.* Currently, the unit is operating out of SCL's shared premises, which is causing disruptions and dragging consolidated margins down to an EBT loss. Sandhar is relocating the machinery to its own premises by Q2 FY27.
*   **Profitability Target:** Upon completing the move, management expects an immediate EBT turnaround by Q3 FY27, with EBITDA margins stabilizing at 7–7.5% in the first year, eventually scaling to the company's baseline of 9–9.5%. If executed, this will mathematically trigger significant earnings upgrades.

### 6. The FY27 "Price Re-trigger"
*   **The Catalyst:** Over the last few years, Sandhar absorbed severe inflationary shocks—most notably, power and gas costs scaling by as much as 800%, alongside wage inflation.
*   **The Mechanism:** OEM pricing agreements are typically locked in based on historical cost structures. Sandhar is currently renegotiating these legacy contracts to pass on this accumulated inflation.
*   **Financial Impact:** If successful, this "price re-trigger" will result in a structural, step-function increase in base pricing. This drives up absolute revenue and drops directly to the EBITDA line without the company needing to manufacture a single additional component.

# Value Chain

### Part 1 — The Complete Automotive Value Chain

Sandhar is an outsourced manufacturing and assembly arm for automotive OEMs. The economic value chain flows as follows:

**Raw Material Suppliers (Steel, Aluminium, Zinc, Magnesium)**
*   *Who performs it:* Commodity players (e.g., Hindalco, Tata Steel).
*   *Bargaining Power:* High. Prices are governed by global LME/domestic indices.

**↓**

**Metal Processing & Component Manufacturing (Sandhar)**
*   *Who performs it:* Sandhar (via entities like Sandhar Ascast, Sandhar Engineering).
*   *Why it exists:* OEMs prefer to allocate their capital to R&D, EV platform development, and marketing rather than capital-intensive foundries and presses.
*   *Where economic value is created:* By transforming raw metal ingots into high-precision, ready-to-fit modules (e.g., machining an aluminium die-cast engine block).
*   *Bargaining Power:* Moderate. Sandhar lacks absolute pricing power but protects margins through lagged (usually 1-to-3-month) raw material pass-through agreements.

**↓**

**OEM Vehicle Assembly (Hero MotoCorp, TVS, HMSI)**
*   *Who performs it:* The OEM.
*   *Why it exists:* Final brand creation and vehicle integration.
*   *Where economic value is created:* Brand premium and distribution.
*   *Bargaining Power:* Very High. Top 2 customers (Hero & TVS) command >50% of Sandhar's revenues.

**↓**

**Dealers & End Customers**
*   *Who performs it:* Dealership networks selling to retail/commercial buyers.

### Part 2 — Sandhar's Internal Value Chain

How Sandhar internally executes from concept to cash:

1.  **Technology Acquisition:** Sourcing core IP. Management is shifting away from capital-heavy Joint Ventures towards asset-light, royalty-based Technology Transfers (ToT).
2.  **Product Design & Engineering:** Customizing the licensed tech (e.g., Smart Locks, EV chargers) for specific Indian OEM platforms.
3.  **Prototyping & OEM Approval:** Submitting pilot lots. For example, Smart Locks were piloted with 2 OEMs before moving to commercial scale (currently ~5,000 sets/month).
4.  **Tooling & Industrialisation:** Setting up the dies and moulds. This involves upfront capital expenditure.
5.  **Manufacturing:** Capital-intensive processes like High-Pressure Die Casting (HPDC), Low-Pressure Die Casting (LPDC), and Sheet Metal pressing.
6.  **Machining & Integration:** The critical value-add. Moving from selling "raw castings" to fully "machined castings" using facilities acquired from TVS/SCL.
7.  **Just-In-Time Delivery:** Supplying finished components from Sandhar's 48 plants located in close proximity to OEM manufacturing hubs.

*OEMs care about this internal chain because Sandhar acts as a single-window "zero to 100" supplier, consolidating their vendor base across multiple metals (Al, Zn, Mg) and processes.*

### Part 7 — The Economic Moat

Sandhar's moat is **not** its technology (which it licenses). Its moat consists of:

1.  **High Switching Costs (Structural):** Once an OEM approves a die-cast engine block or a smart lock for a vehicle platform, they will almost never switch suppliers mid-lifecycle. The cost, time, and friction of validating a new supplier and paying for new tooling destroys the ROI for the OEM.
2.  **Integrated Scale (Structural):** TVS sold its Sundaram-Clayton (SCL) die-casting unit to Sandhar because TVS wanted to focus on core OEM activities. Sandhar can aggregate volumes across TVS, Hero, and HMSI to drive higher utilization on heavy machinery that a single OEM cannot achieve internally.
3.  **Proximity & Vendor Parks (Structural):** Operating 48 plants next to OEM hubs makes logistics highly efficient, barring competitors without capital from entering.

# Unit Economics

### 1. What Is Sandhar's Economic Engine?

**The Core Economic Engine:**
Sandhar acts as an outsourced, capital-heavy manufacturing extension for major OEMs (Hero, TVS, HMSI).
*   **Revenue Creation:** By capturing larger "content per vehicle" (wallet share) across existing OEM platforms through cross-selling commoditized structures (sheet metal) alongside proprietary parts (locks/mirrors).
*   **Margin Creation:** By driving extreme capacity utilization on heavy fixed assets (die-casting machines) and transitioning customers from mechanical locks to higher-margin Smart Locks (premiumisation).
*   **Capital Consumption:** Historically consumed heavily by setting up Greenfield foundries and Joint Ventures (JVs).
*   **Cash Creation:** By aggregating volumes across multiple OEMs to achieve 2.5x–3.0x asset turnover ratios, converting operating leverage into free cash flow.
*   **ROCE Improvement:** By shifting away from capital-intensive JVs toward asset-light, royalty-based Technology Transfers (ToT), utilizing existing plant infrastructure to scale revenue without proportional capex.
*   **Shareholder Value:** Driven by the mathematical expansion of PAT as newly commissioned plants (Khed City, Sanaswadi, SCL Hosur) cross their break-even utilization thresholds, allowing incremental gross margins to flow directly to the bottom line.

**Direction of the Engine:**
The economic engine is structurally **strengthening**, but currently masked by temporary integration friction. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) improved from 11.4% in FY25 to 12.9% in FY26 (Consolidated). However, near-term margins are suppressed by the delayed turnaround of the Sundaram-Clayton (SCL) acquisition and overseas operations.

### 2. What Is Sandhar's Flywheel? (Most Important)

**The Primary Economic Flywheel:**
1. **Asset-Light Tech Acquisition (ToT)** -> 2. **Cross-Selling to Captive OEMs** -> 3. **Higher Wallet Share (Content/Vehicle)** -> 4. **Aggregated Volume Scale** -> 5. **Higher Capacity Utilization** -> 6. **Operating Leverage Kicks In** -> 7. **EBITDA Margin Expansion** -> 8. **Higher ROCE & OCF** -> 9. **Reinvestment in High-Asset-Turn M&A**.

*   **Strongest Links:** *Cross-Selling* and *Aggregated Volume Scale*. Sandhar's ability to cross-sell to HMSI (adding sheet metal, castings, locks, and mirrors simultaneously) proves the "one-stop-shop" model works.
*   **Unproven Links:** *Reinvestment in M&A*. Management intends to raise a ₹500 crore QIP for inorganic growth, targeting 18% pre-tax ROCE. Their track record with SCL (currently suppressing margins at ~4.5% EBITDA in H1 FY26) leaves this link unproven.
*   **Strengthening Links:** *Asset-Light Tech Acquisition*. Management's explicit abandonment of JVs in favor of ToTs structurally increases the ROCE potential of the entire flywheel.

### 3. Incremental Economics (Critical)

If Sandhar deploys ₹100 of incremental capital today, the economics drastically differ based on the allocation bucket:

*   **Bucket A: Technology Transfer (ToT) / Smart Locks (Strategic/Growth)**
    *   *Incremental Revenue:* High (premium pricing).
    *   *Incremental Margins:* 12%–14% EBITDA.
    *   *Incremental ROCE:* >25% (Asset-light; utilizes existing infrastructure).
    *   *Status:* Producing returns now (Commercialized with 2 OEMs).
*   **Bucket B: Aluminium Die Casting - SCL / Khed City (Acquisition/Growth)**
    *   *Incremental Revenue:* ~₹400 crore potential on ₹163 crore investment (2.5x Asset Turn).
    *   *Incremental Margins:* Currently depressing returns (~4.5% EBITDA). Target is 9.5%.
    *   *Incremental ROCE:* Currently negative/low. Management targets 18% pre-tax within 2-3 years.
    *   *Status:* Investment story. Dragging current economics. Expected turnaround Q3 FY27.
*   **Bucket C: Renewable Energy / Solar (Strategic/Margin Preservation)**
    *   *Incremental Revenue:* Nil.
    *   *Incremental Margins:* Drops directly to EBITDA by removing volatile utility costs.
    *   *Status:* Cost mitigation tool.

### 5. Capital Allocation Philosophy

*   **Evolution:** Evolving from highly capital-intensive (Greenfield plants + JVs) to **Sweating Assets** (ToTs + relocating SCL machinery to own premises).
*   **Discipline:** Management has strictly capped maintenance/growth capex at 5% to 7% of revenues going forward.
*   **Aggressiveness:** Raising a ₹500 crore QIP without a named target. This is highly promotional/aggressive and risks ROE dilution if the 18% ROCE target is missed.
*   **Smart Allocation:** Acquiring "Clean Renewable Energy HR 1B" to create captive solar power. This is an excellent, defensive allocation of capital against utility inflation.
*   **Evidence vs. Claims:** Management claims SCL will hit 9.5% margins, but evidence shows it generated only 4.48% EBITDA in H1 FY26. Market expectations require this claim to become evidence by Q3 FY27.

---

### 6. Return On Incremental Capital (Most Important)

*   **Generating Attractive Returns:** The legacy Locking and Vision systems, and the mature Sheet Metal operations.
*   **Depressing Returns:** Sundaram-Clayton (SCL) acquisition, Khed City plant, Sanaswadi plant, and Overseas (Romania) operations. These caused an ₹11.2 crore drag on profitability in Q2 FY26.
*   **Expected to Improve ROCE (Next 2-4 Quarters):** Shifting SCL out of shared premises to Sandhar's own Hosur facility (expected completion Q2 FY27) will immediately cut rental/friction costs and push the unit to EBT break-even by Q3 FY27.
*   **May Never Earn Adequate Returns:** EV Electronics. Highly competitive, R&D intensive, and competing against well-funded specialists. Break-even pushed to FY28.

---

### 7. Operating Leverage

*   **Fixed Costs:** Depreciation (₹193cr in FY26), Interest (₹69cr), Core Employee base (13%).
*   **Variable Costs:** Raw Materials (63% of sales - Aluminium, Zinc, Steel).
*   **If Revenue Increases 10%:** Because RM is fully variable (and passed through), the 37% Gross Margin falls heavily to the bottom line. EBITDA increases non-linearly. *Evidence:* In FY26, Revenue grew 25%, but PAT grew 40%.
*   **If Revenue Falls 10%:** ADC and Sheet Metal suffer disproportionately because the massive depreciation schedules of heavy presses/foundries cannot be adjusted downward. Locking systems would remain relatively resilient due to lower capital intensity.

---

### 8. Cash Conversion Engine

**Revenue → EBITDA → PAT → Operating Cash Flow (OCF)**
*   **The Leak (Working Capital):** Cash conversion deteriorated in FY26 due to working capital expansion. As per the FY26 cash flow statement, receivables increased by ₹199 crore and inventories increased by ₹266 crore.
*   **Why it expanded:** OEM platform launches require initial inventory build-ups. The SCL acquisition and 3 new plants commissioning simultaneously trapped cash in work-in-progress and raw material stockpiles.
*   **Trajectory:** Management claims working capital debt (₹564cr) is purely tied to revenue scaling. If growth normalizes and RM prices stabilize, working capital should release, structurally improving Free Cash Flow (FCF) in FY27.

### 10. Economic Risks

*   **SCL Turnaround Failure (Execution / Controllable):** If shifting the SCL machinery to Hosur in Q2 FY27 does not result in the promised 9.5% EBITDA margins, consolidated ROCE will permanently stagnate.
*   **QIP Capital Destruction (Structural / Controllable):** Diluting equity by ₹500 crore to buy an asset that fails to exceed the cost of capital will permanently destroy ROE.
*   **Commodity Pass-Through Lags (Cyclical / External):** The 1-to-3 month lag (and up to 180 days overseas) causes massive margin volatility during commodity supercycles.
*   **Customer Concentration (Structural / External):** >50% of revenue is tied to Hero and TVS. If they lose market share to EV startups, Sandhar's asset turns will crater.

4.  **Pricing Strategy:** A new narrative emerged in FY26 Q4 regarding a "price re-trigger"—renegotiating baseline pricing with OEMs to absorb accumulated utility/wage inflation.

2. **Inflection Point:** Heavy capex (₹600cr+ over 2 years) is largely done. The business is primed for massive operating leverage if SCL, Khed City, and Sanaswadi scale to utilization targets.
3. **Capital Strategy Pivot:** Moving from JVs to ToT is the single most important strategic decision for long-term ROCE improvement.
4. **Margin Anchor:** SCL is currently dragging consolidated margins down. Moving it to Hosur in Q2 FY27 is the mechanical key to unlocking higher EPS.
5. **Vulnerability:** The 1-to-3 month OEM pass-through lag leaves the economic engine highly exposed to short-term commodity shocks.
