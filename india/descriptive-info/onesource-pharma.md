# Business Model

## The Commercial Engine & R&D-to-Commercial Transition

### Facts

#### 1. The Commercial Model & CDMO Platform Play
*   **Business Definition & Integrated CDMO Service:** OneSource is a specialized, multi-modality CDMO platform focusing on complex, scarce, and high-barrier formulations: Drug-Device Combinations (DDCs), Biologics, Soft Gelatin Capsules (SGC), and Sterile Fill-Finish. Rather than acting as a traditional toll-manufacturer, OneSource provides a fully integrated service value chain: process development, analytical scale-up, drug substance manufacturing, aseptic fill-finish, high-precision automated device assembly (autoinjectors, pens), cartoning, serialization, and ex-works shipping.
*   **Commercial Friction Solved:** Complex peptide therapeutics (e.g., GLP-1/Semaglutide) face acute global supply bottlenecks spanning API, cartridges, custom-molded pen devices, and sterile fill-finish lines. Innovators and generic developers outsource to OneSource because building high-speed aseptic filling lines has long lead times (~2 years to secure and qualify a high-speed line).
*   **Aseptic Assembly Differentiation:** OneSource's primary competitive advantage in the DDC space is its end-to-end device assembly. The company ships fully finished, retail-ready packs directly from its factory gate. Management has explicitly noted that no global or domestic competitor currently offers this level of end-to-end device assembly across 11 distinct device platforms.
*   **Regulatory & Quality Audits:** Operating in highly regulated spaces requires immense quality credibility. OneSource has completed over 210 successful customer and regulatory audits. In FY26 alone, it cleared 49 inspections, including successful surprise USFDA inspections at two of its facilities with subsequent Establishment Inspection Reports (EIRs) issued. It also holds active EU GMP certifications, ANVISA approvals for Brazil, and Health Canada approvals.

#### 2. The Customer Lifecycle: MSA-to-CSA Economic Inflection
*   **RFP $\rightarrow$ MSA $\rightarrow$ CSA Progression:** Customers initially engage with OneSource on a project-by-project basis via Requests for Proposals (RFPs). Progression to a Master Services Agreement (MSA) involves tech transfer, process validation, and regulatory filings. Upon regulatory approval, the relationship transitions to a Commercial Supply Agreement (CSA), which dictates long-term commercial manufacturing and is backed by rigid **take-or-pay structures** and capacity reservation fees.
*   **Operating Campaign Efficiency:** During the R&D/MSA phase, line changeovers are frequent, limiting throughput to just 2 batches in 7 days. Conversely, once a molecule achieves commercial status under a CSA, campaigns scale up to 6 batches in 7 days.
*   **Batch-Size Scaling:** OneSource's legacy Line 1 capacity is constrained to a 200-liter batch size. The newer lines under qualification are engineered to handle 500-liter, 750-liter, and 1,000-liter batches, boosting single-campaign output by up to 2.5x within identical operating time blocks.
*   **Revenue Recognition Policy:** OneSource does not recognize milestone or upfront reservation payments as commercial revenue. Revenue is recognized strictly upon the physical shipment of product from the factory gate (ex-works).

#### 3. Customer Categories, De-Risking, and Concentration
*   **Diverse Customer Profile:** The company's customer base exceeds 75 logos, containing a mix of top-tier global generic majors, national champions, regional leaders, biosimilar players (such as Formycon), and veterinary innovators. Over 50 customers partner with OneSource on two or more distinct programs.
*   **Semaglutide Footprint:**
    *   **Canada:** Health Canada has issued generic Semaglutide approvals to three filers—all of whom are OneSource customers (including Dr. Reddy's and Orbicular's Canadian partner). OneSource holds a 100% share of approved generic Semaglutide filers in this G7 market.
    *   **India:** Commencing launch day-one, OneSource's partners capture over 40% of the generic pen volume and command two-thirds (~66%) of the Indian generic Semaglutide market by value of customer brands.
    *   **MENA:** Hikma (the largest regional player) holds the first generic approval, supplied by OneSource.
*   **Dr. Reddy's Supply Disruption:** DRL announced a temporary shutdown of its API/supply facility in Q1FY27. OneSource mitigated the loss of DRL orders by instantly diverting the unutilized capacity to other active partners, maintaining full capacity utilization.

### Interpretations

#### 1. The Real Moat: High Switching Costs and Aseptic Inertia
The specialty CDMO model is governed by extreme switching costs. Once an innovator or generic player selects OneSource, performs a complex tech transfer (which takes 12–18 months for sterile biologics), and submits a drug-device dossier to regulators (like Health Canada or the USFDA), **the customer is effectively locked in**. Re-filing with a different CDMO requires starting the process over, incurring multi-million-dollar development costs, risking a 2-year regulatory delay, and sacrificing first-to-file (FTF) status. This creates high stickiness, securing highly predictable, recurring cash flows post-commercialization.

#### 2. Operating Leverage: The Dual-Edged Sword of Aseptic Manufacturing
The transition from MSA to CSA is not just a growth driver; it is a fundamental shift in unit economics:

$$\text{Campaign Throughput} \uparrow \ \Rightarrow \ \text{Aseptic Operating Days Utilized} \uparrow \ \Rightarrow \ \text{EBITDA Margin} \uparrow$$

The fixed-cost base of an sterile filling line (depreciation, cleanroom HVAC utilities, specialized regulatory/QA staff) is highly rigid.
*   **The Downside (Q3FY26):** When G7 approvals were delayed (e.g., Dr. Reddy's receiving regulatory queries from Health Canada), OneSource experienced a painful mismatch. The company had already upfronted employee opex (hiring 380+ specialists to support the ramp-up) and cleanroom prep costs. Delayed shipments meant deferred revenues, causing severe negative operating leverage and suppressed margins.
*   **The Upside (Q1FY27):** Once approvals came through, sequential revenues surged 37% YoY to INR 4,490 million. Operating leverage turned positive as fixed line qualification and employee costs were spread over commercial volumes, expanding EBITDA 39% YoY to INR 1,233 million.

#### 3. De-Risking is Proven, Not Aspirational
A critical risk to any CDMO is "anchor customer concentration." The Q1FY27 Dr. Reddy's API disruption was a live stress-test of OneSource's commercial engine. If OneSource were a simple toll-manufacturer for DRL, its DDC facility would have faced an expensive shutdown. Instead, because of its **fungible aseptic lines** and a deep book of over 20 DDC customers, OneSource successfully diverted the open sterile days to fill-finish campaigns for other global players (such as Apotex/Orbicular). This proves that demand for aseptic pen assembly is structurally larger than any single generic player's market share, shielding OneSource from customer-specific execution risks.

For institutional investors, the decision to defer the Steriscience/Brooks consolidation is a major positive sign of management discipline. #### 2. Operational Scaling: The 2.5x Output Multiplier

$$\text{Bioreactor Batch Size (200L to 500L)} \uparrow \ \Rightarrow \ \text{Output per Sterile Day} \uparrow \times 2.5 \ \Rightarrow \ \text{Unit Conversion Opex} \downarrow$$

Because Line 1 is capped at 200L, its throughput is structurally restricted. Line 2's ability to run 500L to 1,000L campaigns means OneSource can produce up to **2.5x to 5x more units within the exact same 225-day operating block**.

Crucially, because the staff and overhead opex for Line 2 was already upfronted in FY26 (evidenced by the 380+ hires and margin compression in H2FY26), the incremental margins on Line 2 will be extremely high.


## Margin Dynamics, Capital Allocation & Cash Flow Quality

Because of its highly rigid and upfronted fixed-cost base, EBITDA plummeted 88% YoY to **₹173 million**, compressing EBITDA margins to **6%** (down 3,018 bps YoY) and yielding an Adjusted PAT loss of **(₹472) million**.

EBITDA surged 39% YoY to **₹1,233 million**, reflecting an EBITDA margin of **27.5%**. This structural recovery was driven entirely by favorable product mix, specifically a higher contribution from high-margin drug-device combination (DDC) commercial revenues.
*   **Base Business Seasonality:** Management notes that the base soft gelatin and sterile injectables businesses are traditionally **H2-heavy**, with soft gelatin volumes ramping up significantly during the cold and flu seasons.

### Interpretation

#### 1. Aseptic Operating Leverage: The Math Behind the 104% EBITDA Drop
The transition year (FY26) exposed the sheer mathematical severity of operating leverage in high-barrier sterile CDMO assets. High-speed aseptic lines and specialized workforce requirements create a highly rigid fixed-cost block:

$$\text{Fixed Cleanroom Utilities (HVAC, QA)} + \text{Upfronted Workforce (380+ FTEs)} = \text{Rigid Opex Floor}$$

We can map the extreme leverage by examining the drop from Q2FY26 to Q3FY26:
*   **Revenue Decline:** From ₹3,758 million to ₹2,903 million (a drop of **₹855 million** or **22.7%**).
*   **EBITDA Decline:** From ₹1,065 million to ₹173 million (a drop of **₹892 million** or **83.8%**).

Because opex was entirely rigid and front-loaded to support the anticipated launches, the incremental EBITDA margin on the deferred revenue was **104.3%**. Every single rupee of deferred revenue flowed directly out of operating profit.

However, Q1FY27 demonstrates the identical math in reverse. As commercial DDC revenues began to flow, the fixed workforce and cleanroom overhead were instantly absorbed, driving EBITDA margins from the 6% trough back up to **27.5%**. As Line 2 and Line 3 scale, the incremental margin on new commercial campaigns will require minimal additional opex, paving a highly visible path to the targeted **40% steady-state EBITDA margin**.

#### 2. Debunking the Inventory Red Flag: True Specialty CDMO Economics
For a traditional pharmaceutical company, a 178% surge in inventory (from ₹158 crore to ₹440 crore) alongside declining revenues would indicate massive channel stuffing, product obsolescence, or poor working capital discipline.

At OneSource, however, first-principles CDMO mechanics apply:
*   **No P&L Risk:** These are ex-works contracts. The ₹440 crore inventory is fully customer-backed and funded by the ₹250 crore advance liability pool.
*   **Fungible Procurement:** Instead of each generic client procuring packaging, pens, and cartridges independently, OneSource pools the procurement. This maximizes purchasing power and lowers unit costs for clients, while preserving OneSource's capacity allocation fees.
*   **Cash Conversion Lag:** This inventory does not represent cash destruction; it represents **unrecognized high-margin service fee revenue**. Because revenue is recognized strictly on ex-works physical shipments, the temporary balance sheet bulge will convert rapidly into cash as regulatory approvals unlock shipments across G7 and emerging markets.

#### 3. Capital Efficiency and the ROCE Target (>50%)
Achieving a structural ROCE of >50% requires outstanding capital efficiency. OneSource achieves this through two distinct strategic levers:
1.  **Workforce and Asset Efficiency:** The legacy Line 1 was capped at a 200L batch size. The newly qualified Line 2 and Line 3 are engineered for **500L, 750L, and 1,000L batch sizes**. Because the staff opex is already fully baked into the P&L, running 500L campaigns instead of 200L campaigns increases throughput by **2.5x to 5x** within the same operating time blocks. This dramatically reduces unit opex, driving asset turnover and capital returns.
2.  **Refinancing Capital Costs:** Securing an "A family" credit rating and reducing interest costs by 200+ bps structurally lowers the company's weighted average cost of capital (WACC) to below 9%. This ensures that incremental capex on the remaining 20% of the $100M program is funded at highly attractive rates, expanding the net spread between ROCE and WACC.

* **Operating Cash Flow (OCF):** OCF is generated upon the liquidation of customer receivables and cash collection, but is heavily influenced by the cash-conversion cycle of inventories (such as cartridges and device components) and stabilized by customer advances, which sit on the balance sheet at approximately ₹250 crores [248, 253, 319].
* **Capital Consumption:** Capital is consumed by high-speed cartridge line installations, sterile fill-finish capacity additions, cleanroom preparation, and early-phase cell line development [26, 67, 167].

#### Interpretation & Challenge
The fundamental economic challenge of the OneSource business model is the severe mismatch between the timeline of capital/operating expenditures and revenue recognition. This mismatch was painfully demonstrated in Q3FY26, where G7 approval delays resulted in deferred revenues, creating severe negative operating leverage and compressing EBITDA margins to a transitional low of 6% [40, 245].

For a 6–12 month momentum investor, the economic engine is transitioning from the highly volatile "opex-upfronting, revenue-deferred" phase into the "positive operating leverage cash-generation" phase. Because the bulk of the employee and cleanroom opex has already been fully expensed on the P&L in FY26, the activation of the high-speed Line 2 in Q2FY27 will act as an immediate margin catalyst, as incremental volumes will require virtually zero additional fixed opex and flow directly to operating profit [381, 435].

### Section 5: Unit Economics of a Single Customer Program

#### Facts
* **Phase 1: Customer Acquisition & MSA Onboarding:** OneSource acquires clients through competitive RFP processes. Customers frequently participate in OneSource's growth capex or pay upfront **capacity reservation fees** to secure dedicated aseptic filling days [182, 195].
* **Phase 2: Master Services Agreement (MSA) & Tech Transfer:** Tech transfer, process validation, and clinical batches are executed over 12–18 months [108]. During this phase, manufacturing is suboptimal: OneSource can only manufacture **2 batches in 7 days** due to frequent line changeovers [260]. Realizations are project-based and milestones are unrecognized as commercial revenue until final shipment [212].
* **Phase 3: Commercial Approval & CSA Transition:** Once regulators (Health Canada, USFDA) clear the drug-device dossier, the contract transitions to a Commercial Supply Agreement (CSA) [35, 66].
* **Phase 4: Commercial Scale-up & High-Speed Campaigns:** Under the CSA, campaign run-rates scale up to **6 batches in 7 days** [260].
* **Phase 5: Throughput & Revenue Recognition:** Physical finished goods exit the factory gate. Operating on an ex-works basis, the customer pays the contract price ex-works, and OneSource recognizes revenue strictly upon shipment [198, 211, 212].
* **Gross Margin & EBITDA:** Commercial DDC revenues carry ultra-high specialty margins. Peer reports indicate Gross Margins are projected to scale to **~72% by FY28E** with EBITDA margins scaling to the **~40% steady-state target** [40, 300].

```
  SINGLE-PROGRAM ECONOMIC LIFECYCLE:
  [Upfront Capacity Reservation / Capex Fee] (Secures the slot)
                       ↓
  [12-18 Month MSA Tech Transfer] (suboptimal 2 batches/7 days; low asset turns)
                       ↓
  [G7 Regulatory Approval EIR / Clearance] (Triggers transition to CSA)
                       ↓
  [CSA Commercial Scale-Up] (Optimal 6 batches/7 days; line changes minimized)
                       ↓
  [Ex-Works Physical Shipment] (Triggers immediate high-margin revenue recognition)
```

#### Interpretation & Challenge
* *What improves unit economics?* The primary drivers of unit economics are **batch-size scale-ups and campaign lengths**. Moving from legacy Line 1 (suboptimal 200L batch size, capping daily yield at ~60,000 pens) to the newly qualified Line 2 (engineered for 500L, 750L, and 1,000L batch sizes) increases single-day cartridge yield by **2.5x to 5x** within the same operating day block [331, 355]. Furthermore, moving from MSA (2 batches/7 days) to CSA (6 batches/7 days) triples available capacity utilization, spreading fixed utility and personnel costs over a 3x larger volume [260].
* *What destroys unit economics?* **Regulatory approval delays and raw material shortages**. Because OneSource hired 380+ specialists and fully provisioned the opex floor for Line 2 ahead of G7 launches, any regulatory delay (such as Dr. Reddy's Health Canada query) temporarily freezes revenue recognition while the fixed-opex burn continues, resulting in negative operating leverage [237, 245].
* *Why is commercial manufacturing superior to development revenue?* Development/MSA revenues are project-based, non-recurring, and technically capped by low capacity utilization (due to frequent line cleaning and product changeovers) [183, 260]. Conversely, commercial/CSA revenues are highly repeatable, backed by long-term take-or-pay structures, and designed to run continuous campaigns that amortize the cleanroom's heavy fixed costs [66, 260].

#### Investment Implication
For a 6–12 month holding period, the massive volume of programs transitioning from MSA to CSA (such as Canada and India Semaglutide) means OneSource is entering a structural margin expansion sweet spot. The fixed overhead base is already fully reflected in the historical P&L, meaning incremental commercial volumes on Line 2 will flow to EBITDA at exceptionally high incremental margins, accelerating near-term EPS growth.

### Group 1: CSA Contract Structure and Durability (Addressing Q1)

#### Facts
* **Guarantees and Penalties:** Commercial Supply Agreements (CSAs) are backed by take-or-pay structures. If a customer fails to take their forecasted volumes, they are contractually obligated to pay a minimum capacity allocation fee or forfeit capex contributions.
* **Duration & Tenure:** While early-stage Master Services Agreements (MSAs) focus on non-recurring clinical or R&D development, CSAs lock in long-term commercial supply visibility "beyond three years". Management proactively renegotiates take-or-pay contracts to extend contract durations even further.
* **Pricing & Volume Tiers:** OneSource’s commercial CDMO pricing is completely geography-agnostic; pricing is fixed based upon pre-negotiated volume tiers rather than the final retail market.
* **Competitive Resets:** In highly sophisticated regulated G7 markets, generic customers typically lock in long-term contracts by granting OneSource a "first sight of refusal" to match competitive market pricing over time.

#### Interpretation
The combination of fixed volume-tier pricing and take-or-pay mechanisms shields OneSource from downstream retail pricing wars and market-share volatility. Upfront capacity reservation fees and customer-funded capex contributions ensure that the client has substantial "skin in the game" before commercial manufacturing begins. By trading minor near-term take-or-pay flexibility for extended multi-year contract tenures, management structurally extends the duration of high-margin commercial revenue streams, enhancing long-term cash flow predictability.

#### Investment Implication
For a 6–12 month momentum investor, the long-term, take-or-pay nature of these CSAs ensures that as G7 and emerging market launches occur, the contracted revenue base is highly defensive and virtually immune to generic market price erosion.

### Group 2: The True Anatomy of the CDMO Moat (Addressing Q2)

#### Facts
* **Device Platform Integration:** OneSource supports 11 distinct automated device platforms (pens, autoinjectors, pre-filled syringes), enabling 100% end-to-end packed and serialized delivery. No domestic or global competitor offers this level of end-to-end integration under a single roof.
* **Switching Costs & Gestation Times:** CDMO relationships are bounded by extremely high gestation periods: tech transfers take 12–15 months for soft gelatin, and 12–18 months for sterile biologics, with clinical to commercial conversion in biologics requiring 2–3 years.
* **Regulatory Interlocking:** Approved drug dossiers are filed with regulatory agencies (Health Canada, USFDA) interlocked with the specific site. For a customer to transition away, they must repeat tech transfer, run validation batches, and submit a new regulatory filing.
* **Line Availability Barrier:** Securing and qualifying a new high-speed aseptic cartridge/filling line takes a minimum lead time of two years globally.

#### Interpretation
Features like "11 device platforms" do not define the moat; rather, they are the hook that interlocks the customer into an incredibly high-switching-cost environment. Once a customer has submitted their regulatory dossier linking their generic or biosimilar therapeutic to OneSource's specific facility, **leaving is economically punitive**. A client attempting to switch CDMOs faces a minimum 12-to-18-month tech transfer delay, risks losing first-to-file market advantages, must fund millions in re-validation costs, and confronts a 2-year global lead-time bottleneck just to secure alternative sterile filling capacity.

### One-Paragraph Conclusion

The financial evidence strongly supports OneSource's specialty CDMO business thesis, indicating that **financial momentum is structurally improving** as of Q1FY27. The P&L and balance sheet mismatch of H2FY26 was a temporary operational transition, not a structural failure [194, 247]. Upfronting employee opex and building a ₹4,400 million customer-funded inventory pool were necessary steps to secure G7 commercial launches, which are now successfully translating into high-margin revenue (₹1,233 million Q1FY27 EBITDA) [59, 252].

For a 6–12 month momentum investor, the **single biggest financial variable likely to change earnings expectations is the commercial qualification and batch scale-up on DDC Line 2 in Q2FY27**. Doubling available capacity to 450 sterile days and shifting to 500-liter campaigns will unlock exceptional positive operating leverage, driving reported earnings significantly ahead of consensus estimates on the fixed **11.5 crore share base**.
