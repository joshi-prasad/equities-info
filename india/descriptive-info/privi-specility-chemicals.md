# Business Model

### a) What Does the Company Actually Do?

**## Facts**
Privi manufactures aroma chemicals—over 75 distinct molecules—that form the foundational scents and flavors used by global FMCG companies and fragrance houses. Its core chemistry relies on extracting molecules from pine tree by-products (Crude Sulphate Turpentine) and synthesizing them into ingredients used in daily consumables.

**## Interpretation**
Privi solves a critical supply-chain problem for global consumer brands: providing a stable, high-quality, and environmentally sustainable source of olfactory molecules completely independent of China. It does not sell finished perfumes or soaps; it sells the essential chemical building blocks that make those products smell appealing.

**## Investment Implication**
Because these molecules go into everyday consumer staples (soaps, shampoos, detergents), demand is inherently recurring and resilient to macroeconomic shocks. For a momentum investor, this means volume growth is highly visible and deeply insulated from cyclical industrial downturns.

### c) Who Are the Customers?

**## Facts**
Privi supplies the top 15 global fragrance and flavor (F&F) houses (e.g., Givaudan, IFF, Symrise) and direct FMCG giants (e.g., Procter & Gamble, Reckitt). Exports account for 70%+ of revenues.

### d) How Does the Business Actually Work?

**## Facts**
The operating cycle:
1. **Sourcing:** Secures Crude Sulphate Turpentine (CST) from 70+ paper mills globally via annual contracts.
2. **Fractionation:** Extracts Alpha and Beta Pinene from CST.
3. **Synthesis & Value Addition:** Converts pinenes into specific aroma molecules.
4. **Waste-to-Wealth (Side-streams):** Extracts remaining by-products (like Limonene) to create additional high-margin chemicals instead of burning them as fuel.
5. **Sales & Collection:** Ships to global FMCG/F&F clients under 6–12 month volume/price contracts.

**## Interpretation**
The highest economic value is created in step 2 and step 4. Extracting pure pinenes from toxic, sulphate-heavy CST requires complex chemical engineering. Monetizing the side-streams ("waste-to-wealth") effectively drops the input cost of the flagship molecules, creating a massive marginal-cost pricing advantage.

**## Investment Implication**
Privi’s ability to extract value from chemical waste means its gross margins are structurally superior to competitors who use direct, expensive petrochemical routes.

**## Interpretation**
In specialty chemicals, the manufacturing infrastructure *is* the technology. Handling highly volatile terpenes and achieving ZLD at a massive scale requires decades of operational tweaking. The barrier to entry is not just capital; it is the institutional knowledge of running these specific, dangerous chemical reactions safely and efficiently.

**## Investment Implication**
Competitors cannot simply replicate Privi's capacity with capital. This structural manufacturing moat protects the company's robust ~19.5% ROCE from new entrants over your 6–12 month holding period.

# Unit Economics

### 1. What Is Privi's Economic Engine?

**## Facts**
Privi generates revenue by processing crude sulphate turpentine (CST), gum turpentine oil (GTO), and specific petrochemicals into 75+ complex aroma molecules. It sells these under 6–12 month back-to-back contracts to the top 15 global flavor and fragrance (F&F) and FMCG companies. The company captures margins through a structural 15–20% cost advantage in processing CST (a paper industry byproduct) versus standard GTO. Cash is generated by optimizing the working capital required to hold these raw materials, which are sourced globally from 70+ paper mills. Capital is consumed through continuous chemical process debottlenecking, high-barrier R&D (114 scientists), and severe compliance mandates like Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD).

**## Interpretation**
The economic engine is fundamentally a "waste-to-wealth" arbitrage model layered with high-barrier process engineering. Privi takes toxic, highly sulphated waste (CST), utilizes its proprietary extraction technology, and monetizes not only the primary alpha and beta pinenes but also the residual side-streams (like limonene and 1,4-Cineol). Because the raw material cost for these side-streams is essentially zero, incremental revenues drop directly to gross profit.

**## Investment Implication**
**Cause-and-Effect Chain:**
Global Paper Mill Contracts (CST Access) → Proprietary Fractionation (Cost Advantage) → Base Molecule Production (Fixed Cost Absorption) → By-product Monetization (Margin Expansion) → Sticky FMCG Sales (Cash Generation) → Internal Accruals Funding High-Value Specialty Capex (ROCE Expansion).
For a momentum investor, this engine implies that as long as capacity utilization remains high (currently ~90%), any new specialty molecule introduced into the mix will disproportionately accelerate earnings over revenue.

### 6. Why Has ROCE Improved?

ROCE improved dramatically from 12.73% (FY23) to 22.42% (FY26).

**Decomposition:**
*   **Margins (Structural):** Transitioning low-value CST byproducts into saleable aroma chemicals essentially created "free" gross margin. Gross margins expanded from ~42.4% (FY24) to 48.6% (FY26).
*   **Working Capital (Structural & Cyclical):** Cash Conversion Cycle compressed from 264 days (FY23) to 158 days (FY26). Aligning back-to-back supply contracts mitigated inventory bloat.
*   **Capacity Utilization (Cyclical moving to Structural):** Running existing assets at 85-90% utilization drastically improved fixed asset turnover.

**Conclusion:** The ROCE improvement is predominantly structural, driven by permanent engineering improvements (continuous processing, ZLD compliance) and a permanent shift in product mix toward multi-specialty molecules.

# FTA and Tariffs

**Effects of USA Tariffs**
Privi Speciality Chemicals expects the overall impact of U.S. tariffs to be highly favorable, despite some earlier market concerns.

*   **Limited Negative Exposure:** Initially, management stated they did not anticipate any significant negative impact from ongoing U.S. tariffs because exports to the U.S. account for only about 7% to 10% of their overall revenue. The company noted that the specialty chemicals they supply are largely not produced domestically within the U.S., making their products a necessity. Furthermore, management indicated that any increased tariff costs would likely be absorbed partially by their Fragrance & Flavor (F&F) partners and customers.
*   **Competitive Advantage:** During the Q3 FY26 earnings call, management highlighted that the U.S. lifting a 25% penalty tariff gives Privi a "clear-cut advantage". Because almost all other supplying countries (especially China) have to bear higher tariffs than India, Privi enjoys a favorable duty rate of 18%, and in some cases, even 0%.
