# Jyoti CNC Automation Ltd

# Business Model

## 1. What Does Jyoti CNC Actually Do?

### 1.1 Core Business Operations
*   **FACT:** Jyoti CNC Automation Limited is an Indian mother-machine manufacturer engaged in designing, developing, manufacturing, selling, exporting, and servicing Computer Numerical Control (CNC) metal cutting machines, their critical subassemblies, and providing lifecycle after-sales support. Operating out of two facilities in Rajkot, Gujarat (237,408 sq. meters) and one subsidiary plant in Strasbourg, France (46,442 sq. meters), the company operates in a single reportable business segment under Ind AS 108.
*   **INTERPRETATION:** Jyoti CNC is a capital-goods technology provider rather than a component manufacturer or trading distributor. It builds subtractive manufacturing equipment—the foundational "mother machines" used by downstream industrial factories to cut, shape, and finish metal alloys into precision components.
*   **INVESTMENT RELEVANCE:** Mother-machine manufacturing sits at the apex of the industrial capex cycle. Demand is non-linear and highly correlated with private corporate capex, industrial localization initiatives ("Make in India"), defense indigenization, and technology transitions (e.g., EV component adoption and EMS localization).

### 1.2 The Manufacturing Problem Solved by CNC Machines
*   **FACT:** Manual machining relies on human operator skill to manipulate cutting tools, resulting in variable tolerances, high rejection rates, low throughput, and an inability to fabricate complex three-dimensional contours. Jyoti CNC's machines utilize automated microprocessors, high-rigidity cast beds, motorized precision spindles, and multi-axis servo drives to execute computer-coded tool paths with micron-level repeatability.
*   **INTERPRETATION:** The primary value proposition to customers is total cost of production reduction through cycle-time compression, minimal scrap rates, automated labor substitution, and the capability to machine difficult-to-cut metals (titanium, Inconel, hardened steel) required in mission-critical applications.
*   **INVESTMENT RELEVANCE:** High-precision capabilities lock in customer relationships, creating switching costs once a machine tool is qualified on a customer's production line for OEM part manufacturing.

### 1.3 Machine Tool Manufacturer vs. Component Manufacturer
*   **FACT:** Jyoti CNC manufactures and sells capital equipment (B2B capex for its clients) ranging from Rs 10 lakh to Rs 20 crore per unit. It does not operate contract job-shops to produce commercial components for third parties, though it manufactures its own internal machine spare parts.
*   **INTERPRETATION:**
    *   *Revenue Model:* Highly transaction-based capital sales supplemented by recurring after-sales service/spares. Revenue depends on winning new machinery orders and dispatching units rather than recurring component volume production.
    *   *Working Capital Profile:* Longer build cycles (3 to 18 months), requiring substantial work-in-progress (WIP) and inventory buffers compared to component makers who run fast daily inventory turns.

### 2.2 Customer End-Markets & Demand Drivers
*   **Aerospace & Defense (~38–41% of Order Book):** Demands ultra-high-precision 5-axis and multi-tasking machines capable of machining exotic alloys. Driven by defense indigenization, fighter aircraft programs (AMCA, LCA Tejas), and global commercial aerospace supply chains.
*   **Automotive & Auto Components (~19% of Order Book):** High-volume production of engine components, gearboxes, and EV motor housings utilizing automated turning centres, VMCs, and specialized alloy wheel machines (AWT series).
*   **General Engineering (~20% of Order Book):** Broad base of job-shops and industrial machinery suppliers requiring versatile entry-level and mid-range VMCs/turning centres.
*   **Electronics & EMS (Emerging Growth Sector):** High-speed drill-tap VMCs for precision smartphone enclosures, semiconductor tooling, and electronic hardware.
*   **Railways & Infrastructure:** Large double-column machines (NX series) replacing imported Taiwanese and Korean equipment.
*   **Die & Mould:** High-precision surface-finishing VMCs. *Note:* Die & Mould order inflows act as a leading indicator, preceding EMS and Auto volume production by 2–3 quarters.

### 2.3 Customer Buying Criteria & Competitive Differentiation
*   **FACT:**
    1.  *Integrated Vertical Manufacturing:* Jyoti manufactures its own iron castings, precision spindles, turrets, rotary tables, tool changers, and sheet metal enclosures in-house. This yields a ~55–57% gross margin compared to 35–40% for domestic assembly peers (BFW, Ace Micromatic, LMW).
    2.  *Proprietary Industry 4.0 & AI Software:* Equips machines with "7th Sense" (process automation) and "PreciProtect" (real-time AI collision avoidance).
    3.  *Application Engineering & Lifecycle Support:* Provides customized tooling setups, time-study estimations, local spare parts availability, and escalatory after-sales service teams.
*   **INTERPRETATION:** Customers do not buy CNC machines solely on hardware specs; they buy guaranteed part cycle-times and production uptime. In-house component manufacturing allows Jyoti to offer custom application-engineered solutions at a lower price point than European/Japanese imports while maintaining industry-leading gross margins.
*   **INVESTMENT RELEVANCE:** High gross margins provide a strategic buffer against raw material fluctuations and enable heavy reinvestment in R&D and capacity expansion.
*   **MONITORABLE:** Sustainability of gross margins above 55% as product mix shifts toward lower-ASP standard turning centres.

### 3.2 Key Commercial Milestones & Revenue Recognition Mechanics
*   **FACT:**
    *   *Enquiry vs. Order Book:* Enquiries represent sales pipeline leads; orders are firm contractual commitments with advance deposits entered into the Rs 4,732–4,850 crore order book.
    *   *Standalone Revenue Recognition:* Recognized upon machine dispatch/delivery to customer site.
    *   *Percentage of Completion Method (POCM):* Applied to long-cycle custom Aerospace & Defense orders (~38% of business) where revenue is recognized progressively based on build completion, creating unbilled receivables prior to final cash milestone collection.
    *   *Huron Accounting Method Shift (FY27 Q1):* Huron adopted a strict delivery-based revenue recognition policy requiring explicit receipt of export licenses and end-user certificates (EUC) prior to revenue booking. Machines produced but awaiting government export clearance are retained in inventory rather than booked as unbilled revenue.
*   **INTERPRETATION:** The accounting shift at Huron removes revenue reversal risks but introduces quarter-to-quarter revenue lumpiness based on regulatory clearance timing (e.g., Rs 67 crore revenue deferred in Q1FY27).
*   **INVESTMENT RELEVANCE:** Standalone revenue recognition is clean and cash-backed, whereas POCM in A&D creates temporary working capital expansion during periods of accelerating order inflows.
*   **MONITORABLE:** Rate of unbilled receivables growth vs. inventory turnover normalisation.

### 4.2 Revenue Architecture & Operating Segments
*   **FACT:**
    1.  *Geographic Mix:* ~60–65% Domestic (India) and ~35–40% Exports/International.
    2.  *Standalone vs. Consolidated:* Standalone India drives >85% of consolidated revenue (~Rs 18.5 bn revenue, Rs 5.7 bn EBITDA in FY26). Huron contributed ~Rs 2.5 bn revenue in FY26, with FY27 guidance set at Rs 3.0–3.25 bn and 8–10% EBITDA margin.
    3.  *Intercompany Elimination:* Substantial component subassemblies and castings are manufactured in Rajkot and shipped to Huron in France; intercompany transactions (~Rs 35 crore in Q1FY27) are eliminated on consolidation.
    4.  *Revenue Streams:* Primary revenue from machinery dispatches; secondary recurring revenue from spare parts sales, tooling, and after-sales maintenance contracts.

### 5.2 Key Transition Drivers & Strategic Initiatives
1.  **Massive Rajkot Capacity Expansion (+10,000 Machine Block):**
    *   *FACT:* Expanding annual Rajkot capacity from 6,000 to 16,000 machines per annum by September 2026 via a Rs 4.26–4.5 bn expansion project (funded via Rs 3 bn term loan + internal accruals).
    *   *INTERPRETATION:* 70–80% of new capacity is dedicated to standardized turning centres and entry-level VMCs targeting high-volume import substitution (competing against imports from Taiwan, China, Korea). Shorter manufacturing cycles (3–6 months vs. 12–18 months for A&D) will accelerate inventory turns and improve operating cash flow.
2.  **Southern Region Footprint Expansion (TMTP Tumakuru, Karnataka):**
    *   *FACT:* Acquired ~20 acres of industrial land at Tumakuru Machine Tool Park (TMTP) along the Chennai–Bengaluru Industrial Corridor.
    *   *INTERPRETATION:* Positions manufacturing operations directly adjacent to South India's major EMS, auto, and defense clusters, reducing freight costs and delivery response times.
3.  **Huron Capacity Doubling & European Base:**
    *   *FACT:* Expanded Strasbourg production facility in late 2025 (2x capacity expansion) to serve European aerospace demand.
    *   *INTERPRETATION:* Serves as the technology backbone for 5-axis R&D while providing a global brand front-end for international sales.
4.  **In-House Controller & Electronics Integration (ECMS / PLI Scheme):**
    *   *FACT:* Developing proprietary CNC controllers, servo drives, and motors. Developed "HUMA" HMI front-end and submitted applications under the Central Government's Electronic Component Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS) / PLI for capital subsidies.
    *   *INTERPRETATION:* Controllers represent 20–30% of machine input costs. In-house controller commercialization could expand gross margins by an incremental 4–5% while eliminating reliance on Siemens and Fanuc.
5.  **Huron Judicial Investigation (DNRED France Overhang):**
    *   *FACT:* In April 2026, French customs intelligence (DNRED) initiated a formal judicial investigation into Huron Graffenstaden SAS regarding alleged EU export control violations for dual-use machinery. Interim measures include EUR 3.02 mn bank balance seizure (EUR ~1mn released) and attachment of 2 properties, causing a Rs 670 mn revenue reversal in FY26.
    *   *INTERPRETATION:* Indian operations (governed by DGFT/SCOMET) are legally and operationally isolated with zero shared manufacturing dependency. Huron accounts for <15% of consol revenue and ~Rs 2.5 bn revenue.
*   **INVESTMENT RELEVANCE:** The market has heavily discounted consol earnings due to Huron overhang, underappreciating the standalone India execution (>85% revenue, 27.5%+ EBITDA margins).
*   **MONITORABLE:** Formal DNRED investigation settlement/closure and FY27 Huron revenue guidance delivery (Rs 3.0–3.25 bn).

## 6. Synthesis: The Business Model as One Connected System

### 6.1 Plain English Business Model Summary
Jyoti CNC Automation Limited is India's leading vertically integrated mother-machine manufacturer. It designs, builds, and services precision CNC machines that other industrial factories use to manufacture metal parts. By producing critical high-precision components (castings, spindles, turrets) in-house and leveraging proprietary European 5-axis technology from its French subsidiary Huron, Jyoti achieves industry-leading gross margins (~55–57%). The company is currently executing a major capacity expansion in Rajkot from 6,000 to 16,000 machines/year. While its existing business focuses on long-build, high-margin Aerospace & Defense machines (12–18 month cycles), the new capacity is focused on standard turning centres and VMCs (3–6 month cycles) to capture a massive domestic import substitution opportunity in EMS, Auto, and General Engineering, driving a working capital normalisation and operating cash flow recovery.

## 7. Key Unanswered Questions & Critical Monitorables

Before evaluating unit economics and detailed financial valuation models, five critical business-model gaps require ongoing monitoring:

1.  **Standard Machine Margin Dilution vs. Fixed Cost Absorption:** What is the exact gross margin differential for the new standard turning/VMC machines produced in the +10,000 block, and will higher operating leverage/fixed cost absorption fully offset lower unit ASPs ?
2.  **Huron DNRED Investigation Resolution Timeline & Liabilities:** What is the legal timeline for resolving the French customs judicial review, what are the maximum potential monetary penalties, and could export restrictions spill over into European customer ordering sentiment ?
3.  **In-House Controller Technical Validation & Adoption Rate:** How rapidly can Jyoti transition from its "HUMA" HMI layer to full proprietary controllers/servo drives without facing customer pushback or reliability concerns in high-precision tier-1 applications ?
4.  **Working Capital Conversion Efficiency:** How quickly will the mix shift toward shorter-cycle standard machines (3–6 months) reduce inventory days and convert unbilled POCM receivables into positive Free Cash Flow ?
5.  **TMTP Karnataka Capex Phasing & Debt Burden:** What is the total estimated capex schedule for the 20-acre Tumakuru facility, and can it be executed entirely via internal accruals without increasing net debt above the management target of ~1x EBITDA ?
