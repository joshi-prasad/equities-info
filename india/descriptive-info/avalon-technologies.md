### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: THE MOMENTUM LENS
From a momentum perspective, the stock market does not merely reward a business for being "good"; it rewards a business whose fundamentals are **getting better at an accelerating rate**, leading to consecutive quarters of positive earnings revisions [81, 82].

Our systematic, first-principles analysis of Avalon Technologies Limited (AVALON) across FY26 reveals a business undergoing a **structural, margin-accretive transformation**. This is driven by three powerful levers:
1. **The Dual-Shore Synergy:** Transitioning high-cost US prototyping (Atlanta and Fremont) into high-volume, low-cost India manufacturing (Chennai and Bengaluru) [2, 33, 51].
2. **Product Mix Escalation:** Rapidly scaling the contribution of complex, mission-critical "Box Builds" (from 49% in FY25 to 56% in Q4 FY26) [27, 47].
3. **Exceptional Working Capital Efficiency:** Reducing Net Working Capital (NWC) days from a bloated 163 days in June 2024 to an efficient 112 days in March 2026 [15, 40, 56].

The result is **seven consecutive quarters of sequential improvement** in operating performance [56], culminating in FY26 revenue growth of **46% YoY** to **INR 1,603 Cr** [56, 58], EBITDA growth of **50.9% YoY** to **INR 173 Cr** [56], and PAT growth of **78.0% YoY** to **INR 113 Cr** [56]. Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) has expanded from **15.7% to 20.6%** [49], demonstrating that AVALON’s earnings momentum is accelerating as it enters FY27 with clear visibility.

### Section A: What the Company Actually Does & Outsourcing Value Proposition

#### 1. Plain-English Operational Core
Avalon Technologies is a fully integrated Electronic Manufacturing Services (EMS) company [15]. It does not simply "solder electronic chips onto boards." Instead, it is an end-to-end provider of high-value, precision-engineered electronic systems, specializing in **complex "Box Builds" and system integration** [15, 64].

An electronic system is not a single component. It is a combination of several physical disciplines:
* **Printed Circuit Board Assembly (PCBA):** The electronic "brain" of the product, where components are mounted using Surface Mount Technology (SMT) or Through-Hole Technology (THT) [10, 12, 13].
* **Cables and Wire Harnesses:** The "nervous system" that transmits power and signals between various modules [11].
* **Magnetics:** Customized inductors and transformers that regulate voltage and current [11, 70].
* **Sheet Metal and Machining:** The precision-engineered enclosure that houses the electronics [10, 11].
* **Plastics:** Custom-molded structural components [10, 11].

**Box Build / System Integration** represents the final stage of manufacturing [10, 64]. Avalon manufactures or sources each of these sub-assemblies internally, mounts them together, installs the software, tests the completed machine under load, and delivers a fully finished, retail-ready product to the customer [10, 65, 76].

#### 2. The Real-World OEM Pain Point
For an Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM), coordinating with individual suppliers for sheet metal, wire harnesses, plastics, magnetics, and PCB assemblies is a supply-chain nightmare. It leads to:
* High coordination drag and communication overheads.
* Quality disputes between suppliers (e.g., the sheet metal enclosure supplier blaming the PCB manufacturer for a misfit).
* Heavy capital outlays for in-house testing and assembly lines.

**Avalon’s vertical integration solves this coordination problem completely [10].** By acting as a "One-Stop Shop," Avalon manages the entire micro-supply chain [10]. This guarantees that all components integrate flawlessly, significantly reduces lead times, and allows the OEM to compress its time-to-market.

#### 3. The Division of Labor: What is Kept vs. Outsourced
The economic division of labor between the OEM and Avalon is clearly demarcated [11, 66]:

| What the OEM Keeps In-House | What the OEM Outsources to Avalon |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Product Conceptualization & Core IP:** Defining what the product must achieve and its intellectual property. | **Design for Manufacturing (DFM):** Optimizing the customer's design to make it cheaper, faster, and more reliable to produce [11, 42]. |
| **High-Level Design & R&D:** Original schematic and firmware development. | **Prototyping & NPI:** Building the first physical replicas of the product to test and qualify the lines [10, 11, 65]. |
| **Brand Management & Marketing:** Building brand awareness and sales channels. | **Component Sourcing & Procurement:** Leveraging scale to source hundreds of individual electronic parts globally [65, 70]. |
| **Final Sales & Customer Relationship:** Distributing the finished product to the end user. | **Sub-Assembly & Box-Build Assembly:** Performing precision assembly of all sub-parts into the final system [10, 11]. |
| | **Functional Testing & Quality Assurance:** Subjecting the completed box to simulated real-world conditions [10, 69]. |

### Section C: The Nature of Demand & Customer Stickiness

#### 1. Demand Composition
Avalon’s revenue stream is not built on transactional, one-time sales. It is a highly sticky, recurring model driven by program wins [81, 82].

*   **Recurring Production (Core):** Once a customer program is won and qualified, Avalon receives rolling Purchase Orders (POs) over the program’s 5-to-7-year lifecycle [22, 47].
*   **New Program Ramp-ups (Growth):** Fresh orders from existing logos or new clients entering commercial production.
*   **Prototype / NPI Work (Pipeline):** Low-volume, high-margin design and validation orders that serve as the direct precursor to multi-year volume production [33].

#### 2. The Mechanics of the Customer Lock-In
EMS relationships in mission-critical verticals are incredibly sticky [22, 47].
1.  **Astronomical Switching Costs:** Complex parts can require **200 to 300 hours of manual labor and customized engineering setup** [22]. Moving a program to a competitor takes 12 to 24 months, requires re-qualifying the assembly lines, and risks production shutdown.
2.  **Stringent Regulatory Qualification:** Sectors like Aerospace, Medical, and Railways require multi-month, facility-level audits and strict certifications (e.g., NADCAP, IATF 16949) [15, PR Q1/Q2/Q3]. Once qualified, customers are highly reluctant to change suppliers [22].
3.  **Sticky Co-dependencies:** By fabricating the custom metals, magnetics, and plastics in-house, Avalon becomes deeply integrated into the customer’s design loop, creating strong product co-dependency [10, 47].

#### 3. What Happens When Customer Demand Fluctuates?
If the end customer’s market demand falls, they do not cancel their program; they adjust their rolling POs downward. This causes Avalon’s short-term revenue to decline, but the customer remains with Avalon. When demand recovers, volumes scale back up without customer acquisition costs.

### Section D: The 12-Step Program Lifecycle & Order Book Microeconomics

Unlike an EPC or capital goods company where an order book is executed once and cleared, an EMS order book represents **near-term revenue visibility** under multi-year customer relationships [17, 27, 81].

```
               THE 12-STEP PROGRAM LIFECYCLE

 [Step 1] Customer Engagement  ==>  [Step 2] RFQ / Quotation
                                            ||
 [Step 4] Prototype / NPI     <==  [Step 3] Design for Mfg (DFM)
          (Front-loaded US plant)
   ||
 [Step 5] Line Qualification   ==>  [Step 6] Commercial PO / Contract
          & Certifications                 (15-36 month backlog)
                                            ||
 [Step 8] India Mfg & Assembly <==  [Step 7] Procurement of Materials
          (PCBA, Cables, Metals)            (Cash Outlay)
   ||
 [Step 9] Box Build Integration ==>  [Step 10] QA & Functional Testing
                                            ||
[Step 12] Repeat Run & Ramp   <==  [Step 11] Shipment & Revenue Recog.
```

*   **Step 1: Customer Engagement:** Avalon’s sales team onboards global OEMs (e.g., in Industrial or Defense verticals) [Q3 Conf Call].
*   **Step 2: RFQ & Quotation:** The OEM submits schematics; Avalon quotes based on the Bill of Materials (BOM) and value-add margins [65, 76].
*   **Step 3: Design for Manufacturing (DFM):** Avalon's Sienna ECAD team optimizes the layout to lower manufacturing cost and component failure rates [11, 42].
*   **Step 4: Prototype & Rapid NPI:** Built in localized US facilities (Fremont or Atlanta) for rapid feedback and real-time collaboration with the customer’s design teams [10, 33, 42].
*   **Step 5: Customer Qualification & Audits:** The customer qualifies the production line, confirming certifications (e.g., IPC-A-610 J) [15].
*   **Step 6: Commercial Production Backlog:** The program is logged into the **Order Book** (the near-term 12-14 month executable book, standing at **INR 2,196 Cr in March 2026**) and long-term contracts (executable over 15-36 months, standing at **INR 1,245 Cr**) [56, 57].
*   **Step 7: Raw Material Procurement:** Sourcing silicon, chips, passives, and raw copper. This is where **initial cash is outlaid**, and inventory begins to build [65, 70, 76].
*   **Step 8: Component Manufacturing:** India factories (Chennai/Bengaluru) manufacture wire harnesses, custom magnetics, sheet metal boxes, and plastic enclosures in parallel [10, 11, 42].
*   **Step 9: Surface Mount Technology (SMT) PCBA:** Solder paste inspection (SPI), component mounting, reflow soldering, and automatic optical inspection (AOI) [10, 12].
*   **Step 10: Box-Build & System Integration:** Bringing all manufactured sub-components together into the fabricated metal enclosure [10].
*   **Step 11: Testing & Quality Assurance:** Burn-in tests, functional testing under heat, load, and vibration to guarantee field reliability [10].
*   **Step 12: Shipment, Revenue Recognition & Cash Collection:** Delivered to the customer. **Revenue is recognized upon transfer of risk** [65, 76]. Trade receivables arise, and cash is collected typically 60-90 days later [65, 76].

### Section F: How the Company Makes Money (Revenue Model & Margin Logic)

#### Why Gross Margins Exist in EMS
If Avalon were just a contract manual assembler, it would operate at a very low single-digit gross margin. However, Avalon consistently reports gross margins between **33.7% and 35.8%** [14, 24, 27, 46, 56]. These margins represent payment for:
*   **Engineering Value:** Avalon's Sienna ECAD engineers actively redesign client schematics for manufacturing efficiency (DFM) [11, 42].
*   **Vertical Integration Premium:** Doing PCBA, metal, wire, plastics, and magnetics in-house allows Avalon to capture a margin on each capability, compounding into a higher gross margin on the final Box Build.
*   **stringent Certifications:** High-reliability sectors require strict compliance, allowing Avalon to charge a premium for certified assembly lines.

### Section G: Key Costs & The Material Pass-Through Concept

| **Finance Costs / Interest** | Strictly Fixed | Working capital debt and term loans [49]. |

#### 2. The Raw Material Pass-Through Concept & Margin Optics
A critical concept in EMS investing is that **revenue can expand rapidly simply because material costs rise, which are passed through to the customer [58, Suresh V.R. Q4 Conf Call].**
*   **The Optical Margin Impact:** In Q4 FY26, Suresh V.R. (CFO) noted: *"we passed on substantially all of the tariff impact to customers. So, absolute gross margins were not affected. However, since both revenue and costs were grossed by the tariff pass-through, gross margin percentage was optically impacted by approximately 110 basis points."*
*   **First-Principles Deduction:**
    Suppose a product has:
    *   BOM Cost = $60
    *   Avalon Value Addition = $40
    *   Revenue = $100
    *   Absolute Gross Profit = $40 (Gross Margin % = 40.0%)

    Now, let's say a tariff increases the BOM cost by $10, which is passed through completely to the customer:
    *   New BOM Cost = $70
    *   Avalon Value Addition = $40
    *   New Revenue = $110
    *   New Absolute Gross Profit = $40 (Gross Margin % = 36.36%)

    **Crucial Takeaway for Momentum Investors:** While revenue grew by 10% and absolute gross profit remained unchanged, the gross margin percentage **optically declined by 364 basis points.**
    Therefore, a momentum investor should not focus solely on gross margin percentage expansion. They must track **absolute EBITDA dollar growth** and **EBITDA per unit of value addition** to identify true operating leverage.

#### Chronological Flow & Financial Impact
1.  **Month 1: RFQ & DFM Engineering:**
    *   *What Avalon does:* Sourcing team drafts the BOM; ECAD team optimizes the inverter circuit layout [11, 42].
    *   *Financial impact:* Avalon spends cash on engineering salaries. No revenue is recognized.
2.  **Month 2–3: Prototype & NPI (US Plant):**
    *   *What Avalon does:* Fremont plant builds 5 prototype units [42]. Customer design team works side-by-side on SMT lines [10, 33].
    *   *Financial impact:* High-margin, low-volume prototyping revenue recognized. Cash is spent on high US labor costs [2].
3.  **Month 4: Approval & Backlog Entry:**
    *   *What Avalon does:* Units are qualified; Chennai SEZ plant is certified for commercial production [7].
    *   *Financial impact:* Zero immediate cash flow, but the remaining contract value ($5 million) enters the long-term backlog [17, 27].
4.  **Month 5–6: Material Sourcing (Front-loading):**
    *   *What Avalon does:* Procurement team orders silicon chips, raw copper, and steel sheets globally [70].
    *   *Financial impact:* **Heavy cash outflow.** Avalon must pay raw material vendors. Raw material inventory builds up on the Balance Sheet [PR Q1/Q2/Q3].
5.  **Month 7: Printed Circuit Board Assembly (Chennai):**
    *   *What Avalon does:* Chennai SMT line applies solder paste, mounts chips on pick-and-place machines, and runs reflow soldering [10].
    *   *Financial impact:* Cash outflow on utility power and factory wages. Work-in-Progress (WIP) inventory increases.
6.  **Month 8: Component Manufacturing (Chennai):**
    *   *What Avalon does:* Coils are wound on the magnetics line; sheet metal casing is laser cut and powder-coated; wire harnesses are cut and crimped [10, 11].
    *   *Financial impact:* Value-add is applied in-house, retaining margin [10].
7.  **Month 8: Box Build Assembly & Testing:**
    *   *What Avalon does:* Assemblers integrate PCBAs, magnetics, and harnesses into the metal enclosure, and run functional load tests [10].
    *   *Financial impact:* WIP inventory converts to Finished Goods (FG) inventory.
8.  **Month 9: Shipment & Invoice Delivery:**
    *   *What Avalon does:* 1,000 units are shipped. Invoice is delivered to the customer in the US.
    *   *Financial impact:* **REVENUE RECOGNIZED.** Finished goods inventory is cleared from the Balance Sheet, and a **Trade Receivable of $1,000,000 arises.**
9.  **Month 11–12: Cash Collection:**
    *   *What Avalon does:* The customer pays the invoice after 60-90 days credit.
    *   *Financial impact:* **CASH INFLOW.** Receivables decline, bank cash balances increase, and the working capital cycle clears.

#### Why Growing Profits Consume Working Capital

When Avalon’s revenue is growing at 46% YoY [56], it must purchase raw materials **6 months ahead of final box-build delivery** to satisfy the ramping volumes [PR Q1/Q2/Q3]. Because global semiconductor vendors demand quick payments while major OEMs negotiate long credit terms, **cash outflows on inventory occur far ahead of cash inflows from collections.**

If the working capital cycle is long (e.g., 142 days) [15], the cash required to fund this inventory bridge can exceed reported net profits, resulting in negative operating cash flows during high-growth periods. Hence, **reducing NWC days is structural to converting paper profits into hard cash flow.**

### Section L: Operating Leverage Dynamics

#### 1. How Operating Leverage Works at Avalon
As revenue scales, fixed costs like depreciation, corporate salaries, lease rentals, and US front-end overheads remain stable [77, 74]. This allows a higher percentage of gross profit to flow directly to EBITDA.
*   **The FY26 Evidence:**
    *   Revenue growth: **46.0% YoY** (from INR 1,098 Cr to INR 1,603 Cr) [56].
    *   EBITDA growth: **50.9% YoY** (from INR 115 Cr to INR 173 Cr) [56].
    *   This demonstrates an operating leverage multiplier of **1.11x** at the group level.

#### 2. The US Prototyping Leverage Trigger
The greatest lever for margin expansion in Avalon is **increasing capacity utilization at the US manufacturing plant [21, 33].**
1.  During low-utilization phases, US labor and facility overheads are fixed and create severe margin drag.
2.  As top-line sales grow, these fixed costs are covered, and the US operations turn profitable. Since the US front-end is also the origin point for high-volume India manufacturing, scaling the US front-end drives high-margin export volumes to India [33].

### **SECTION 1: OPERATIONAL PHYSICS, MANUFACTURING COMPLEXITY, & VERTICAL INTEGRATION**
*(Merging Q1: SMT/THT Complexity, Q2: Raw Material In-House vs. Procurement, Q3: IP Boundaries, Q4: Component Assembly vs. Box Build Margins, and Q7: Customer Wallet Share Strategy)*

#### **Q1: SMT vs. THT Complexity, Capex, and Environment**
**Surface Mount Technology (SMT)** and **Through-Hole Technology (THT)** are the two primary methodologies used to mount components onto bare printed circuit boards (PCBs) to create functional Printed Circuit Board Assemblies (PCBAs).
*   **Surface Mount Technology (SMT):** Microscopic electronic components (chips, resistors, capacitors, integrated circuits) are mounted directly onto the surface of the PCB. SMT is a **highly automated, high-precision, and extremely dense packaging technology**. It requires multi-million dollar robotic lines consisting of high-speed solder paste printers, solder paste inspection (SPI) systems, pick-and-place placement machines, reflow soldering ovens, and automated optical inspection (AOI) units.
*   **Through-Hole Technology (THT):** Component leads are inserted into pre-drilled holes on the PCB and soldered on the reverse side. THT is less dense and is primarily a semi-automated or manual process. It is used for larger components (such as heavy-duty connectors, power magnetics, and transformers) that require superior physical shear strength to withstand mechanical stress.
*   **Capex & Specific Environment:** Setting up modern, high-speed SMT lines requires **substantial upfront capital expenditure**. SMT operations are highly sensitive to electrostatic charges and airborne contamination. They require **precise, ESD-protected (electrostatic discharge) and climate-controlled cleanroom environments** with strict regulations on temperature, dust particle filtration, and relative humidity to prevent latent component degradation and soldering micro-voids [25, PR Q2].

#### **Q2 & Q3: Raw Material Procurement, Vertical Integration, and IP Boundaries**
*   **In-house Production vs. External Procurement:** A crucial first-principles distinction must be made between *silicon component inputs* and *structural/electromechanical inputs*. Avalon does **not** manufacture the baseline raw material inputs—such as semiconductors, resistors, silicon chips, complex microprocessors, or multi-layer bare glass-epoxy boards. These are always procured externally from global component manufacturers and distributors.
*   **What Avalon Produces In-house:** Rather than buying finished sub-assemblies (like ready-made cables, stamped metal frames, wound transformers, or molded plastic casings) from third parties, Avalon fabricates these components internally. They design and assemble the PCBA, wind the copper magnetics, stamp the sheet metal enclosures, extrude or injection-mold the plastics, and assemble the custom wire harnesses.
*   **IP Boundaries:** The **conceptual design and product schematics remain the exclusive Intellectual Property (IP) of the OEM customer**. The customer provides the product architecture. Avalon's engineering contribution lies in **Design for Manufacturability (DFM)**—formalizing, physical routing, optimization of metal casing, thermal management, structural design, and prototyping to translate the customer’s logical concept into a physical product that can be reliably and cost-effectively mass-produced.

#### **Q4: Component Assembly vs. Box Build margin and structural mechanics**
*   **Component Assembly:** Supplying individual subsystems (e.g., just a PCBA or a loose cable harness).
*   **Box Build (System Integration):** Consists of **multiple distinct component assemblies integrated together into a complete, finished, and fully enclosed electromechanical system**. For example, a complete Box Build involves placing multiple PCBAs, winding complex magnetics, routing intricate cable systems and wire harnesses, enclosing them inside custom-fabricated sheet metal or molded plastic housings, and adding heat sinks, fans, and interfaces.
*   **Margin Trajectory:** Box builds command structurally higher margins. Component supply has lower entry barriers and intense price competition. An end-to-end Box Build requires **integration complexity, functional system-level testing, and complete supply chain management**. This vertical integration allows Avalon to retain the manufacturing margins on the sheet metal, cables, and magnetics in-house, driving superior group profitability.

#### **Q7: Wallet Share Expansion Per Customer**
Avalon employs a highly structured **"Commodity-to-Box" land-and-expand client delivery model**:
1.  **Entry Point:** They secure customer acquisition by winning a contract for a single commodity segment (e.g., supplying just the wire harnesses or loose sheet metal brackets).
2.  **Cross-Selling:** Over a 2-to-3-year window, they leverage their multi-disciplinary capabilities to cross-sell additional subsystems (e.g., "We are already making cables for you, let us also supply the PCB assembly and the plastic modules").
3.  **Forward Integration:** Once the trust is established and multiple components are qualified, they forward-integrate the client's business into a high-value, complete Box Build. This forward integration effectively consolidates the client's multi-vendor supply chain into a single point of delivery, multiplying Avalon's dollar wallet share per customer program by 5x to 10x.

This also helps explain why management may willingly incur costs before revenue ramps.

2. The clearest execution priorities appear to be: scaling higher-complexity/value-add programs, improving US economics, and controlling working capital. These three priorities map directly into the financial KPIs we have already identified: incremental margins, US standalone losses, and NWC days.

3. Front-loading is an important feature of Avalon's economics. Management may incur manpower, training, inventory and NPI costs before the corresponding revenue arrives. This creates a potentially useful quarterly pattern: `cost ↑ first → program ramps → revenue ↑ → utilization ↑ → margin ↑`

For our momentum horizon, identifying where Avalon currently sits in that cycle could be valuable.

4. Avoiding backward integration into bare PCBs is strategically consistent with the financial model we've identified. Avalon appears to prefer engineering/integration capability and asset turns rather than owning every manufacturing process. That fits our finding of very high fixed-asset productivity.

5. Customer-controlled program timing is probably one of the most important sources of quarterly forecast error. This is especially relevant to semiconductor and other complex programs. A program can be commercially won and strategically valuable while still missing the quarter in which investors expect revenue.

6. Management appears willing to accept lower reported EBITDA-margin percentages when the underlying business generates attractive absolute profit and return on capital without requiring substantial fixed assets.

# KPIs and Scenario Analysis

Based on everything we've established so far, I would keep the FY27 monitoring framework **very tight**. The objective is to detect whether Avalon is moving toward **positive earnings revisions**, not to monitor every operating metric.

## 5 KPIs to monitor in FY27

| Rank  | KPI                                      |                   FY26 Reference | What I Want to See                                          | Why It Matters                                                                                                            |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------: | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **Revenue growth / guidance trajectory** | FY26 +46%; FY27 guidance ~24–27% | Growth at/above guidance, followed by **guidance upgrades** | Best indication that existing programs + new ramps are running ahead of management's initial trajectory                   |
| **2** | **Incremental EBITDA margin**            |                 ~11.6% FY25→FY26 | **Above consolidated EBITDA margin and preferably rising**  | Tells us whether each incremental ₹1 of revenue is becoming more profitable. Critical for PAT to grow faster than revenue |

## FY27 Bull / Base / Bear framework

I would **not attach arbitrary probabilities yet**. More importantly, I wouldn't define the scenarios primarily by absolute revenue numbers. The distinction should be whether Avalon performs **above, around, or below the trajectory management has communicated**.

|                               | **BULL**                                                                                               | **BASE**                                                                              | **BEAR**                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Revenue**                   | Growth runs **above 24–27% guidance**; management upgrades guidance during FY27                        | Delivers broadly around **24–27% guidance**                                           | Growth falls **below guidance** / guidance cut                              |
| **Incremental EBITDA margin** | **Rises materially above consolidated margin**                                                         | Remains around/slightly above consolidated margin                                     | Falls below consolidated margin                                             |
| **US operations**             | BESS/US ramps strongly; losses decline **faster than planned**; breakeven achieved earlier/comfortably | Losses progressively decline; breakeven around management's **later-FY27** timetable  | US revenue ramp disappoints and losses remain elevated                      |
| **BESS**                      | Strong commercial ramp drives US utilization materially higher                                         | Continues planned ramp                                                                | Ramp slows / customer volumes disappoint                                    |
| **Semiconductor**             | FAI → production approval → **meaningful FY27 commercial revenue**                                     | Qualification progresses; some commercial production, but limited consolidated impact | Qualification/customer approvals slip; FY27 contribution remains negligible |
| **Other new programs**        | One or more programs become **financially material** and add growth above baseline                     | Gradual contribution without changing consolidated trajectory                         | Program timelines slip                                                      |
| **NWC days**                  | **<112 / continued improvement** despite strong growth                                                 | Roughly stable around current levels                                                  | Meaningful deterioration                                                    |
| **CFO/PAT**                   | **Strong improvement** despite growth                                                                  | Gradual improvement                                                                   | Remains weak or deteriorates                                                |
| **ROCE**                      | Continues expanding as margins + capital turns improve                                                 | Around current levels / modest improvement                                            | Declines due to weaker margins and/or working-capital build                 |
| **Earnings outcome**          | **PAT growth materially exceeds revenue growth**                                                       | PAT broadly grows somewhat faster than revenue                                        | PAT growth converges toward or falls below revenue growth                   |
| **Overall interpretation**    | **Positive earnings-revision cycle**                                                                   | Strong execution but broadly expected                                                 | **Earnings expectations need to fall**                                      |

### What actually separates Bull from Base

This is important.

**24–27% revenue growth itself is not the bull case.** That's broadly management's starting trajectory.

For me, the bull case requires at least **two or three incremental developments**:

**1. Core business exceeds guidance**
Existing/vintage programs and order-book execution are stronger than management initially expected.

**2. US operating leverage arrives faster**
BESS/other US volume causes losses to fall faster than the “later FY27” breakeven trajectory.

**3. A new program becomes financially material**
Semiconductor would be the most interesting candidate, but Kavach/aerospace/other programs could also qualify. The key is **commercial revenue**, not FAI/prototype progress.

If those occur while incremental EBITDA margins remain attractive, we get:

> **Revenue expectations ↑ → margin expectations ↑ → PAT expectations ↑ faster still.**

That is the scenario most relevant to the momentum thesis.

### What would make me move toward Bear

I wouldn't become concerned simply because semiconductor takes longer—management has already warned that it is a long-gestation program.

I'd become much more concerned if **several core indicators deteriorate simultaneously**:

> Revenue slips below guidance
>
> * US losses stop narrowing
> * incremental EBITDA margin deteriorates
> * NWC days rise.

That would suggest the problem is no longer timing around one new program; **the underlying economic engine itself is weakening**.

So if you want an extremely compact quarterly Avalon dashboard, I would literally keep just these five numbers at the top of your notes every quarter:

**Revenue growth/guidance | Incremental EBITDA margin | US loss | NWC days | CFO/PAT**

Everything else helps explain **why those five moved**.
