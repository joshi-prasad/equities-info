# Business Model

### Company Business Model

Welspun Living Limited (WLL), part of the Welspun Group, operates an integrated, **vertically integrated "farm-to-shelf" business model** spanning the design, manufacture, marketing, and global distribution of home textiles, flooring solutions, and advanced textiles across more than 60 countries.

* **Operating Structure:** The parent entity (WLL) primarily conducts core manufacturing operations in India and the US, while dedicated subsidiaries handle global marketing, design, and distribution. Specifically, **Welspun Global Brands Limited (WGBL)** manages international marketing, order management, and multi-channel distribution across 50+ countries, and **Welspun USA (WUSA)** provides local sales facilitation, market analytics, warehousing, and supply chain logistics in North America.
* **Channels & Market Approach:**
  * **B2B Program Sourcing:** Welspun partners with leading global retailers (including Walmart, Costco, Target, IKEA, Tesco, Macy’s, and Kohl’s) via multi-year institutional supply programs and cost-plus arrangements.
  * **Branded & Consumer (B2C/D2C):** Sells through an owned portfolio (such as *SPACES*, *Welspun*, *Christy*, *Welhome*) and licensed global labels (such as *Disney Home*, *Wimbledon*, *Martha Stewart*, *Charisma*), distributed across brick-and-mortar retail, franchise-owned (FOFO) stores, and digital/e-commerce platforms.
* **Strategic Pillars:** Operations are built on high backward integration (~70% of yarn and fabric requirements), technological innovation (50 global patents, proprietary lines like *HygroCotton®* and *Wel-Trak® 2.0* blockchain traceability), and sustainability leadership (ranked #1 globally in the S&P Global Corporate Sustainability Assessment for Textiles, Apparels & Luxury Goods).

### Business Verticals, Revenue Share, and Textile Ecosystem (Addressing Questions 1 & 2)

**Business Verticals & Product Offerings:**
Welspun Living operates across three distinct business verticals:
1. **Home Textiles:** Terry towels, bath robes, bath rugs, sheets (flat and fitted), utility bedding, rugs, and carpets.
2. **Flooring Solutions:** Integrated surface coverings, including carpet tiles, broadloom/wall-to-wall carpets, area rugs, artificial turf, and Stone Plastic Composite (SPC) click-and-lock tiles.
3. **Advanced Textiles:** Non-woven fabric technologies (Spunlace, Needlepunch, and Thermal Bond) serving personal care/hygiene, medical, baby care, industrial filtration, and protective apparel.

**Revenue Share by Vertical:**
* **Full-Year FY26 Net Sales Mix:**
  * **Home Textiles (HT):** **87%** of net sales (comprising HT B2B at **70%** / ₹5,881 crore, HT Branded at **14%** / ₹1,158 crore, and E-commerce at **3%** / ₹292 crore).
  * **Flooring Solutions:** **8%** of net sales (Flooring B2B at **6%** / ₹502 crore, Flooring Branded at **2%** / ₹182 crore).
  * **Advanced Textiles:** **5%** of net sales (₹422 crore).
* **Quarterly Trends in FY27:**
  * **Q1 FY27 (Net Sales):** HT B2B **73%** (₹1,852 cr), HT Branded **16%** (₹407 cr), Advanced Textiles **4%** (₹103 cr), Flooring B2B **5%** (₹132 cr), and Flooring Branded **2%** (₹43 cr).
  * **Q2 FY27 (Gross Sales):** HT B2B **67%** (₹1,744 cr), HT Branded **20%** (₹516 cr), Flooring B2B **7%** (₹170 cr), Flooring Branded **2%** (₹52 cr), and Advanced Textiles **4%** (₹104 cr).

**Textile Ecosystem in Which the Company Operates:**
Welspun operates within the **home textiles and home living ecosystem**, specifically dominating the **Bath Solutions** (world’s #1 supplier in towels and bath rugs) and **Sleep Solutions** (top 2 in sheets, scaling into pillows and utility bedding) spaces. Additionally, it participates in the **technical textiles ecosystem** via non-woven industrial and hygiene fabrics, and the **surface coverings/flooring ecosystem**. Within these ecosystems, it operates as a **vertically integrated composite manufacturer** encompassing spinning, weaving, processing, printing, and sewing, integrated directly with retail platforms.

### Perceived Impact of FTAs and Company Positioning (Addressing Question 3)

**Perceived Impact of Various FTAs:**
* **India–UK Free Trade Agreement (CETA):** Eliminates historical **10–12% import tariffs** on Indian home textiles as of July 15, 2026, granting duty-free access to 99% of Indian exports. This places Indian exporters on an equal tariff footing with competing nations such as Pakistan (which historically held over 50% of the UK home textile import market under duty-free status) and Bangladesh. It enables UK retailers to execute multi-year sourcing contracts and shifts trade from contract manufacturing toward design-led ODM models.
* **India–EU FTA Framework:** Negotiated to provide zero-duty access on **99% to 99.5%** of Indian exports, opening a **\$25–30 billion addressable home textiles market**. The zero-duty regime enables scaling of high-margin sustainable lines (recycled polyester/rPET and organic cotton, which command 15–20% higher margins), entry into technical home textiles, and Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) expansion without landed-duty burdens.
* **Active FTA Network & Bilateral Accords:** Comprises active agreements with **Australia, UAE/GCC (under CEPA/IMEC corridor reducing transit times by 30%), New Zealand (effective October 2026)**, and ongoing **India–US Bilateral Trade Agreement (BTA)** discussions. Total addressable home textile market potential across active FTA partner countries exceeds **\$85 billion**.

**Company Positioning to Benefit:**
* **First-Mover Scale & Capacity:** Welspun has vertically integrated infrastructure capable of immediately absorbing diverted volume shifts without extending delivery lead times.
* **Established Regional Footholds:** The company owns heritage UK brand **Christy** (supplier to Wimbledon for nearly 40 years, with retail distribution in Harrods and John Lewis), providing established local brand equity in the UK and European markets.
* **Sustainability Compliance Moat:** Welspun’s #1 global ESG ranking, 100% renewable electricity at its primary plant (Anjar), and tamper-proof *Wel-Trak® 2.0* supply chain traceability satisfy strict European and UK sustainability and supply chain due diligence regulations.

### Group 1: Company Strategy and Capex Focus for Bath Linen Utilization
**Question addressed:** *How the company plans to address the ~83% capacity utilization in Bath Linen, and whether capital expenditure is being focused on the bath linen section.*

* **Debottlenecking and Modernization:** Rather than building a new greenfield bath linen plant, the company is addressing capacity and throughput constraints primarily through brownfield debottlenecking, automation, and technology replacement. In July 2026, the Board approved a **₹121 crore debottlenecking and modernization project at the Anjar facility** to replace older technology with advanced machinery, improve plant utilization, and meet evolving demand.
* **Network Flexibility and Ancillary Units:** During operational constraints—such as the temporary flooding disruptions at the Vapi towel facility—the company maintained bath linen output and high utilization by shifting production to Anjar, leveraging external outsourcing, and utilizing capacity from ancillary units and investments in Welspun Home Solutions Limited (WHSL).
* **Broader Capex Deployment:** Out of the total planned **₹400 crore to ₹500 crore annual FY27 capex** (of which ~₹150 crore was spent in H1 FY27 and ~₹350 crore is allocated for H2 FY27), the remaining deployment is explicitly focused on debottlenecking operations to increase production output across manufacturing facilities. Management has also noted they are evaluating additional investment opportunities beyond the current capex budget to capture incremental market demand.

### Group 7: Company Vertical Integration Across the Textile Value Chain and First-Principles Breakdown (Addressing Questions 2 & 3)

**Welspun's Vertical Integration & Value Chain Coverage:**
Welspun Living operates an integrated "farm-to-shelf" model, with backward integration covering **~70% of its yarn and fabric requirements**. Its specific presence across the value chain includes:
* **Cotton (Raw Material):** The company does not own cotton farms, but procures cotton through structured buying covering 4–5 months of inventory, with 95% sourced from sustainable programs (BCI, organic). It also operates a 4–5 tonnes/day in-house cotton fibre recycling plant at Anjar.
* **Spinning (Yarn):** Operates internal spinning units that convert raw cotton and fibres into cotton yarn.
* **Weaving (Fabric):** Houses weaving looms for terry towel and bed linen production, as well as non-woven fabric bonding lines (Spunlace, Needlepunch, Thermal Bond).
* **Coloring & Finishing (Processing/Dyeing/Printing):** Operates in-house dyeing, printing, bleaching, washing, and coating lines.
* **Cutting & Sewing (Fabric to Finished Goods):** Manages integrated conversion, cutting, stitching, and finishing of towels, bed sheets, and utility bedding.
* **Logistics & Distribution:** Controls 11 global warehouses and dedicated distribution/marketing subsidiaries across 60+ countries.

**Textile Value Chain from First Principles:**
1. **Raw Fibre:** Sourcing the fundamental raw material—either natural polymers (raw agricultural cotton) or synthetic polymers (polyester/nylon).
2. **Yarn Manufacturing (Spinning):** Cleaning, carding, drawing, and twisting individual loose fibres together to form continuous, tensile strands of yarn.
3. **Fabric Construction (Weaving / Knitting / Bonding):** Interlacing yarns orthogonally (warp and weft weaving) to construct woven sheets and terry loops, or entangling loose fibres mechanically/chemically (non-woven bonding).
4. **Coloring & Chemical Finishing (Processing):** Applying dyes, pigment prints, washing treatments, and chemical agents to alter colour, hand-feel, absorbency, and durability.
5. **Conversion (Cutting & Sewing):** Measuring, cutting, edge-stitching, hemming, and assembling finished fabric into final end-user dimensions (towels, sheets, robes).
6. **Supply Chain & Merchandising:** Packaging, inventory warehousing, logistics routing, and retail fulfillment to deliver goods to institutional shelves or end consumers.

# Management's Mental Model and Capital Allocation

### Group 1: Long-Term Vision, Strategic Priorities, and Growth Philosophy
**Questions addressed:** *Envisioning the company 5–10 years forward (businesses, products, geographies, and transformation drivers) and management's growth philosophy across verticals, resource allocation, and areas chosen not to pursue (Questions 1 & 3).*

* **Medium-Term Scale & Vision:** Management's stated medium-term aspiration is to build a **₹15,000 crore revenue enterprise**. This evolution—termed **"Welspun 2.0"**—is structured around six strategic value drivers: regional expansion, brands, emerging businesses, innovation, digitisation, and ESG.
* **Portfolio & Geographic Transformation Targets:**
  * **Geographic Mix:** Targeting **50%+ of revenues from non-US markets** (compared to ~41% currently) by expanding in the UK and EU (capitalizing on FTAs), Middle East, Australia/New Zealand, Japan, and domestic India.
  * **Product & Category Leadership:** Transitioning from being primarily a bath supplier to dominating both **Bath Solutions** (#1 globally) and the sequential **Sleep ecosystem** (sheets, utility bedding, and pillows).
  * **Emerging Businesses:** Targeting **~30% of total revenue** from emerging businesses, specifically **Flooring Solutions** and **Advanced Textiles**.
  * **Branded & Consumer Mix:** Targeting sustained **double-digit revenue growth** in branded businesses, expanding domestic labels (*SPACES*, *Welspun*) and global labels (*Christy*, *Welhome*, licensed lines).
  * **Nearshore Expansion:** Scaling the US pillow manufacturing footprint (Ohio and Nevada facilities) to double annual pillow revenue to **\$60 million**.
* **Resource Allocation & Business Choices:**
  * **Prioritization:** Capital and management attention are directed toward margin-accretive, differentiated segments: innovation-led patented products (targeting >25% of sales), branded omni-channel retail, and certified sustainable product lines.
  * **Deliberately Avoided / Deprioritized:** Management is pivoting away from **undifferentiated commodity contract manufacturing**, which is subject to high tariff volatility and margin compression. In manufacturing assets, the company is choosing **in-situ debottlenecking over large greenfield domestic expansions**.

### Group 3: Capital Allocation Framework, Investment Hurdles, and M&A Philosophy
**Questions addressed:** *How management prioritizes capital allocation across expansion, debottlenecking, automation, acquisitions, debt reduction, and shareholder returns, alongside investment criteria and M&A philosophy (Questions 4 & 5).*

* **Capital Allocation Hierarchy:**
  1. **Debottlenecking, Modernization & Automation:** Priority is given to high-throughput modernization of existing domestic lines (e.g., the Board-approved **₹121 crore Anjar project**) to replace older machinery and raise utilization rather than building new domestic plants.
  2. **Strategic Customer Proximity Capex:** Deploying targeted nearshore capacity, such as the Ohio and Nevada automated pillow facilities.
  3. **Debt Reduction & Balance Sheet Resilience:** Prioritized debt elimination to shift from net debt of ₹775.40 crore (March 31, 2026) to a **net cash positive balance sheet** by H1 FY27.
  4. **Shareholder Distributions:** Surplus cash is distributed via systematic share buybacks and dividends.
* **Investment Criteria & Financial Hurdles:** Management states that projects must enhance operational efficiency, asset turnover, and support return on capital. However, **specific numerical hurdles (such as exact internal rate of return [IRR] or payback period cutoffs) are not disclosed** in the company's reporting.
* **Organic Growth vs. Acquisitions Philosophy:**
  * **Organic & Nearshore Focus:** Current domestic and nearshore expansions are executed organically (e.g., greenfield Nevada pillow plant, Anjar brownfield lines).
  * **Selective Inorganic Additions:** Historical acquisitions have been deployed selectively to acquire international brand equity and heritage (e.g., *Christy* in 2006). Management indicates that any future expansion is evaluated based on strategic alignment and market demand, though **formal acquisition valuation criteria and integration scorecards are not published** in available reports.

### Group 4: Return on Capital, Cash Flow Discipline, Shareholder Returns, and Capital Allocation Track Record
**Questions addressed:** *Long-term targets for ROCE, ROE, margins, and free cash flows; reinvestment vs. dividend/buyback decisions; and the 5–10 year capital allocation track record and accountability (Questions 6, 7 & 8).*

* **Long-Term Return & Margin Targets:**
  * **EBITDA Margins:** Management guided to **low-teens EBITDA margins in the near term**, a **15%+ normalized target within 2 years**, and an aspirational **20% EBITDA on its vision board**.
  * **ROCE:** Management set a target of achieving **low-teens ROCE in FY27**, achieving **12.7% pre-tax ROCE in Q2 FY27** ahead of schedule, with a long-term goal of sustaining **14% to 15%**.
  * **Cash Conversion:** Working capital cash conversion cycle was reduced from 82 days (March 2026) to **44 days in Q2 FY27** (with a guided sustainable steady-state range of **55–60 days**).
* **Reinvestment vs. Shareholder Returns Policy:**
  * **Reinvestment Scope:** Annual capex is guided in the range of **₹400 crore to ₹500 crore for FY27** (~₹150 crore spent in H1 FY27, ~₹350 crore allocated for H2 FY27), funded predominantly out of internal cash accruals.
  * **Capital Return Mechanisms:** The company returns surplus cash via dual channels:
    * **Share Buybacks:** ₹252 crore tender offer buyback approved in May 2026 at ₹175/share; ₹278.43 crore buyback executed in August 2024 at ₹220/share; earlier buybacks completed in May 2023 and July 2021.
    * **Dividends:** Dividend Distribution Policy balances profit retention with distributions. Approved 10% dividend (₹0.10/share; ₹9.44–₹9.59 crore outflow) for FY26 compared to 170% dividend (₹1.70/share; ₹162.46–₹163.06 crore outflow) for FY25.
* **Historical Track Record & Accountability:**
  * **Return Fluctuations:** Over FY21–FY26, pre-tax ROCE fluctuated between **14.6% (FY24)** and a low of **5.6%–5.7% (FY23 and FY26)**, and ROE ranged from **16.3% (FY21)** to **4.2% (FY26)** due to external tariff volatility, container freight spikes, and commodity cycle disruptions.
  * **Solvency Discipline:** Net Debt-to-Equity steadily decreased from **0.64 in FY21** to **0.16 in FY26**, culminating in a **net cash position in H1 FY27**.
  * **Project-Level Disclosures:** While management measures success through aggregate balance sheet de-leveraging, working capital compression, and overall ROCE recovery, **it does not publish individual project-by-project historical IRR comparisons or post-mortem ROI rankings for past investments**.

# Growth Triggers

The major structural growth triggers capable of driving significant improvements in Welspun Living’s sales and earnings include:

### 1. Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) & Tariff Removal (UK & EU)
* **Equal Footing in the UK:** The India–UK FTA (effective July 15, 2026) eliminates **10%–12% import tariffs**, placing Indian exporters on a zero-duty footing with Pakistan, which historically held over 50% of the UK import market. Management expects this to unlock multi-year supply contracts and sustain **20%+ top-line growth** across the UK and Europe.
* **India–EU FTA Market Access:** Negotiated zero-duty access on **99%–99.5% of exports** opens an addressable European home textiles market worth **\$25–\$30 billion**. The zero-duty regime also enables higher adoption of premium sustainable product lines (such as recycled polyester and organic cotton), which command **15%–20% higher margins**.
* **Broad Active Network:** Across active trade agreements (including Australia, UAE/GCC, and New Zealand), the addressable market exceeds **\$85 billion**.

### 2. Rapid Scaling of US Onshore Pillow Manufacturing
* **Capacity & Tariff-Shielded Nearshoring:** The ramp-up of the automated facility in **Ohio** (13.5M pcs capacity) and the commencement of commercial production at the greenfield facility in **Nevada** (10.8M pcs capacity) provide domestic US supply assurance and fast replenishment.
* **Sales & Margin Expansion:** The US pillow business grew **2.3x–2.4x year-on-year** in H1 FY27, is on track to double revenue to **\$60 million in FY27**, and is guided to scale to **\$80–\$100 million by FY29** with **lower-teen EBITDA margins**.

### 3. Turnaround and Capacity Headroom in Emerging Businesses
* **Flooring Solutions Margin Expansion:** Following portfolio restructuring, Flooring EBITDA margins expanded from a 3.2% trough in Q4 FY26 to **10.8% in Q2 FY27**. With capacity utilization currently at **51%**, volume ramp-up in commercial flooring and geographical expansion (Australia, Canada) offer substantial operating leverage toward reaching an **₹800–₹1,000 crore revenue target**.
* **Advanced Textiles Rebound:** Positioned to overcome short-term commodity headwinds to achieve **double-digit top-line growth** and a target **20% EBITDA margin** on the company's vision board.
* **Portfolio Mix:** Together, emerging businesses are targeted to generate **~30% of consolidated group revenues**.

### 4. Premiumisation via Patented Innovation and Owned Brands
* **Patented Offerings:** An intellectual property portfolio of **50 global patents** (such as *HygroCotton®*) contributes **~25% of revenues**, generating pricing power and customer stickiness.
* **Branded Business Scaling:** High-margin branded businesses (representing ~22% of revenue) are targeted to sustain **double-digit growth**, driven by domestic labels (*SPACES*, *Welspun*) expanding into quick commerce and global labels (*Christy*, *Welhome*) expanding in department stores and e-commerce.

### 5. High-Growth Domestic B2C Market
* **Rapid Retail & E-commerce Expansion:** Benefiting from India's consumer formalization and urban housing demand, Welspun's domestic business grew **28.2% YoY in Q2 FY27**, supported by expansion across modern retail, quick commerce, and festive/gifting lines.

### 6. Operating Leverage, Low-Capex Debottlenecking, and Balance Sheet De-leveraging
* **Shift to Net Cash:** Transitioning from net debt of ₹775.40 crore (March 2026) to a **net cash positive balance sheet** in H1 FY27 materially reduces financing costs and improves cash flow.
* **Throughput Improvements:** Utilizing an approved **₹121 crore brownfield modernization at Anjar** boosts plant throughput and asset turnover without heavy greenfield capital expenditure.
* **Margin Expansion Runway:** Driven by operating leverage on fixed costs, working capital compression (cash conversion cycle dropped to 44 days), and tariff-share recovery, EBITDA margins have expanded from a 6.8% trough to **12.9% in Q2 FY27**, with management targeting **15%+ EBITDA** within two years.
