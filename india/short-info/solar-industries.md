# Solar Industries India Ltd. – Investment Summary

---

# Company Snapshot

Solar Industries is evolving from a traditional industrial explosives manufacturer into an **integrated energetic materials and defence systems platform** serving mining, infrastructure, domestic defence and global defence markets.

Unlike conventional explosives companies that primarily manufacture bulk blasting materials, Solar increasingly competes through:

- proprietary energetic materials
- deep backward integration
- defence platform integration
- long qualification cycles
- regulatory barriers
- localized global manufacturing
- execution capability
- international defence commercialization

The business today operates through four distinct economic engines.

| Business | Economic Role |
|----------|---------------|
| Domestic Industrial Explosives | Legacy cash generation + customer relationships |
| International Industrial Explosives | Stable growth engine + diversification + high cash generation |
| Domestic Defence | Margin expansion + technology platform + long-term value creation |
| Export Defence | High-growth earnings engine + capital-efficient commercialization |

The company should not be viewed simply as an explosives manufacturer.

Instead, Solar is becoming:

**An integrated energetic materials platform that is moving steadily up the defence value chain by converting proprietary explosives chemistry into complete ammunition, rocket systems, loitering munitions and future integrated weapon platforms.**

---

# Core Investment Thesis

The investment thesis is **NOT** higher coal production.

Nor is it simply about India's defence spending.

The current opportunity is driven by:

- rapid scaling of the defence business
- commercialization of integrated weapon systems
- expansion of export defence
- platform integration
- superior product mix
- localization across international mining markets
- operating leverage from previously built defence infrastructure
- monetization of proprietary energetic materials

Over the last several years Solar invested heavily in:

- energetic materials
- propellant manufacturing
- defence R&D
- integrated manufacturing facilities
- international manufacturing footprint
- qualification programs
- defence platform development
- capacity expansion

The business is now transitioning from:

**Industrial Explosives → Integrated Defence & Energetic Materials Platform**

The key question is no longer:

**"Can Solar manufacture explosives?"**

The key question is:

**"Can Solar efficiently convert its rapidly expanding defence order book into revenue, operating cash flow and sustained returns while successfully commercializing increasingly complex integrated defence platforms?"**

# Business Model Summary

## 1. Domestic Industrial Explosives (Legacy Cash Engine)

The historical foundation of Solar.

Products include:

- Bulk explosives
- Cartridge explosives
- Detamators
- Initiating systems
- Mining services

Major customers include:

- Coal India
- Singareni
- Private miners
- Infrastructure companies

Key advantages:

- nationwide manufacturing footprint
- long customer relationships
- logistics network
- regulatory licenses
- recurring demand

Strategic role today:

- generates operating cash flow
- funds defence investments
- maintains customer relationships
- provides manufacturing scale

Management is intentionally reducing dependence on this business as defence becomes a larger contributor.

---

## 2. International Industrial Explosives (Growth & Diversification Engine)

This has quietly become one of the highest-quality businesses inside Solar.

Operations span manufacturing facilities across multiple mining jurisdictions including:

- Africa
- Australia
- Southeast Asia
- Türkiye

Unlike domestic mining, international operations benefit from:

- better pricing
- localized manufacturing
- reduced logistics costs
- lower dependence on Indian monsoons
- diversified commodity exposure

This business provides:

- stable cash generation
- geographic diversification
- superior margins versus domestic mining
- structural growth

Importantly, international operations increasingly stabilize consolidated earnings whenever domestic mining volumes weaken.

---

## 3. Domestic Defence (Technology & Margin Engine)

This is the most strategically important business inside Solar.

Products include:

- Pinaka rockets
- Artillery ammunition
- Loitering munitions
- Warheads
- Propellants
- High-energy materials
- Counter-drone systems

Unlike industrial explosives, this business competes through:

- qualification
- engineering capability
- energetic material expertise
- integrated manufacturing
- proprietary know-how

The segment generates:

- significantly higher margins
- strong pricing power
- long-duration order visibility
- technological leadership

However, it also consumes enormous amounts of:

- capital
- inventory
- working capital
- engineering resources

Today's weak cash conversion is largely the result of deliberately scaling this business.

---

## 4. Export Defence (Hidden Earnings Engine)

This may become Solar's most important earnings driver over the next several years.

International defence orders now represent a substantial portion of the overall defence order book.

Compared with domestic defence, export defence offers:

- faster commercialization
- shorter execution cycles
- better pricing
- reduced procurement delays
- attractive capital efficiency

Importantly, Solar leverages the same energetic-material capabilities and manufacturing infrastructure already developed for Indian defence programs.

Rather than building entirely new capabilities, management is increasingly monetizing existing technology across global defence customers.

This creates a powerful combination of:

**Higher Margins + Faster Revenue Conversion + Better Capital Productivity**

---

## 5. Energetic Materials Platform (The Hidden Business Model)

This is arguably Solar's most valuable business.

Solar is steadily evolving through the following technology ladder:

```
Industrial Explosives

↓

Energetic Materials

↓

Warheads & Propellants

↓

Integrated Ammunition

↓

Rocket Systems

↓

Loitering Munitions

↓

Complete Weapon Systems
```

The company's true competitive advantage is not assembling rockets or drones.

It is owning the proprietary chemistry and manufacturing capability behind:

- HMX
- propellants
- energetic materials
- warheads
- explosive payloads

These capabilities can then be reused across multiple defence platforms.

Increasingly, Solar creates value by commercializing energetic-material expertise across an expanding portfolio rather than developing entirely unrelated products.

# Why The Moat Exists

The moat is **not** explosives manufacturing.

The moat is:

**Energetic Materials + Qualification + Backward Integration + Platform Integration + Regulatory Barriers**

Key evidence:

- proprietary energetic materials
- integrated propellant manufacturing
- deep backward integration
- decades of explosives expertise
- difficult regulatory approvals
- long defence qualification cycles
- integrated defence manufacturing
- localized international manufacturing
- 40+ manufacturing facilities globally

The strongest competitive advantage lies in:

**Controlling the energetic materials that power increasingly sophisticated defence platforms.**

Manufacturing facilities can eventually be replicated.

Energetic-material expertise, defence qualifications and regulatory approvals cannot.

# Financial Inflection

FY26 appears to represent Solar's largest strategic inflection.

Major developments include:

- Defence revenue nearly doubled
- Defence order book expanded dramatically
- Export defence accelerated
- International business achieved record performance
- EBITDA margins expanded materially
- Large defence capex continued
- Platform integration accelerated
- Working capital increased sharply
- Operating cash flow weakened despite strong earnings

The most important development is:

**Solar has successfully built the manufacturing capabilities, qualifications and order book required for the next phase of defence commercialization.**

However, the next phase now depends on:

**Qualification → Manufacturing → Execution → Cash Conversion**

rather than

**Capacity Expansion → Order Book Growth**

Management has already invested heavily in:

- defence infrastructure
- integrated manufacturing
- energetic materials
- platform development
- international manufacturing

The investment thesis now depends primarily on:

**Execution, commercialization and normalization of cash conversion rather than additional capacity creation.**

# Bull / Base / Bear Framework

## Bull Case

- Export defence scales faster than expected
- FY27 defence guidance is achieved or exceeded
- Pinaka execution accelerates
- 155mm artillery shells receive commercial approval
- Export order book converts rapidly into revenue
- Working capital normalizes as deliveries increase
- EBITDA margins remain above historical averages
- New platforms (Bhargavastra, future missile programs, UAVs) begin commercial contribution
- ROCE remains structurally high despite heavy capex

---

## Base Case

- Defence grows broadly in line with management guidance
- Export defence becomes the primary growth engine
- International industrial explosives continue double-digit growth
- Domestic mining remains stable despite seasonal fluctuations
- Working capital remains elevated but gradually improves
- Cash conversion slowly catches up with earnings
- Margin expansion is sustained through product mix improvement
- Platform integration continues steadily

---

## Bear Case

- Defence qualification timelines slip
- Export defence execution slows
- Government procurement is delayed
- Inventory continues building faster than revenue
- Operating cash flow remains weak
- Working capital consumes incremental earnings
- Large capex generates slower-than-expected revenue
- Defence margins fail to offset weaker industrial explosives performance

---

# Key Risks

- Persistent working-capital intensity
- Delays in defence qualification
- Inventory remaining structurally elevated
- Execution delays across large defence programs
- Government procurement delays
- Slower export defence conversion
- Heavy capex reducing capital efficiency
- Monsoon-driven slowdown in domestic mining
- Regulatory delays for new defence products

The biggest near-term risk is:

**Rapid earnings growth failing to convert into operating cash flow because inventory and working capital continue expanding faster than deliveries.**

# Final Investment View

Solar Industries appears to be transitioning from:

- an industrial explosives manufacturer,
- mining-services company,
- chemical explosives business

toward

- an integrated energetic materials platform,
- defence technology company,
- global ammunition manufacturer,
- complete weapon systems supplier.

The legacy industrial explosives business continues to provide:

- recurring cash generation
- manufacturing scale
- customer relationships
- international diversification

while future value creation increasingly depends on:

- Export Defence
- Domestic Defence
- Energetic Materials
- Platform Integration
- Qualification-to-Commercialization conversion
- Higher-value integrated weapon systems

Importantly, Solar should not be viewed simply as a beneficiary of higher defence spending.

The real strategic advantage lies in its ownership of the **energetic materials layer**, which enables the company to progressively move up the defence value chain from explosives to integrated weapon platforms.

Management is no longer attempting to grow simply by selling larger volumes of industrial explosives.

Instead, it is attempting to create substantially higher economic value by:

- manufacturing proprietary energetic materials,
- integrating those materials into ammunition and rockets,
- commercializing increasingly complex defence platforms,
- expanding export defence,
- and leveraging a globally distributed manufacturing network.

The current investment debate is therefore no longer:

**"Can Solar grow its explosives business?"**

The key question is:

**"Can Solar efficiently monetize its integrated defence platform by converting its rapidly expanding order book into sustainable revenue, operating cash flow and long-term returns while maintaining leadership in energetic materials and defence manufacturing?"**

If management succeeds, Solar could gradually evolve from:

```
Industrial Explosives

↓

Energetic Materials

↓

Integrated Ammunition

↓

Rocket & Drone Systems

↓

Global Defence Manufacturing Platform
```

The company's long-term competitive advantage is unlikely to come from explosive volumes alone.

It is increasingly being built on:

- proprietary energetic materials,
- deep backward integration,
- difficult defence qualifications,
- integrated manufacturing,
- export commercialization,
- and the ability to repeatedly move higher up the defence value chain while preserving attractive returns on capital.

Ultimately, the investment thesis should be monitored through one simple framework:

**Energetic Materials → Qualification → Platform Integration → Execution → Cash Conversion → Reinvestment**

As long as this cycle continues strengthening, Solar has the potential to evolve from India's leading explosives manufacturer into one of the country's most strategically important private defence platforms.
