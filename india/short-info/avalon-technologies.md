# Avalon Technologies
---
# 1. Business Model

## 1.1 The Core Business Model

Avalon Technologies is best understood not as a seller of electronic products, but as a **seller of manufacturing capability**. The Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) owns the end-market opportunity, product architecture and generally the underlying intellectual property. Avalon embeds itself into the process of converting that intellectual property into a physical, manufacturable product—initially through engineering, prototyping or individual components and, over time, potentially through complete system integration or **Box Builds**.

This distinction is fundamental. Avalon does not need to create consumer brands, invent end-products or generate demand for those products. Its role is to become an increasingly important manufacturing partner to OEMs whose products require complex, reliable and often highly customized electronic and electromechanical assemblies.

The business can therefore be reduced to four economic variables:

`Program volume × Avalon content per program × manufacturing value-add × efficiency of capital/cash employed`

Growth does not depend solely on selling more units. Avalon has **three independent dimensions through which revenue can expand**:

`Higher volumes within existing programs × more programs/customers × more manufacturing content captured per program`

The third dimension—capturing more manufacturing content—is particularly important. Avalon can enter a customer relationship by manufacturing one component and progressively expand into several sub-assemblies and eventually the complete system. Consequently, revenue per customer can increase even without a corresponding increase in the customer's end-product volumes.

This creates Avalon's central business flywheel:

`Customer/program win → qualification → production → repeat orders → deeper manufacturing content → larger wallet share → stronger customer integration → additional programs`

The attractiveness of the model comes from the interaction of this flywheel with long program lives, difficult qualification processes, vertical integration and Avalon's India-US manufacturing footprint.

## 1.2 What Avalon Actually Manufactures

Avalon is a vertically integrated Electronic Manufacturing Services (EMS) provider specializing in high-mix, relatively complex electronic and electromechanical systems.

A finished electronic system generally consists of several distinct manufacturing disciplines:

| Capability                                | Function                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Printed Circuit Board Assembly (PCBA)** | Mounting electronic components onto bare PCBs using Surface Mount Technology (SMT) and Through-Hole Technology (THT) |
| **Cables & Wire Harnesses**               | Carrying electrical power and signals between modules                                                                |
| **Magnetics**                             | Custom transformers, inductors and coils used in power conversion                                                    |
| **Sheet Metal & Precision Machining**     | Enclosures, chassis and structural components                                                                        |
| **Injection-Molded Plastics**             | Customized structural and enclosure components                                                                       |
| **Box Build / System Integration**        | Combining multiple sub-assemblies into a finished system and performing final testing                                |

Avalon does **not** manufacture fundamental electronic inputs such as semiconductors, processors, resistors or bare multilayer PCBs. These are procured from global component manufacturers and distributors.

Its value addition begins when these inputs must be converted into customized assemblies. Avalon assembles PCBAs, manufactures wire harnesses, winds magnetics, fabricates sheet-metal structures, produces plastic components and integrates these into larger systems.

The distinction matters economically. Avalon avoids the highly capital-intensive fabrication of basic electronic components while retaining many of the manufacturing processes where customization, engineering and integration create value.

### From PCBA to Box Build

At the lower end of the value chain, Avalon may supply a customer with only an individual component or sub-assembly—for example, a PCBA, wire harness or sheet-metal enclosure.

At the upper end is the **Box Build**.

A Box Build combines several separate sub-assemblies into a complete electromechanical system. Multiple PCBAs, magnetics, cables and harnesses are installed inside custom sheet-metal or plastic structures along with interfaces, cooling systems and other components. Avalon then performs system-level functional testing before shipping the finished assembly.

Box Builds accounted for approximately **56% of revenue by Q4 FY26**, up from roughly 44% historically.

Their importance extends beyond reported revenue mix. A Box Build allows Avalon to capture manufacturing value across several processes simultaneously and gives it responsibility for the functioning of the complete system rather than an isolated component.

The source material indicates that Box Builds are structurally more attractive than standalone component assembly because of greater integration complexity, vertical integration and customer stickiness. However, **Avalon does not disclose the precise margin differential between Box Builds and individual component assemblies**. The economic superiority of Box Builds should therefore be understood primarily through higher manufacturing content, gross-profit dollars, wallet share and customer integration rather than assuming a specific undisclosed margin premium.

## 1.3 Why the OEM Outsources to Avalon

The OEM retains the activities that differentiate its product: product conception, core intellectual property, high-level R&D, branding, customer relationships and end-market distribution.

Avalon performs the manufacturing-intensive activities required to turn that design into a reliable physical product.

| OEM Retains                     | Avalon Provides                          |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Product concept and core IP     | Design for Manufacturability (DFM)       |
| High-level product architecture | Prototyping and New Product Introduction |
| Core R&D and firmware           | Component sourcing and procurement       |
| Brand and distribution          | PCBA and electromechanical manufacturing |
| End-customer relationship       | Box-Build system integration             |
| Product market strategy         | Functional testing and quality assurance |

The customer therefore avoids having to manage separate suppliers for electronics, cables, magnetics, metal structures, plastics and final assembly.

This addresses an important coordination problem. If multiple vendors independently manufacture each part of a complex system, the OEM must coordinate specifications, delivery schedules, quality standards and engineering changes across the supplier base. Problems can create disputes over which component or supplier caused a failure.

Avalon's **one-stop-shop model internalizes much of this coordination**.

The customer provides the product architecture; Avalon applies Design for Manufacturability to make the design easier, cheaper and more reliable to manufacture at scale. Its engineers can optimize physical routing, thermal management, structural design and production processes without taking ownership of the OEM's underlying product IP.

Vertical integration therefore serves two purposes simultaneously:

1. **Economic:** Avalon captures manufacturing value that would otherwise accrue to several suppliers.
2. **Strategic:** The OEM has fewer manufacturing interfaces to manage and becomes more deeply integrated with Avalon.

## 1.4 The Commodity-to-Box Strategy: Growing Wallet Share

A defining feature of Avalon's business model is that customer acquisition does not necessarily begin with a complete Box Build.

The company can use an individual manufacturing capability as a **land-and-expand entry point**.

The progression typically resembles:

`Single component → multiple components → sub-system → complete Box Build`

For example, Avalon may initially qualify as a supplier of wire harnesses. Once manufacturing quality and delivery reliability have been demonstrated, it can bid for the customer's PCB assembly or metal fabrication. Over time, Avalon can combine these capabilities and offer responsibility for the complete integrated system.

The source material characterizes this as a **"Commodity-to-Box" model** and indicates that the progression can occur over a two-to-three-year period. Moving from individual commodities to a complete Box Build can potentially increase Avalon's wallet share in a customer program by several multiples.

This is important because it creates an internal source of growth that is partially independent of end-market growth.

Consider an OEM producing the same number of machines in two consecutive years. Avalon can still grow substantially if its content moves from one cable assembly in year one to the cable assembly, PCBA, magnetics, enclosure and final integration in subsequent years.

The most useful way to think about customer economics is therefore not simply **"How fast is the customer's market growing?"**, but also **"How much of the customer's manufacturing wallet is Avalon capturing?"**

---

## 1.5 How a Customer Program Develops

Avalon's revenues are organized around **programs rather than transactional product sales**.

A simplified program lifecycle is:

`Customer engagement → RFQ → DFM → prototype/NPI → qualification → commercial PO → material procurement → manufacturing → Box Build → testing → shipment → repeat production`

The initial Request for Quotation establishes the Bill of Materials and manufacturing requirements. Avalon then works with the customer on manufacturability and prototypes the product.

The qualification stage is particularly important. Customers—especially those in aerospace, defense, railways, medical and other high-reliability applications—must validate the manufacturing process, quality systems and production lines before allowing commercial volumes.

This process can take **roughly 15–36 months** for complex programs.

Once qualification is completed, Avalon procures materials, manufactures the relevant components, integrates the system, performs functional testing and ships the finished product. Revenue is recognized when the relevant risk transfers to the customer.

The same qualified program can subsequently generate recurring purchase orders over a product lifecycle that can extend for **5–7 years and, in certain industries, considerably longer**.

This produces a different revenue profile from project businesses in which an order is won, executed and permanently disappears. Avalon continuously executes purchase orders against longer-lived customer programs while simultaneously winning additional programs and increasing its content within existing relationships.

---

## 1.6 Order Book Economics: Visibility, Not Lifetime Contract Value

Avalon's reported order book should be interpreted as a **rolling window of executable purchase orders**, rather than the lifetime economic value of its customer relationships.

At March 2026, the near-term executable order book stood at approximately **₹2,196 crore** and represented firm purchase orders scheduled broadly over the following 12–14 months. Separately, long-term contracts stood at approximately **₹1,245 crore**.

This distinction explains why order-book growth can materially trail revenue growth without necessarily indicating deteriorating customer relationships.

FY26 revenue increased **46%**, while the near-term order book increased approximately **24.7%**. As production ramps, Avalon is simultaneously receiving new orders and rapidly consuming existing orders through shipments. The reported order book is therefore the **net result of replenishment minus execution**, not simply gross order wins.

For an investor, this creates a replenishment treadmill:

`Opening backlog + new PO intake − revenue executed = closing backlog`

As Avalon's revenue base increases, absolute new order intake must also increase merely to maintain the same level of forward visibility.

The deeper economic asset is consequently not the reported order-book number alone. It is the underlying base of **qualified, long-duration programs capable of repeatedly generating new purchase orders**.

---

## 1.7 Why Customer Relationships Become Sticky

Avalon's customer relationships can become unusually durable because changing an EMS supplier for a complex program is not equivalent to changing a commodity vendor.

Three mechanisms create stickiness.

### 1. Qualification and certification

Complex programs require customers to qualify manufacturing lines, processes and quality systems. In aerospace, defense, railways and medical applications, certifications and audits create additional barriers.

A new supplier may therefore require extensive requalification before commercial production can move.

### 2. Engineering and manufacturing co-dependency

As Avalon progresses from a single component toward a Box Build, it becomes involved in more of the customer's manufacturing architecture.

A supplier making one cable is relatively replaceable. A supplier simultaneously responsible for PCBAs, cables, magnetics, metal fabrication, system integration and functional testing is considerably harder to replace.

### 3. Switching risk

Certain complex assemblies require **200–300 hours of manufacturing labor**. Moving such a program can require transferring tooling, manufacturing knowledge, process controls and quality approvals while maintaining uninterrupted production.

The source material estimates that supplier transitions can require approximately **12–18 months or longer**, depending on the program.

The resulting moat is not based primarily on proprietary product technology. It comes from **qualification, accumulated manufacturing know-how, integration complexity and the operational risk customers incur by switching**.

This also explains the flywheel: greater wallet share creates greater integration, which increases stickiness, which improves Avalon's probability of winning additional programs.

## 1.8 The Dual-Shore Model: Why Avalon Manufactures in Both India and the US

Avalon's India-US footprint is one of the most important elements of its business model.

The simplistic interpretation is:

`US = prototyping → India = manufacturing`

The actual model is more nuanced.

### United States: customer proximity and localized manufacturing

The Fremont and Atlanta facilities provide proximity to US OEM engineering teams. They support rapid prototyping, New Product Introduction and pilot production, allowing customer engineers to work closely with Avalon during the difficult early stages of a program.

However, the US operations are **not merely prototype laboratories**. They also manufacture commercially where localization is required because of customer preferences, regulatory considerations, supply-chain security or tariffs.

### India: scale and cost-efficient production

Once a program is qualified and economics permit, higher-volume manufacturing can be performed through Avalon's Indian facilities in Chennai and Bengaluru.

India benefits from substantially lower manufacturing labor costs and houses Avalon's vertically integrated production capabilities. India consequently represents the primary profit engine of the group.

The strategic advantage is therefore **manufacturing optionality**.

Avalon can offer customers:

`US manufacturing | India manufacturing | US-to-India hybrid manufacturing`

Rather than forcing every customer into the lowest-cost location, Avalon can optimize the manufacturing footprint according to the customer's regulatory, tariff, localization, lead-time and cost requirements.

This is particularly valuable when customers are attempting to redesign global supply chains. Avalon can participate whether production needs to remain localized in the US or can migrate toward lower-cost Indian manufacturing.

The dual-shore footprint therefore functions both as a **customer-acquisition mechanism and a manufacturing optimization mechanism**.

## 1.9 Revenue Architecture

Avalon's business can be viewed through three overlapping lenses: manufacturing capability, geography and end-market exposure.

| Lens                        | Approximate FY26/Q4 Position                                                                |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Capability**              | Box Build ~56%; component/sub-assembly ~44%                                                 |
| **Manufacturing Geography** | India ~79%; US ~21%                                                                         |
| **Major Verticals**         | Industrial, Clean Energy, Railways/Mobility, Aerospace & Defense, Communications and others |

The vertical diversification is economically important because these markets have different demand cycles, qualification requirements and program lives.

Industrial represented the largest revenue pool, while Clean Energy, Railways, Aerospace & Defense and Communications provide additional growth avenues. High-reliability sectors such as aerospace and railways generally carry longer qualification periods but also create greater barriers to switching once a program enters commercial production.

Aerospace also illustrates why Avalon's manufacturing capabilities extend beyond what might superficially look like "electronics." Precision metal chassis, cockpit structures and related components can provide mechanical protection, thermal management and electromagnetic shielding for electronic systems. Such capabilities therefore form part of the complete electromechanical manufacturing proposition rather than representing an unrelated diversification.

## 1.10 Manufacturing Footprint, Capacity and Scalability

Avalon operates approximately **15 manufacturing operations**, with more than **575,000 sq. ft.** of design and manufacturing space and around **65 production lines operating across two shifts**.

Despite the physical manufacturing footprint, the model is relatively **capex-light**.

FY25 capex was approximately **₹58 crore**, followed by approximately **₹56 crore in FY26**, even as FY26 revenue reached ₹1,603 crore. Net fixed-asset turns reached approximately **9.9x**.

The reason is structural. Avalon generally avoids manufacturing the highly capital-intensive fundamental electronic components. Instead, capital is concentrated in assembly-oriented equipment such as SMT lines, cleanrooms, testing systems, wire-harness equipment, metal fabrication and associated tooling.

Existing buffer capacity also means revenue can grow materially without proportional increases in factory space.

Consequently, **physical manufacturing capacity does not appear to be the primary near-term constraint on growth**. More important constraints are:

* the time required for customers to qualify new programs;
* availability of electronic components;
* working capital required to procure materials ahead of production; and
* execution of program ramps.

This distinction matters because incremental growth can potentially generate high returns on fixed capital, while simultaneously consuming substantial working capital.

## 1.11 How Avalon Makes Money

Avalon's gross profit is compensation for much more than assembly labor.

The company adds economic value through **engineering, procurement, manufacturing integration, quality assurance, certification and supply-chain coordination**.

Its gross margins have generally been in the **mid-30% range**, reflecting this manufacturing value addition.

The cost structure contains both variable and fixed elements. Raw materials and electronic components represent the largest variable cost and account for roughly two-thirds of revenue. Freight and logistics are also predominantly variable. Direct labor and utilities are semi-variable, while corporate salaries, engineering infrastructure, depreciation and facility overhead contain a meaningful fixed component.

This creates operating leverage: once manufacturing infrastructure and skilled personnel are in place, higher production volumes can spread fixed costs over a larger revenue base.

### Material pass-through and revenue optics

A complication in interpreting EMS revenue is **raw-material pass-through**.

If the cost of an electronic component or tariff rises and Avalon passes that increase to the customer, reported revenue rises even though Avalon's absolute manufacturing value-add may be unchanged.

For example:

`₹60 material + ₹40 Avalon value-add = ₹100 revenue`

If material cost rises to ₹70 and the additional ₹10 is fully passed through:

`₹70 material + ₹40 Avalon value-add = ₹110 revenue`

Reported revenue rises 10%, while absolute gross profit remains ₹40 and the gross-margin percentage falls from 40% to 36.4%.

Therefore, **reported revenue growth and percentage gross margin cannot be interpreted independently**. The economically relevant questions are whether absolute gross profit is growing, whether manufacturing value-add per program is increasing and whether incremental revenue generates attractive operating profit.

## 1.12 The Working-Capital Character of the Model

Avalon's business is capex-light but **working-capital intensive**.

Electronic components must often be procured well before the final system is shipped. New programs can require inventory to be built several months ahead of commercial production. Once manufacturing begins, raw materials become work-in-progress and then finished goods. After shipment, the economic value moves into trade receivables until the customer pays.

The operating cycle therefore resembles:

`Cash → raw-material inventory → WIP → finished goods → shipment/revenue → receivable → cash`

Rapid growth can consequently consume cash even while reported profits are rising.

A missing semiconductor or specialized component can also prevent an otherwise completed Box Build from being shipped, trapping capital in inventory.

Working-capital management is therefore not incidental to the business model. Together with manufacturing margin and fixed-asset utilization, it determines the ultimate return Avalon earns on its growth.

This leads back to the core economic equation:

`Program volume × Avalon content × manufacturing value-add × capital/cash efficiency`

## 1.13 Business Model Moat and Vulnerabilities

Avalon's moat is best described as **operational rather than technological**.

The OEM retains the core product IP, so Avalon does not possess the type of proprietary-product moat associated with a semiconductor designer or branded equipment manufacturer.

Instead, competitive advantage is built progressively through:

**Vertical integration → qualification → execution track record → deeper manufacturing content → higher switching costs**

The strongest barriers appear in complex, regulated and long-lifecycle programs where certifications, engineering knowledge and qualified manufacturing processes make supplier substitution difficult.

The model nevertheless has clear vulnerabilities. Avalon remains dependent on externally sourced electronic components; customer program delays can leave inventory, trained labor and capacity underutilized; tariffs can change the optimal India-US production mix; and rapid growth can strain working capital. Because Avalon ultimately manufactures products designed and sold by OEMs, it also remains exposed to changes in the underlying customer's end-market demand.

The business is therefore not insulated from customer cycles. Its protection comes primarily from **diversification, long program lives and the difficulty of replacing a qualified manufacturing partner**, rather than immunity from volume fluctuations.

## 1.14 Business Model — Investor Mental Model

The simplest way to understand Avalon is as a **manufacturing-content compounder**.

It acquires a customer or program, survives a lengthy qualification process and then attempts to expand simultaneously along three axes:

| Growth Axis  | Mechanism                                                          |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Volume**   | Existing qualified programs manufacture more units                 |
| **Programs** | Avalon wins additional programs and customers                      |
| **Content**  | Avalon moves from individual components toward complete Box Builds |

Vertical integration makes the third axis possible. The dual-shore footprint improves the probability of winning global programs. Qualification and integration increase customer stickiness. Long product lifecycles generate repeat purchase orders. High fixed-asset turns allow manufacturing volumes to scale without proportionate capex.

The resulting flywheel is:

`Win program`
`↓`
`Engineer & qualify`
`↓`
`Enter production`
`↓`
`Execute reliably`
`↓`
`Win repeat orders`
`↓`
`Add more components / Box Build`
`↓`
`Increase wallet share`
`↓`
`Deepen customer integration`
`↓`
`Win additional programs`

**The central investment question is therefore not simply whether the EMS market grows. It is whether Avalon can continuously move deeper into its customers' manufacturing architecture while adding new programs, without allowing working-capital requirements or execution complexity to overwhelm the economics.**

If it succeeds, Avalon can grow faster than its customers' underlying unit volumes because **manufacturing content per program and the number of programs can rise simultaneously**. That is the defining economic characteristic of the business model.

# 2. Unit Economics

## 2.1 The Economic Engine

Avalon's unit economics are best understood at the **program/incremental revenue level**, rather than per individual electronic assembly. The company does not disclose contribution margins by product or program, making conventional per-unit economics impossible to calculate reliably.

The economic engine can instead be expressed as:

`Revenue growth → gross profit → fixed-cost absorption → EBITDA → working-capital rotation → cash flow → ROCE`

FY26 provides strong evidence that this engine is improving. Revenue increased **46% to ₹1,603 crore**, while EBITDA increased approximately **50% to ₹173 crore** and PAT increased approximately **78% to ₹113 crore**. Profit therefore grew faster than revenue, demonstrating positive operating leverage.

The four variables that matter most to Avalon's economics are:

1. **Manufacturing mix:** India is substantially more profitable than the currently underutilized US operations.
2. **Manufacturing content:** Box Builds capture more manufacturing value than individual component assemblies.
3. **Fixed-asset utilization:** Higher volumes spread factory and employee overhead across a larger revenue base.
4. **Working-capital velocity:** Inventory and receivables determine how much capital must be committed to generate that profit.

The result is a business that is **fixed-asset-light but working-capital-heavy**.

---

## 2.2 Incremental Economics: Is Growth Becoming More Profitable?

The clearest evidence comes from FY25–FY26 incremental economics.

| Metric        |      FY25 |      FY26 |                  Change |
| ------------- | --------: | --------: | ----------------------: |
| Revenue       | ₹1,098 Cr | ₹1,603 Cr |            **+₹505 Cr** |
| EBITDA        |   ₹115 Cr |   ₹173 Cr |             **+₹59 Cr** |
| EBITDA Margin |    ~10.4% |    ~10.8% |           **Expanding** |
| PAT           |    ₹63 Cr |   ₹113 Cr |             **+₹50 Cr** |
| Capex         |    ₹58 Cr |    ₹56 Cr | **Flat despite growth** |

The additional ₹505 crore of revenue generated approximately **₹58.7 crore of incremental EBITDA**, implying an **incremental EBITDA margin of ~11.6%**, above the FY26 consolidated EBITDA margin of ~10.8%.

This is economically important: **the next rupee of revenue is currently more profitable than the average rupee already in the business.**

The mechanism is operating leverage. Avalon incurs employee, facility, engineering and depreciation costs before programs reach full production. Once volumes ramp, those costs do not increase proportionately with revenue.

The typical program therefore follows:

`Costs & inventory ↑ → qualification → revenue ramp ↑ → utilization ↑ → margin ↑`

This also means quarterly margins can temporarily weaken when several new programs are being prepared simultaneously. Front-loaded costs are not necessarily evidence of deteriorating economics if subsequently followed by commercial production and improved utilization.

FY26's indicative incremental ROCE was approximately **29%**. This should not be extrapolated mechanically because incremental capital-employed calculations are sensitive to balance-sheet timing, but it reinforces the broader conclusion that recent growth has generated attractive incremental returns.

---

## 2.3 Fixed Assets Are Not the Primary Capital Constraint

Avalon's strongest economic characteristic is its fixed-asset productivity.

FY26 capex was only **₹56 crore**, or approximately **3.5% of revenue**, despite ₹505 crore of incremental sales. Net fixed-asset turns reached approximately **8.3x**, while management's Net PP&E measure reached **9.9x**.

This is possible because Avalon does not manufacture capital-intensive semiconductor chips or bare PCBs. Its capital is concentrated in assembly equipment, SMT lines, testing infrastructure, cleanrooms and electromechanical manufacturing.

Consequently:

`High asset turns + operating leverage = potentially high incremental returns`

Existing buffer capacity further allows revenue to increase without proportionate investment in physical infrastructure.

The primary capital bottleneck is therefore **not factory capex—it is working capital**.

---

## 2.4 Working Capital: The Critical Constraint

Avalon must purchase electronic components well before finished systems are shipped. Rapidly growing programs therefore require inventory to be front-loaded, while large OEM customers receive credit after delivery.

During FY26, net working capital increased materially even as efficiency improved. Inventory increased by approximately **₹125 crore** and receivables by approximately **₹65 crore**, reflecting the capital required to support rapid revenue expansion.

The important offset was faster working-capital rotation.

Net Working Capital days fell sequentially through FY26:

`142 days → 131 days → 118 days → 112 days`

This is a major economic improvement. Without the reduction in working-capital days, 46% revenue growth would have required substantially more capital.

The key distinction is therefore:

**Working capital can increase in absolute rupees while working-capital efficiency simultaneously improves.**

For Avalon, this is one of the most important determinants of sustainable ROCE.

---

## 2.5 ROCE: Why Returns Are Improving

Avalon's calculated operating ROCE improved from approximately **7.8% in FY24 → 13.6% in FY25 → 18.8% in FY26**. Management reports FY26 ROCE at **20.6%**, with the difference largely arising from methodology and adjustments to capital employed.

More important than the exact figure is **why ROCE improved**.

Two things happened simultaneously:

`Higher operating margin × faster capital turnover = higher ROCE`

Operating EBIT margin increased from approximately **4.6% in FY24 to 8.7% in FY26**, while capital-employed turnover improved from approximately **1.69x to 2.16x**.

This is a relatively high-quality improvement because returns are rising through **better underlying profitability and greater capital efficiency**, rather than increased financial leverage.

---

## 2.6 Cash Conversion: The Remaining Weakness

The principal weakness in Avalon's economics remains the conversion of accounting profits into cash.

| Cash Metric         |     FY25 |        FY26 |
| ------------------- | -------: | ----------: |
| PAT                 |   ₹63 Cr | **₹113 Cr** |
| Operating Cash Flow |   ₹25 Cr |  **₹57 Cr** |
| CFO / PAT           |   ~0.39x |  **~0.50x** |
| Capex               |   ₹58 Cr |  **₹56 Cr** |
| Free Cash Flow      | Negative |  **~₹1 Cr** |

Despite ₹113 crore of PAT, Avalon generated only ₹57 crore of operating cash flow in FY26, with virtually all of this subsequently absorbed by capex. CFO/PAT therefore remained only **~0.5x**.

The weakness is primarily attributable to growth-related inventory and receivables rather than excessive fixed-asset investment.

This distinction matters. Weak cash conversion during rapid growth is tolerable **if working-capital efficiency continues improving and the inventory ultimately converts into revenue and cash**. It becomes concerning if revenue slows while inventory, receivables and NWC days rise.

Thus, the next stage in the improvement of Avalon's unit economics requires not merely higher earnings, but **higher cash conversion from those earnings**.

---

## 2.7 The Unit Economics in One Framework

Avalon's FY26 economics can be summarized as:

`Revenue +46%`
`↓`
`Incremental EBITDA margin ~11.6% > Group margin ~10.8%`
`↓`
`Operating leverage improving`
`↓`
`Capex intensity only ~3.5% of sales / PP&E turns ~9.9x`
`↓`
`But growth requires substantial inventory + receivables`
`↓`
`NWC days compressed to 112`
`↓`
`ROCE rises toward ~19–21%`
`↓`
`CFO/PAT still only ~0.5x`

**The central conclusion is that Avalon's incremental economics are attractive and improving, but the business has not yet fully translated those economics into free cash flow.** Fixed assets are highly productive, incremental revenue is currently margin-accretive, and improving working-capital velocity has materially increased ROCE. The remaining test is whether Avalon can sustain these returns while converting a progressively larger share of earnings into cash.

For the investment thesis, the ideal progression is therefore:

`Revenue growth → incremental margin ≥ group margin → working-capital days stable/falling → CFO/PAT improves → high ROCE sustained`

If that sequence holds, Avalon is not merely becoming larger; **each additional unit of growth is making the underlying economic engine stronger.**

# 3. Growth Triggers

## 3.1 Growth Framework: Separate the Baseline from the Accelerators

Avalon's growth opportunity should not be viewed as a collection of unrelated new sectors. The more useful framework is to separate **growth already embedded in existing programs** from catalysts capable of pushing revenue or earnings **above the current trajectory**.

FY26 established a strong starting point: revenue increased **46% to ₹1,603 crore**, while the executable order book reached **₹2,196 crore**. Management enters FY27 with an underlying revenue-growth trajectory of approximately **24–27%**.

The FY27 growth thesis can therefore be organized into four layers:

| Layer                          | Driver                                                              | Role in FY27                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Baseline engine**         | Existing/vintage programs + ₹2,196 Cr rolling order book            | Supports the underlying ~24–27% growth trajectory                        |
| **2. Earnings accelerator**    | **BESS/US program ramp → US loss compression**                      | Allows PAT to potentially grow materially faster than revenue            |
| **3. Incremental upside**      | Kavach, aerospace and other programs entering commercial production | Can push revenue above the baseline                                      |
| **4. Step-change optionality** | **Semiconductor equipment**                                         | Potentially very large, but FY27 financial materiality remains uncertain |

Europe, hydrogen, early EV programs and very early aerospace/power opportunities sit further out and should **not currently be incorporated into the core 6–12 month investment thesis**.

The central question for FY27 is consequently not whether Avalon can grow. The existing program base provides substantial visibility for that.

The more important question is:

> **Can Avalon generate positive earnings revisions by growing above the baseline and/or extracting substantially more profit from that growth?**

---

## 3.2 Trigger 1 — Existing Programs and Order-Book Execution: The Baseline Engine

The first growth driver is also the least glamorous: **execution of programs Avalon has already won**.

Avalon's executable order book reached **₹2,196 crore in Q4 FY26**, up approximately 25% YoY. This backlog represents firm purchase orders scheduled broadly within a rolling **12–14 month shipment window**, rather than the lifetime value of Avalon's longer customer relationships.

This distinction is important. Many customer programs operate under multi-year relationships, but only near-term firm purchase orders enter the reported backlog. The order book therefore continuously gets consumed by production and replenished with fresh purchase orders.

The operating flywheel is:

`Program win → qualification → production → repeat orders → deeper manufacturing content → larger wallet share → additional programs`

This means Avalon does not need an entirely new customer or end-market for every incremental rupee of growth. Growth can come from:

* higher volumes from existing customers;
* programs moving from NPI into production;
* additional assemblies being awarded within existing relationships;
* Avalon moving from individual components toward complete Box Builds; and
* new purchase orders replenishing the rolling backlog.

This existing engine needs to generate approximately **24–27% FY27 growth** before any emerging opportunity is considered an upside catalyst.

### Why the order book still needs monitoring

FY26 revenue grew 46%, whereas the order book increased approximately 25%. This is not necessarily deterioration because rapidly expanding production consumes purchase orders faster. However, as Avalon's revenue base becomes larger, the **absolute rate of new order intake must also increase** to sustain high growth.

The important test is therefore not simply whether the order book grows every quarter. It is whether order replenishment remains sufficient to sustain the next year's growth trajectory.

**Investment interpretation:** delivering 24–27% growth from existing programs is the **base case, not the bull case**. A meaningful positive revision requires additional acceleration.

---

## 3.3 Trigger 2 — BESS Ramp + US Breakeven: The Highest-Conviction Earnings Accelerator

The most important near-term earnings trigger is the **US Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) ramp and the resulting improvement in US manufacturing economics**.

These should be viewed as **one catalyst rather than two separate opportunities**:

`BESS/US volume ↑ → US plant utilization ↑ → fixed-cost absorption ↑ → US loss ↓ → consolidated PAT ↑`

Unlike several newer programs, BESS has already moved beyond qualification into **commercial volume production**. Management indicated that the ramp had begun and should continue over the following 12 months. US standalone revenue increased **59% YoY in FY26**, while Clean Energy reached approximately **20% of consolidated revenue** and grew approximately **45%**.

This matters because Avalon's US facilities currently operate below optimal utilization.

US standalone losses have already compressed from approximately **₹9 crore per quarter in H1 FY26 to ₹5 crore in Q4 FY26**. Management expects the US business to approach breakeven during the **later part of FY27**.

At the Q4 loss run-rate, eliminating the remaining drag would add approximately:

`₹5 Cr quarterly loss eliminated × 4 = ~₹20 Cr annualized PAT uplift`

This is particularly powerful because the manufacturing infrastructure and employee base already exist. The earnings improvement therefore comes predominantly through **utilization and fixed-cost absorption rather than substantial incremental capex**.

### Why this matters more than BESS revenue alone

The investment case should not be framed as simply:

`BESS revenue grows → Avalon grows`

The more important second-order effect is:

`BESS revenue grows → US utilization improves → losses disappear → PAT grows faster than revenue`

Consequently, US profitability is a more informative indicator than BESS revenue in isolation.

### What would constitute a positive surprise?

Management's current reference point is breakeven in the later part of FY27.

Therefore:

* gradual loss compression toward breakeven = **expected execution**;
* breakeven significantly earlier than indicated = **positive earnings catalyst**;
* continued losses around ₹5 crore despite strong US revenue growth = **warning that operating leverage is weaker than expected**;
* losses increasing again = **material thesis deterioration**.

For a 6–12 month horizon, this is arguably Avalon's **highest-conviction earnings accelerator**.

---

## 3.4 Trigger 3 — Semiconductor Equipment: The Largest Optionality, but Not Yet the Core Thesis

Avalon's entry into semiconductor equipment manufacturing is potentially its most strategically important new opportunity.

However, it is critical to distinguish **technical progress from financial materiality**.

Avalon is not manufacturing semiconductor chips or operating a semiconductor fab. It is manufacturing highly complex, Industry 4.0-compliant **Box Builds for semiconductor capital equipment**.

This fits directly with Avalon's strategy of moving toward increasingly complex integrated systems and represents what management describes as among the highest-end Box Builds it has undertaken.

### Current status

By Q4 FY26:

* approximately **50–60% of sub-programs had progressed through First Article Inspection/customer acceptance**;
* approximately **40% remained in trials or work-in-process**;
* production approvals were still progressing; and
* management explicitly described the program as **long gestation**.

The critical analytical point is:

> **60% of sub-programs completing FAI does not mean 60% of the revenue opportunity has begun.**

FAI is a technical milestone. The financial trigger occurs only when approval is followed by **repeat commercial production orders at meaningful volumes**.

### Why semiconductor could become a major accelerator

If qualification converts into commercial production, semiconductor equipment offers three potential benefits:

1. **New revenue pool:** adds a large new high-complexity manufacturing program.
2. **Mix improvement:** reinforces Avalon's shift toward sophisticated Box Builds.
3. **Capability signalling:** successful execution can demonstrate Avalon's ability to manufacture exceptionally complex systems, potentially supporting additional customer wins.

The long-term potential is therefore substantial.

But the source material does **not provide sufficient evidence to quantify FY27 semiconductor revenue reliably**.

For the current investment horizon, semiconductor should therefore be treated as:

**Medium probability × potentially very high impact.**

### What turns semiconductor into an investable FY27 catalyst?

Not another qualification announcement.

The required evidence is:

`FAI completion → customer production approval → purchase orders → commercial shipments → financially visible revenue`

Until this occurs, semiconductor remains **step-change optionality**, rather than a component required to achieve the baseline FY27 growth trajectory.

This distinction prevents the investment thesis from becoming dependent on an exciting but customer-controlled program whose timing remains uncertain.

---

## 3.5 Trigger 4 — Kavach: Emerging Domestic Revenue Upside

Avalon is developing control units for India's **Kavach railway safety/signalling system**.

By Q4 FY26, testing had progressed and Avalon was completing prototypes/control units ahead of potential volume orders. The program therefore sits between technical qualification and meaningful commercial production.

Kavach could become financially relevant because it combines:

* domestic manufacturing;
* specialized electronics;
* a potentially meaningful deployment opportunity; and
* the possibility of production scaling once qualification is completed.

The potential timing indicated in the source is broadly **FY27, particularly H2**, although commercial volume remains less certain than BESS.

The correct signal is again not prototype completion itself. It is:

`Prototype/FAI → confirmed order → commercial deliveries → visible Mobility revenue`

Kavach therefore represents **credible incremental upside**, but should not yet be required for the base case.

---

## 3.6 Trigger 5 — Aerospace: Slow-Building, High-Stickiness Growth

Aerospace is structurally attractive for Avalon because programs tend to involve long qualification cycles but can become durable once approved.

The most advanced near-term opportunity is **aerospace cabin subassemblies**, where Avalon had passed the initial practical inspection and was progressing toward steady commercial volumes by Q4 FY26.

Other aerospace and defence opportunities—including advanced cockpit assemblies, landing-gear-related parts and cable commodities—are at earlier stages.

The key characteristic of aerospace is therefore:

`Long qualification period → difficult customer approval → long production relationship`

This creates attractive long-term stickiness but makes aerospace a less likely source of sudden near-term earnings acceleration than BESS.

For FY27, aerospace should be viewed primarily as **gradual incremental growth**, with upside if programs transition into production faster than expected.

---

## 3.7 Box-Build Mix: A Growth-Quality Accelerator

Not every trigger requires entering a new end-market.

A strategically important internal growth driver is Avalon's continuing shift toward **complete Box Builds and system integration**.

Box Builds reached approximately **56% of Q4 FY26 revenue**. These products incorporate more of Avalon's capabilities—PCB assembly, magnetics, wire harnesses, sheet metal, plastics and final integration—within a single customer system.

The economic effect is:

`More Avalon content per customer program → more value-add per unit → better fixed-cost absorption → potentially higher profit per program`

This means Avalon can grow economically even when the underlying customer's unit volumes are unchanged, simply by capturing **more manufacturing content per system**.

However, percentage gross or EBITDA margin should not be interpreted mechanically. Management appears willing to accept programs that produce attractive absolute profit and ROCE even if material pass-through makes the reported margin percentage appear lower.

The important signal is therefore whether rising Box-Build penetration translates into **higher absolute EBITDA, attractive incremental margins and strong returns on capital**, rather than merely a higher reported gross-margin percentage.

---

## 3.8 Longer-Dated Opportunities: Do Not Capitalize Them Yet

Several additional opportunities appear repeatedly in Avalon's strategic commentary:

* European customer sourcing following improved India-Europe trade access;
* hydrogen;
* electric vehicles;
* high-power inverter platforms;
* aerospace cockpit and structural components;
* defence cable programs; and
* other industrial/power-sector opportunities.

These may eventually become meaningful, but the source document does not provide sufficient evidence of firm orders or near-term commercial scale to justify incorporating them into FY27 earnings expectations.

European expansion remains primarily business-development activity. Hydrogen and EV exposure remains early, while current Clean Energy growth is overwhelmingly associated with **BESS rather than these emerging technologies**.

They should therefore be treated as **free optionality until evidence changes**.

This distinction matters because combining every announced opportunity into a single TAM narrative would substantially overstate Avalon's near-term growth visibility.

---

## 3.9 Growth Trigger Ranking

The most useful ranking is not by theoretical market size, but by:

`Financial impact × probability of execution × timing`

| Priority | Trigger                                     | FY27 Financial Relevance  | Conviction      | What Must Happen                                                  |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1**    | **Existing program/order-book execution**   | **Very High**             | **High**        | Deliver ~24–27% baseline growth                                   |
| **2**    | **BESS ramp → US loss compression**         | **Very High**             | **High**        | US losses decline sequentially toward zero                        |
| **3**    | **Semiconductor commercialization**         | Potentially **Very High** | **Medium**      | FAI → production approval → meaningful revenue                    |
| **4**    | **Kavach commercialization**                | **Medium–High**           | **Medium**      | Prototype/qualification → volume deliveries                       |
| **5**    | **Aerospace production ramps**              | **Medium**                | **Medium–High** | Approved programs reach steady production                         |
| **6**    | **Higher Box-Build content**                | **Medium–High**           | **High**        | Value-add/mix rises while incremental economics remain attractive |
| **7**    | Europe / hydrogen / EV / early new programs | **Low near term**         | **Low–Medium**  | Requires tangible commercial orders                               |

The ranking highlights an important asymmetry in the investment case:

**Avalon does not need semiconductor, Kavach or Europe to succeed in order to grow strongly in FY27.**

The existing program base is expected to provide the baseline growth. New programs matter primarily because they create the possibility of **growth above expectations**.

---

## 3.10 What Creates a Positive Earnings-Revision Cycle?

The strongest FY27 outcome would involve multiple layers of the growth architecture working simultaneously:

```text
Existing programs execute at/above plan
                ↓
Revenue meets or exceeds 24–27% trajectory
                +
BESS drives US utilization higher
                ↓
US losses fall faster than expected
                +
One emerging program reaches meaningful production
                ↓
Revenue expectations move higher
                +
Incremental margins remain attractive
                ↓
PAT expectations rise faster than revenue
```

That is the critical distinction between **growth** and an **earnings accelerator**.

Simply delivering the existing 24–27% trajectory would confirm that Avalon remains a strong growth company. It would not necessarily create a positive revision cycle because that growth is already management's stated trajectory.

A more powerful outcome requires at least two of the following:

1. **Core business grows above guidance.**
2. **US losses decline faster than the later-FY27 breakeven timetable.**
3. **A new program becomes financially material.**

If those developments occur while incremental EBITDA margins remain above or around the consolidated margin, the effect becomes multiplicative:

`Revenue expectations ↑ → margin expectations ↑ → PAT expectations ↑ faster`

---

## 3.11 What Could Break the Growth Thesis?

The greatest risk is not that one speculative program gets delayed.

Semiconductor, for example, has already been described as long gestation. A delay there would reduce optionality but would not automatically invalidate the core thesis.

More concerning would be **simultaneous deterioration in the underlying engine**:

`Revenue below guidance + US losses stop narrowing + incremental margins weaken + NWC days rise`

That combination would imply that the issue is no longer customer timing around an individual program. It would suggest deterioration in the economics of the core business itself.

Other important execution risks include customer-controlled production schedules, slower order-book replenishment, working-capital slippage, component/PCB supply disruptions and delays in converting prototypes into commercial orders.

---

## 3.12 Investment Conclusion — What Actually Matters in FY27

Avalon's FY27 growth thesis should be viewed as a **stack of increasingly uncertain upside layers**.

The bottom layer—existing programs and the ₹2,196 crore rolling executable order book—provides the foundation for approximately **24–27% revenue growth**.

Above that sits the most important near-term earnings accelerator: **BESS-driven US utilization and loss compression**. Because US infrastructure already exists, reaching breakeven can potentially add approximately **₹20 crore of annualized PAT** from the Q4 FY26 loss run-rate without requiring proportionate growth in capital employed.

The next layer consists of **Kavach, aerospace and other programs progressing toward commercial production**. These provide credible opportunities for revenue to exceed the baseline.

Finally, **semiconductor equipment represents the largest step-change optionality**. The technical progress is real, but the program should become part of near-term earnings expectations only when FAI and qualification translate into production approvals, purchase orders and financially material commercial shipments.

The resulting FY27 hierarchy is therefore:

**Baseline:** Existing programs execute
**Highest-conviction accelerator:** BESS → US breakeven
**Incremental upside:** Kavach + aerospace + other program ramps
**Step-change optionality:** Semiconductor commercialization
**Long-dated optionality:** Europe + hydrogen + EV + early aerospace/power programs

For the 6–12 month investment case, the most important development may therefore **not** be Avalon's newest or most technologically exciting opportunity. It may be the continued ramp of an existing BESS customer through Avalon's underutilized US infrastructure.

The two questions that matter most are:

> **Does BESS/US growth drive US losses toward zero faster than management's later-FY27 timetable?**

and

> **Does any emerging program—particularly semiconductor—move from technical progress to financially material commercial revenue quickly enough to push growth above the 24–27% baseline?**

If the answer to both becomes yes while the existing business continues executing, Avalon would move from a strong growth trajectory into a potentially powerful **positive earnings-revision cycle**.

# 4. Management's Mental Model

## 4.1 Long-Term Programs Over Quarterly Optimization

Avalon's management appears to operate with a **multi-year compounding mindset rather than optimizing for smooth quarterly results**. Management repeatedly frames its ambition around doubling revenue over three-year periods and explicitly accepts that individual quarters can be affected by customer-controlled program timing.

This is consistent with the economics of the business: qualification can take 15–36 months, but successful programs can subsequently generate revenue for many years. Management therefore appears willing to tolerate near-term volatility when the underlying program remains intact.

The operating mindset can be summarized as:

`Win long-duration program → invest ahead of revenue → qualify → scale → deepen wallet share → repeat`

This makes **program progress more important than quarter-to-quarter timing**.

---

## 4.2 The Three Problems Management Is Trying to Solve

Management's actions suggest three clear priorities:

| Priority                          | Management Objective                                        | Economic Outcome                                   |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Move up the complexity ladder** | Components → integrated Box Builds → highly complex systems | Higher wallet share and customer stickiness        |
| **Fix US economics**              | Increase utilization through BESS and other US programs     | Fixed-cost absorption and elimination of US losses |
| **Improve capital efficiency**    | Control working capital while remaining capex-light         | Higher ROCE and cash generation                    |

The strategy is therefore not simply **"maximize revenue growth."** Management is trying to increase the amount and complexity of manufacturing value captured while keeping the capital required to support that growth under control.

---

## 4.3 ROCE Appears to Matter More Than Reported Margin Percentage

One of management's clearest strategic choices is what it **does not** manufacture.

Avalon has resisted backward integration into bare PCB manufacturing despite potential incentives because PCB fabrication is capital intensive and requires substantial scale. Instead, management prefers to deploy capital into engineering, assembly and system integration, where asset turns are considerably higher.

The apparent optimization hierarchy is therefore:

`ROCE / Asset Turns > Margin % in isolation`

Management appears comfortable accepting lower reported EBITDA-margin percentages when material pass-through or product mix still generates attractive **absolute profit with limited incremental fixed capital**.

This is consistent with FY26's **9.9x net fixed-asset turns and 20.6% reported ROCE**.

---

## 4.4 Willingness to Invest Before Revenue

Management consciously **front-loads costs**.

Employees may be recruited and trained months before production starts, while inventory is purchased ahead of expected program ramps. The resulting quarterly pattern can be:

`Manpower + inventory + NPI costs ↑ → qualification → revenue ramp → utilization ↑ → margins ↑`

This creates an important analytical distinction: temporary margin or working-capital deterioration can be acceptable **if it precedes a visible commercial ramp**. It becomes problematic if the expected revenue repeatedly fails to materialize.

---

## 4.5 Conservative Guidance, but Increasing Confidence

Management's communication during FY26 evolved materially.

Early in FY26, management emphasized conservatism and resisted aggressive forecasting. As execution strengthened, revenue guidance was upgraded repeatedly, eventually reaching approximately **40% growth**. Avalon ultimately delivered **46% growth**, reduced NWC days to **112 versus the guided 120–130**, and reached **20.6% ROCE**.

This suggests that management's conservatism is partly driven by a genuine limitation: **Avalon does not fully control customer qualification and production timing**.

Consequently, guidance upgrades backed by actual program ramps are more informative than optimistic commentary around opportunities still undergoing qualification.

---

## 4.6 Investor Interpretation

Management's mental model can ultimately be summarized as:

> **Build capabilities ahead of demand, win difficult long-duration programs, move progressively toward higher-value system integration, accept temporary costs during ramps, and maximize returns through high asset turns and working-capital discipline.**

The framework appears internally coherent and FY26 execution supports it. The principal risk is that this same willingness to invest ahead of revenue can become costly when customer-controlled programs are delayed.

For investors, the critical distinction is therefore:

**Front-loading followed by commercial production = deliberate investment.**

**Front-loading followed by repeated delays = capital misallocation.**

That distinction is the most useful lens through which to judge management execution going forward.

That would indicate that the growth triggers identified earlier are translating into actual earnings acceleration rather than merely operational milestones.

# 6. Bull / Base / Bear Scenario

The FY27 scenarios should be defined primarily by whether Avalon performs **above, around or below management's ~24–27% revenue-growth trajectory**, rather than by speculative absolute forecasts.

|                               | **Bull**                                                          | **Base**                                                  | **Bear**                                  |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Revenue**                   | Growth **above 24–27%**; guidance upgraded                        | Delivers broadly **24–27%**                               | Below guidance / guidance cut             |
| **Incremental EBITDA Margin** | Materially above consolidated margin                              | Around/slightly above consolidated margin                 | Falls below consolidated margin           |
| **US Operations**             | BESS ramp drives **earlier-than-expected breakeven**              | Losses decline progressively; breakeven around later FY27 | Ramp disappoints; losses remain elevated  |
| **Semiconductor**             | FAI/qualification converts into **meaningful commercial revenue** | Qualification progresses; limited FY27 contribution       | Approvals materially delayed              |
| **Kavach / Aerospace**        | At least one becomes financially material                         | Gradual contribution                                      | Program ramps delayed                     |
| **NWC Days**                  | **≤112 days** despite strong growth                               | Broadly controlled around current levels                  | Sustained increase toward/above 125–130   |
| **CFO/PAT**                   | Strong improvement                                                | Gradual improvement                                       | Remains weak/deteriorates                 |
| **Earnings Outcome**          | **PAT materially outgrows revenue**                               | PAT grows somewhat faster than revenue                    | PAT growth converges toward/below revenue |

## 6.1 Bull Case — Positive Earnings-Revision Cycle

The **24–27% revenue trajectory itself is not the Bull case**; it is broadly management's starting expectation.

Bull requires two or more incremental developments:

1. Existing programs execute strongly enough for management to **upgrade revenue guidance**.
2. BESS drives US utilization sufficiently for losses to decline faster than expected, bringing breakeven forward.
3. Semiconductor, Kavach or another emerging program becomes **financially material**, rather than merely achieving technical milestones.

If incremental margins remain attractive and NWC stays controlled, the result becomes:

`Revenue expectations ↑ → margin expectations ↑ → PAT expectations ↑ faster`

This is the scenario with the strongest potential for positive earnings revisions.

## 6.2 Base Case — Strong but Expected Execution

Existing programs and the ₹2,196 crore executable order book deliver approximately **24–27% revenue growth**. BESS continues ramping, US losses progressively narrow toward management's later-FY27 breakeven target, and emerging programs advance without becoming major consolidated revenue contributors.

Incremental EBITDA margins remain around or modestly above group margins, NWC stays controlled and cash conversion gradually improves.

This would represent **healthy fundamental execution but broadly confirmation of expectations**, rather than a major earnings-revision event.

## 6.3 Bear Case — Core Economics Begin Weakening

A semiconductor delay alone **does not constitute the Bear case**. Management has already identified semiconductor as a long-gestation program, making timing uncertainty partly expected.

The Bear case requires deterioration across several core indicators:

`Revenue below guidance + US losses stagnate + incremental margins fall + NWC days rise`

This would indicate that weakness is no longer isolated to customer timing around one program; **the underlying economic engine itself is deteriorating**.

## 6.4 Scenario Decision Rule

The investment thesis can therefore be reduced to one distinction:

> **Bull:** Avalon grows faster than expected **and** converts that growth into disproportionately higher earnings.
> **Base:** Avalon executes the growth already expected of it.
> **Bear:** Growth slows while incremental profitability and capital efficiency deteriorate simultaneously.

For the next 6–12 months, **US profitability is the key swing factor between Base and Bull, while simultaneous deterioration in revenue, margins and working capital is the clearest path from Base toward Bear.**
