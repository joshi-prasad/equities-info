# Podcast Notes: Global Macro, AI Bubble, Gold, Oil and India's Investment Opportunities

In this **SOIC podcast**, host Ishmohit meets with global macro investor **Ritesh Jain** to discuss shifting global macroeconomic regimes, the AI tech bubble, structural changes in the US economy, and the long-term outlook for Indian markets.

https://youtu.be/SC_UOxjsxIM?si=jS7WYX9ez0eJ7lEa

## 1. USD, Chinese Yuan, Gold and US Treasury Bonds

- The speaker argues that the US dollar is depreciating against the Chinese yuan, while other currencies are depreciating against the dollar. The Chinese yuan is therefore emerging as a relative outperformer.
- Under normal circumstances, this currency and geopolitical environment should be supportive of gold prices. However, gold has not rallied as expected.
- One possible explanation offered is that the US is pressuring other countries to purchase US Treasury bonds.
- Persistently elevated oil prices are putting pressure on oil-importing countries. To finance these imports, some countries may be selling gold and reallocating capital to US Treasuries.
- India has reportedly sold some gold and purchased additional US Treasuries. The speaker speculates that this could be connected to the India–US trade negotiations, although this linkage remains unverified.
- Marco Rubio reportedly said that a trade deal was “weeks away.”

## 2. AI Bubble and Potential Capital Rotation into Emerging Markets

- If the AI bubble bursts, capital could flow out of US markets and into emerging economies, including India.
- The yield on CoreWeave bonds is reportedly approaching 13–14%. The speaker interprets this as a sign that financing is becoming more expensive and investors are demanding higher returns to fund AI-related businesses.
- Rising borrowing costs could expose the financial vulnerabilities of companies that have committed substantial capital to AI infrastructure.

## 3. AI Infrastructure Spending and the Financial Sustainability of Hyperscalers

- The speaker questions whether the enormous capital expenditure on AI infrastructure will generate adequate returns for hyperscalers.
- Even companies with historically strong cash-generating businesses, such as Google, are facing substantial data-centre investment requirements that could put pressure on free cash flow.
- Several companies are reportedly raising long-term debt, including bonds with maturities of up to 30 years, to finance infrastructure investments.
- The speaker considers this unusual and potentially concerning, particularly if the useful economic life of AI infrastructure or the returns it generates do not justify the duration of the debt.
- The central concern is whether AI monetisation will catch up with the scale of investment before financing costs and competitive pressures erode returns.

## 4. India's Outlook and the Importance of Lower Oil Prices

- India could benefit significantly if crude oil prices decline.
- Lower oil prices would reduce the import bill, ease inflationary pressures, improve the external balance and potentially support corporate profitability.
- An early resolution to the ongoing war would be beneficial for India and other oil-importing economies.
- If the conflict persists and oil prices remain elevated, the resulting economic pressure could affect countries dependent on imported energy.

## 5. Declining Strategic Importance of Oil and the Strait of Hormuz

- Over the next four to five years, the speaker expects oil's strategic importance—and the importance of the Strait of Hormuz in particular—to diminish.
- The underlying thesis is that countries will accelerate investments in alternative energy sources to reduce their dependence on imported oil and vulnerable shipping routes.
- This could gradually reshape global energy security, trade flows and geopolitical relationships.

## 6. India's Next Investment Winners May Come from Outside the Major Indices

- The speaker believes that the next generation of high-performing Indian stocks may not necessarily come from established indices such as the Nifty 50 or Nifty Next 50.
- Instead, attractive opportunities could emerge among companies outside these indices, particularly smaller or less widely followed businesses benefiting from structural growth trends.
- The implication is that investors may need to look beyond index constituents and conduct bottom-up research to identify emerging winners.

## 7. Indian Sectors and Industries with Potential to Outperform

The speaker identifies the following areas as potential beneficiaries of India's structural growth and changing global supply chains:

- **Precision manufacturing:** Companies benefiting from advanced manufacturing, industrial automation and the shift towards higher-value production.
- **Healthcare:** Businesses supported by rising healthcare demand and expanding medical infrastructure.
- **Pharmaceuticals:** Companies positioned to benefit from domestic demand and global pharmaceutical supply chains.
- **Electrification:** Businesses involved in electrical equipment, power infrastructure, grid modernisation and the broader electrification trend.
- **Free Trade Agreement (FTA) beneficiaries:** Indian companies that could gain improved market access, export opportunities or competitive advantages from trade agreements with the UK and the EU.

## 8. Gold Could Become Increasingly Unaffordable for Retail Buyers

- The speaker expects gold prices to rise to levels at which retail consumers may find it increasingly difficult to afford meaningful quantities.
- If this happens, jewellery demand, consumer purchasing behaviour and the composition of gold investment demand could change.
- This is a bullish long-term price thesis, although its outcome will depend on factors such as central-bank demand, real interest rates, currency movements and investor sentiment.

## 9. US Midterm Elections and Political Constraints on Trump

- The speaker expects the Republican Party to perform poorly in the US midterm elections, potentially losing control of both chambers of Congress.
- Such an outcome could constrain Trump's ability to implement his policy agenda, including decisions relating to trade, tariffs, taxation and foreign policy.
- The resulting political uncertainty could affect global markets, international trade relationships and investor sentiment.

## 10. Potential Geopolitical Realignment and India's Manufacturing Opportunity

- The speaker anticipates a possible strategic realignment involving the US, India and Russia, with China and Europe occupying different positions in the emerging geopolitical order.
- Europe is characterised in the podcast as a region facing significant industrial cost disadvantages, particularly in manufacturing.
- The speaker believes that some manufacturing activity could shift from Europe to India, supported by India's cost competitiveness, industrial capabilities and prospective FTAs with the UK and EU.
- If realised, this could create opportunities for Indian exporters, precision manufacturers, industrial suppliers and companies integrated into global supply chains.

## Overall Investment Thesis

The podcast presents a broad macroeconomic thesis built around five interconnected themes:

1. **Global capital rotation:** A potential correction in AI-related US assets could redirect capital towards emerging markets.
2. **Energy transition:** Lower dependence on oil could reduce India's vulnerability to geopolitical shocks and create new investment opportunities.
3. **Manufacturing shift:** India could capture a greater share of global manufacturing as supply chains and trade relationships evolve.
4. **Structural sector winners:** Precision manufacturing, healthcare, pharmaceuticals and electrification could offer attractive long-term growth opportunities.
5. **Alternative investment opportunities:** Gold and companies outside the major Indian indices could benefit from changing macroeconomic conditions.

**Central takeaway:** The speaker's overarching thesis is that a combination of geopolitical realignment, an AI investment correction, energy diversification and global manufacturing shifts could create the next set of investment opportunities in India.

--------

### 1. Global Macro Shifts & US Market Polarization
* **US Fiscal Stimulus & GDP Growth:** Post-2020 COVID policies, accelerated depreciation benefits under the Trump administration, and massive AI investments have driven US nominal GDP growth to ~7% for a \$35 trillion economy.
* **Extreme Market Concentration:** The US accounts for **30% of global GDP but 64% of global market cap**, with just 8 stocks representing **45% of the S&P 500**. Outside of AI and mega-cap tech, other segments of the US economy (e.g., housing starts, car loan delinquencies) are experiencing significant stress.
* **Breakdown of Long-Term Correlations:** Historical macro relationships are shifting. Over the past 10 years, Emerging Market (EM) debt has handsomely outperformed Developed Market (DM) and US debt. Additionally, currencies like the Chinese Yuan have strengthened against the USD despite interest rate differentials.

---

### 2. Signs of the AI Tech Bubble Deflating
* **Fixed Income & Bond Market Warning Signals:** The bond market is flagging trouble ahead of equities. For example, cloud infrastructure firm CoreWeave saw its bond yields rise from 9.25% to 15–16%.
* **Circular Financing:** Current AI financing models resemble the dot-com bubble of 1999–2000, where hardware providers like Nvidia invest in the equity of cloud providers, which in turn purchase chips from them.
* **Negative Cash Flows for Hyperscalers:** For the first time in 15 years, major hyperscalers are experiencing negative cash flows as capital expenditures surge while stock buybacks stall.

---

### 3. US De-Hegemony, Geopolitics & Gold
* **US Shift Away from Hegemony:** With debt-to-GDP at **125%** and manufacturing down to **9.5% of GDP**, the US administration is prioritizing re-industrialization, supply chain re-shoring, and growing out of its debt over maintaining its global reserve currency burden.
* **Resource Alignment & Cold War 2.0:** Global dynamics are shifting toward a modern Cold War between US state-controlled capitalism and Chinese state-controlled capitalism, forcing smaller countries to align with specific spheres of influence. The US and Russia may eventually align due to mutual resource dependencies, while resource-poor Europe faces structural headwinds.
* **Central Bank Gold Accumulation:** Central banks (especially China, Middle East nations, and India) are aggressively accumulating physical gold to diversify away from US Treasury risk. Jain foresees gold returning to its historical status as an institutional reserve asset rather than a retail trading instrument.

---

### 4. Energy Security & Crude Oil Dynamics
* **Refining Capacity Bottleneck:** The main bottleneck in energy is not raw crude oil supply, but complex refining capacity.
* **Impact of Oil Prices:** High crude oil prices act as a liquidity drain across global asset classes and raise the cost of capital. Short term, high oil prices trigger inflation; long term, they induce economic slowdowns.
* **Beneficiaries:** If crude oil prices stabilize or decline, **India stands out as one of the biggest global beneficiaries**. Governments globally are doubling down on energy security, grid infrastructure, and electrification.

---

### 5. Indian Market Outlook & Key Growth Themes
* **Bottom-Up Market:** India is driven by a **12% nominal GDP growth rate**, making it a bottom-up stock picker's market rather than a top-down index play.
* **Index vs. Tomorrow's Winners:** Major benchmark indices reflect past winners. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) have been selling heavyweights in large-cap IT, banking, and oil & gas while expanding allocations across mid- and small-cap companies.
* **High-Conviction Investment Themes for India:**
  1. **Free Trade Agreements (FTAs):** FTAs with the UK, New Zealand, Europe, and Canada will unlock massive new export volumes (e.g., engineering goods exports target \$150 billion).
  2. **Electrification & Precision Engineering / Defense:** Infrastructure buildout, grid expansion, and local manufacturing support.
  3. **Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals:** AI adoption is accelerating drug discovery timelines and reducing R&D costs, positioning healthcare as a primary beneficiary.
  4. **Wealth Management:** A capital-light growth sector benefiting from financialization without direct credit default exposure or severe regulatory overreach.

---

### 6. Investor Frameworks & Inculcating Macro Thought
* **Avoiding AI Shortcuts:** Jain stresses that automated summaries or short social media content cannot replace the deep reading of full books, long-form reports, and paid macro research.
* **Connecting Macro to Micro:** Developing a macro lens requires years of consistent effort, studying financial history, and connecting top-down global themes with bottom-up company visits and ground reality.
