In this video from *The N Show*, host Neeraj interviews market strategist **Ritesh Jain** to analyze why global financial markets are behaving counterintuitively and how the shift toward **fiscal dominance** is reshaping asset classes.

---

### **1. The Core Shift: Fiscal Dominance vs. Central Banking**
* **Government Over Central Banks**: Markets are no longer driven primarily by central bank monetary policy (such as the US Federal Reserve or RBI). Instead, **fiscal authorities and government spending dictate market direction**.
* **Massive Government Footprint**: Government expenditure makes up roughly 40% of GDP in the US and Canada, and up to 55% in France. Central bank rate hikes cannot easily cool economies when governments continue high spending funded by debt.

---

### **2. The US Debt & Yield Paradox**
* **Rising Debt and Yields**: US Debt-to-GDP has climbed past **126%**, and Treasury bond yields across short- and long-term maturities have risen significantly.
* **Defying Economic Rules**: Traditional economic theory states that higher bond yields and interest rates should strengthen the currency and pull capital out of equities and gold. However, equities have stayed strong, and gold has remained resilient.
* **Debt Rollover as Stimulus**: Around **\$10 trillion of US bonds mature and roll over** this year at higher rates (e.g., ~5% vs. previous ~3%). This transfers **\$50–\$75 billion in additional interest income** directly to bondholders, effectively injecting liquidity into the economy borrowed from future generations.

---

### **3. "Extend and Pretend" Tactics**
* **Financial Engineering**: To keep bond yields from breaking the financial system, the US Treasury can employ tools such as **yield curve control**, reducing the issuance of long-term (30-year) bonds, or revaluing government gold holdings at market rates to buy back long-term debt.
* **Shift in Treasury Buyers**: Traditional foreign central banks and surplus countries (such as China and Saudi Arabia) are reducing their holdings of US Treasuries. The primary buyers have shifted toward offshore entities and hedge funds.

---

### **4. Impact on Key Asset Classes**
* **Bonds**: **Focus strictly on short-term bonds**. Avoid long-term bonds, as governments ultimately inflate away debt burdens over time.
* **Gold**: Serves as a fundamental hedge against rising debt-to-GDP ratios and fiscal devaluations. Although high yields create short-term headwinds, its structural outlook remains strong as long as fiscal deficits expand.
* **Equities & AI**: Massive government backing for **artificial intelligence (AI)** and infrastructure spending sustains corporate earnings and draws capital into mega-cap technology equities.

---

### **5. Takeaways for Indian Markets & Investors**
* **Large-Cap Value**: Large-cap Indian equities have become attractive and relatively cheap compared to over-extended mid- and small-caps.
* **Structural Themes**: Promising long-term themes include **electrification and grid infrastructure**, **defense manufacturing**, **engineering and tech**, **wealth management**, and beneficiaries of Free Trade Agreements (FTAs).
* **Real Estate**: Opportunities lie in **Tier-2 and Tier-3 towns** that benefit from new manufacturing facilities or growing Global Capability Centers (GCCs).

---

### **Key Conclusion**
Investors should track **political leaders, prime ministers, and treasury departments** rather than central bankers, because government spending and debt creation now drive market liquidity and global asset returns.

* **Impact of Fiscal Dominance:** High fiscal spending and rising government debt create a long-term tailwind for gold, as investors lose confidence in fiat currencies and debt paper.

If the government reduces spending and the Debt-to-GDP ratio declines, the outlook for gold turns negative. At that point, fixed-income yields (like a 5% 10-year US Treasury) become more attractive than gold, which pays no yield or dividend and incurs holding costs.

### **2. Mechanism of "Inflating Away" Debt**
**Question Addressed:** What is inflating away the debt.

* Governments run persistent fiscal deficits and borrow money from the future to spend in the present rather than raising sufficient tax revenues.
* To handle this rising debt, authorities expand currency liquidity, causing systemic inflation that steadily reduces the real purchasing power of fiat currency and effectively erodes the real burden of the debt over time.

**Fiscal dominance** occurs when government spending, national debt, and fiscal policy become the primary drivers of economic growth and market liquidity, overshadowing the monetary policies of central banks like the US Federal Reserve or RBI.

Here is step-by-step how the mechanism works:

---

### **1. Massive Government Footprint**
In major economies, government expenditure accounts for a huge portion of economic activity (around 40% of GDP in the US and Canada, and up to 55% in France). Because government spending represents such a large share of the total GDP, the broader economy becomes heavily reliant on public spending rather than private sector dynamics alone.

---

### **2. Borrowing from the Future to Inject Present Liquidity**
Governments fund continuous spending programs by running large deficits and issuing bonds—essentially borrowing against future generations to spend in the present.
* When existing national debt matures and rolls over at higher interest rates, the government pays out significantly higher interest to bondholders.
* This interest payout acts as a direct transfer of cash into the hands of investors and institutions, continuously pumping new liquidity into the financial system.

---

### **3. Neutralizing Central Bank Policy**
Under normal economic conditions, when a central bank raises interest rates to curb inflation, borrowing cools down, bond yields rise, and money flows out of risk assets like equities into fixed income. Under fiscal dominance:
* High government spending and interest payouts offset central bank rate hikes.
* Corporate earnings and asset prices (such as stocks and gold) remain surprisingly resilient because system-wide liquidity remains abundant despite high interest rates.

---

### **4. "Extend and Pretend" Financial Engineering**
To manage soaring debt loads and prevent high interest rates from collapsing the budget or housing market, treasury departments deploy financial engineering tactics:
* Shifting borrowing from long-term 30-year bonds to short-term bills.
* Implementing yield curve control or revaluing government assets (like gold holdings) to buy back debt.
* Gradually inflating away the debt over time by expanding the money base, which lowers the real purchasing power of paper currency while eroding the real value of the debt burden.
